## 🚀 The Real-World Hook (Dual Coding Theory)

Picture yourself as the lead architect on a high-stakes public hospital project. A wheelchair user enters the washroom. The Philippine law is rigid—there are no "ranges" here, only absolute minimums and maximums. If your toilet seat is 460mm instead of exactly 450mm, you fail the inspection. **Chunk the rules: Clearance → Support → Heights.**

## 🎯 Core Concepts (Mnemonic Anchor: "C-S-H")

- **Clearance (C)**: The "1.8 Rule." 1800x1800 mm is your unobstructed floor space mantra.
- **Support (S)**: The "800 Rule." Grab bars are locked precisely at 800 mm AFF.
- **Heights (H)**: The "450/800 Rule." Seat at 450 mm, Lavatory max 800 mm.

_Repeat after me: "1.8 clears the path, 800 steadies the grip and the wash, 450 sits them down."_

## ⚠️ The "ADA vs. BP 344" Exam Traps (Von Restorff Effect)

The ALE board examiners _want_ you to confuse foreign codes (ADA) with Philippine law (BP 344). **Burn these traps into your memory:**

1. ❌ **The Range Trap:** "Seat height is 430-480 mm."
   ✅ **The BP 344 Truth:** It is EXACTLY **450 mm**. Do not pick ranges.
2. ❌ **The Grab Bar Trap:** "Grab bars are at 800-900 mm."
   ✅ **The BP 344 Truth:** Toilets require EXACTLY **800 mm**. (700/900mm are for _stairs and ramps_).
3. ❌ **The Call Button Trap:** "Call button is 1200 mm high."
   ✅ **The BP 344 Truth:** It must be **400-600 mm** so a fallen person can reach it. 1200mm is for light switches!
4. ❌ **The Old Code Trap:** "Turning space is 1.50m x 1.50m."
   ✅ **The BP 344 Truth:** The 2024 IRR upgraded this. It is now **1.80m x 1.80m**.
