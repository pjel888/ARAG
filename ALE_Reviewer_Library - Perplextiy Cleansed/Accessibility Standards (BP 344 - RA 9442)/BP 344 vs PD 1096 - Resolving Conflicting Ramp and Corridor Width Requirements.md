# 🏛️ BP 344 (2024 IRR) vs. PD 1096: The "Stricter Rule" Framework

_An Immersive ALE Review Module – Mastering Legislative Conflict Resolution_

## 🚀 The Real-World Hook (The Liability Trap)

Picture yourself defending your floor plans. PD 1096 (National Building Code) says your corridor only needs to be **1.10 m** wide. But BP 344 (The Accessibility Law) says that if this hallway is part of an accessible route, it MUST be **1.20 m**. If you design for 1.10 m, a wheelchair user cannot pass a walking person, you fail the inspection, and you lose points on the ALE.

**The Golden Rule of Conflicts: The Stricter Rule Prevails.** In the Philippines, the Special Law (BP 344) _supersedes_ the General Law (PD 1096) for PWD-related routes.

## 🎯 The Legal Hierarchy (Schema Building)

1.  **PD 1096 (General Baseline):** Standards for "standard" egress and safety.
2.  **BP 344 (Special Precision):** Standards specifically for Persons with Disabilities (PWDs).
3.  **The Result:** On any route designated for PWDs (entrances, main halls, elevators), **BP 344 wipes the floor with PD 1096.**

## 📊 The Code Clash Cheat Sheet (Contrastive Learning)

_Notice how BP 344 is consistently wider, gentler, and more spacious than PD 1096._

| Feature              | PD 1096 (General Egress)     | BP 344 (2024 IRR Update)      | The ALE "Stricter" Winner 🏆 |
| :------------------- | :--------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :--------------------------- |
| **Clear Width**      | **1.10 m** (Occupants > 10)  | **1.20 m** (Accessible Route) | **1.20 m** (BP 344)          |
| **Max Ramp Slope**   | **1:8 (12.5%)** (Sec. 1007)  | **1:15 (6.67%)** (Max Limit)  | **1:15** (BP 344)            |
| **Max Rise per Run** | Not specified for egress     | **700 mm** (for 1:15 slope)   | **700 mm** (BP 344)          |
| **Landing Length**   | **1.20 m** (Width of stairs) | **1.80 m** (Ramp Landings)    | **1.80 m** (BP 344)          |
| **Turning Space**    | **1.50 m** (Turning circle)  | **1.80 m** (Turnabout zone)   | **1.80 m** (BP 344)          |

## ⚠️ The Board Exam Traps (Error Management Training)

Examiners want to see if you are "lazy-reading." **Self-Test These Score-Killers:**

1.  **The "Corridor" Trap:** A question asks for the minimum width of a corridor for 15 occupants _but doesn't mention PWDs_.
    - ❌ **BP 344 is not the answer.** Use PD 1096 (**1.10 m**).
    - ✅ If the question says "Accessible corridor," use BP 344 (**1.20 m**).
2.  **The "Slope" Trap:** "What is the maximum slope for an emergency egress ramp?"
    - ❌ **Don't pick 1:15.** PD 1096 allows a steeper **1:8** for general egress.
    - ✅ Only pick **1:15** (or **1:20**) if the word "Accessible" or "PWD" is in the prompt.
3.  **The "Old 1.50m" Trap:** Many old review books still use the 1.50m turning circle.
    - ❌ **Discard it.** The 2024 IRR has upgraded critical maneuver spaces (turning, corridors, landings) to **1.80 m**.

## 🧮 The "Gap" Computation (Cognitive Load Reduction)

**Scenario:** You have a rise of **700 mm**.

- **PD 1096 (1:8)**: You only need **5.6 meters** of horizontal distance.
- **BP 344 (1:15)**: You need **10.5 meters** of horizontal distance.
- **The Architect's Lesson:** Designing for accessibility roughly **doubles** your ramp's footprint. If you forget this during space planning, your building won't fit on the site.

## 🧠 Active Recall Battery (The Testing Effect)

**Q1:** In a PWD accessible corridor, two wheelchairs need to pass each other. What is the updated turning/passing space required?

> **Ans: 1.80 m x 1.80 m.**

**Q2:** You are designing a standard egress ramp for a warehouse (non-accessible). What is the maximum slope?

> **Ans: 1:8 (PD 1096).**

**Q3:** A hallway serves as the main entrance to a public library. What is the minimum clear width?

> **Ans: 1.20 m.** (Since public buildings must be accessible, BP 344's stricter 1.20m overrides PD 1096's 1.10m).

## 🔗 The "Protégé Effect" Challenge

Explain to a classmate why a 1:12 ramp (the old rule) is now "too steep" for the 2024 standards. If you can explain the physical effort required for a user to push themselves up a 1:15 slope vs. a 1:12 slope, you have mastered the intent of the law.
