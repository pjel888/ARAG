## 🚀 The Real-World Hook (The Parking Puzzle)

You are site-supervising a commercial retrofit. A wheelchair user arrives but cannot exit their van because the parking slot is too narrow. Later, they get stuck in a dead-end corridor because there isn't enough room to turn around. **Failure in spatial planning = an inaccessible building + a board exam score of zero.** Regardless of local codes, you must design for the physical dimensions of a wheelchair. For the ALE, we anchor on the **2024 Updated Standards** which aggressively expanded these spaces to reflect modern motorized wheelchairs.

## ⚠️ The Board Exam Traps (Error Management Training)

Examiners know you studied old materials. They will bait you with the pre-2024 numbers. **Dodge these specific traps:**

1.  **The "3700mm" Trap:** The question asks for the width of an accessible parking slot. Option A is 3.70m.
    - ❌ _Ignore it!_ 3.70m is the dead, old rule. The modern standard is **4000 mm (4.00 m)**.
2.  **The "Double Aisle" Trap:** "A 4000mm parking slot needs an _additional_ 1500mm access aisle."
    - ❌ _False!_ The 4000 mm total width _already includes_ the standard 2500 mm parking space plus the 1500 mm access aisle (2500 + 1500 = 4000).
3.  **The "1.50m Turn" Trap:** "The turning space required at a dead-end corridor is 1500 mm."
    - ❌ _False!_ 1500 mm is the old rule. The new standard turning circle/space is **1800 mm**.
4.  **The "General vs. Accessible" Trap:** Applying standard egress widths (e.g., 1.10 m) to a designated accessible corridor.
    - ❌ _False!_ Special accessibility needs always override general egress baselines. Use **1500 mm**.
