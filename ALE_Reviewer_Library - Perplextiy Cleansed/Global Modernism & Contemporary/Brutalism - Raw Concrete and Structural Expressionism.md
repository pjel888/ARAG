# Brutalism: Raw Concrete and Structural Expressionism
*An Immersive ALE Review Module*

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)
Imagine you are designing a new public building in the Philippines. You want to incorporate elements of Brutalist architecture, which emphasizes structural expression through exposed raw concrete. How would you ensure that your design complies with Philippine construction standards while adhering to this architectural style?

## Core Concepts
- **Brutalism**: An architectural movement characterized by the use of raw materials and minimalistic forms.
- **Raw Concrete**: Unadorned, unfinished concrete used in building structures.
- **Structural Expressionism**: A design approach that emphasizes visible structural elements.

## ALE Exam Traps and Common Mistakes
- **Misinterpreting Raw Concrete**: Candidates might confuse raw concrete with other types of finishes or materials.
- **Overlooking Structural Expressionism**: Failing to emphasize visible structural elements can result in a design that does not align with Brutalist principles.

## Additional Details

The preparation, signing, and dry-sealing of ALL architectural documents (including plans, designs, drawings, specifications, estimates, and similar materials) are exclusively within the scope of work for registered and licensed architects as defined under R.A. No. 9266, The Architecture Act of 2004, and its Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR). This includes architectural interiors which fall under the purview of registered and licensed interior designers according to R.A. No. 8534, The Interior Design Act of 1987.

The use of raw concrete in structural components such as floor slabs and partitions is specified with specific requirements for reinforcement cover and slab depth (e.g., Solid R.C. slab with an overall depth of 150 mm). Additionally, the preparation of civil/structural documents has been limited to registered and licensed engineers since the approval of R.A. No. 9266.

The design emphasis on structural expression through exposed concrete elements is a characteristic feature that aligns closely with Brutalist architecture but isn't explicitly named as such in official construction standards. This architectural style, known for its raw materials and minimalistic forms, has influenced building designs by highlighting the structure's inherent form without additional ornamentation or decoration.

The specifications provided under PD 1096 RIRR 2004 also detail fire resistance requirements for various types of assemblies and materials used in construction. For instance, solid concrete partitions are required to have a thickness that provides one-hour fire-resistance ratings as part of the overall building's structural integrity and safety measures.

These details collectively underscore the importance of adhering to specific architectural guidelines while allowing elements associated with Brutalist architecture to be incorporated into modern Philippine constructions through raw material usage and exposed structural forms.
