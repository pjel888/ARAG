## Additional Details

Inca Architecture is renowned for its sophisticated construction techniques, including ashlar masonry and terracing, which were used to create some of South America's most impressive engineering marvels. Although Incan architectural practices are not directly addressed in Philippine official documents or regulations, understanding these methods provides insight into advanced pre-Columbian building technologies.

### Ashlar Masonry
Ashlar masonry is a construction technique characterized by precisely cut and fitted stones without the use of mortar to hold them together. This method was extensively used by the Incas for their monumental structures such as Machu Picchu, where each stone fits so perfectly that no blade can be inserted between them. The precision in cutting and fitting these stones demonstrates an advanced understanding of geometry and engineering principles.

### Terracing
Terracing is a farming technique but also plays a significant role in Incan architecture by stabilizing slopes and creating flat areas for construction on mountainous terrain. By building terraces, the Incas were able to construct large urban centers like Cusco and fortresses such as Sacsayhuaman at elevations where traditional foundations would be impractical or impossible.

### Engineering Marvels
Inca engineering marvels include not only their architectural feats but also sophisticated water management systems. For instance, they built extensive networks of aqueducts to supply cities with fresh water from distant sources and constructed drainage channels that prevented flooding in urban areas. These innovations showcase the Incas' deep understanding of hydrology and civil engineering.

While these practices are specific to Incan civilization rather than Philippine architecture, studying them can provide valuable lessons on sustainable construction techniques and environmental integration.
