# PD 1096 Rule VII: Building Height Limits (BHL), FLAR, and GFA/TGFA Computations
*An Immersive ALE Review Module*

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)
Imagine you are designing a commercial building in an urban area with strict zoning laws. You need to determine the maximum allowable height of your structure based on its use or occupancy category while considering factors like Floor to Lot Area Ratio (FLAR) and Total Gross Floor Area (TGFA).

## Core Concepts
- **Building Height Limit (BHL)**: The maximum allowed height for a building/structure, excluding projections such as signage.
  - [Source File, Rule VII]
  
- **Floor to Lot Area Ratio (FLAR)**: A ratio that determines the allowable Gross Floor Area based on Total Lot Area and use or occupancy category.
  - [Source File, Table VII.G.1]

- **Gross Floor Area (GFA)**: The total floor space within a building's perimeter walls, including main and auxiliary buildings but excluding non-GFA areas like parking lots.
  - [Source File, Rule VIII]
  
- **Total Gross Floor Area (TGFA)**: Includes GFA plus all other enclosed support areas above and below grade level attached to the building.
  - [Source File, Table VII.G.2]

## The Hard Data (Cheat Sheet)
| Building Use | FLAR Designation/ Rights | Maximum BHL (m) |
|--------------|--------------------------|------------------|
| Residential R-4/R-5 | Up to 18 storeys or 54 m | -              |
| Commercial C-2       | Up to 6 storeys or 18 m   | -              |
| Commercial C-3       | 16-60 storeys, up to 180 m | -              |

## Step-by-Step Computation (If Applicable)
**Example Calculation:**
Given a Total Lot Area of 800 sqm and an FLAR designation of 9.0 for Commercial C-3 use:
\[ \text{GFA} = TLA \times FLAR \]
\[ GFA = 800 \, \text{sqm} \times 9.0 \]
\[ GFA = 7200 \, \text{sqm} \]

**TGFA Calculation:**
Using Table VII.G.2:
- Conversion multiplier from TGFA to GFA is 1.5.
\[ \text{TGFA} = \frac{\text{GFA}}{1.5} \]
\[ TGFA = \frac{7200}{1.5} \]
\[ TGFA = 4800 \, \text{sqm} \]

## ALE Exam Traps and Common Mistakes
- **Misinterpreting FLAR values**: Higher FLAR allows for taller buildings but must be calculated accurately.
- **Omitting non-GFA areas in TGFA computation**: Including all enclosed support areas is crucial to avoid underestimating the total area.
