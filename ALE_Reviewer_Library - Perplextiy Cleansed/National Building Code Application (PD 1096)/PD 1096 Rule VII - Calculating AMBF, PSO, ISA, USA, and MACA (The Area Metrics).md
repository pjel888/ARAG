## The Scenario (See It Before You Read It)

You are standing on a vacant 500 sq.m lot in Quezon City, zoned Basic R-2. Your client hands you a sketch: "I want the biggest house possible, and pave everything else for cars."

You look at the sketch and immediately know three things:

1. The building cannot cover more than **55%** of the lot (no firewall) or **60%** (with firewall).
2. At least **15%** (no firewall) or **10%** (with firewall) of the lot must remain as exposed soil and planting.
3. The total hard surface — building plus paving — cannot exceed **85%** (no firewall) or **90%** (with firewall).

**How do you know this instantly?** Because you have internalized Table VIII.1. This module teaches you to do exactly that.

### The Six Terms — Defined Once, Defined Right

> All definitions below are drawn verbatim from **Section 803 of Rule VIII, 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096**.

| #   | Term     | Full Name                            | What It Is                                                                                                                                          | Unit |
| --- | -------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| 1   | **TLA**  | Total Lot Area                       | The entire area of the lot within property lines                                                                                                    | sq.m |
| 2   | **PSO**  | Percentage of Site Occupancy         | AMBF ÷ TLA — the maximum percentage of the lot that may be covered by an enclosed building at the ground floor                                      | %    |
| 3   | **AMBF** | Allowable Maximum Building Footprint | The actual area (in sq.m) of the building at ground level. The "true buildable area on which a fully enclosed building/structure can be erected."   | sq.m |
| 4   | **ISA**  | Impervious Surface Area              | Maximum allowable paved, tiled, or hardscaped open surface at ground level, as a percentage of TLA                                                  | %    |
| 5   | **USA**  | Unpaved Surface Area                 | Minimum required portion of the lot with exposed soil and planting (softscaped) — "this is the true open space" per the IRR                         | %    |
| 6   | **TOSL** | Total Open Space within Lot          | All area NOT occupied by the building: courts, yards, gardens, light wells, uncovered driveways, access roads, and parking spaces. TOSL = ISA + USA | %    |
| 7   | **MACA** | Maximum Allowable Construction Area  | Combined total of PSO + ISA — everything "hard" on the lot                                                                                          | %    |

### The Four Governing Equations

Memorize these. They are the skeleton key.

| Equation              | In Words                              | Why It Matters                                                             |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PSO + TOSL = 100%** | Building + Open = Whole Lot           | If one goes up, the other must go down                                     |
| **TOSL = ISA + USA**  | Open Space = Hardscape + Softscape    | TOSL is not just "yard" — it includes paved open areas                     |
| **MACA = PSO + ISA**  | All Hard Surfaces = Building + Paving | The cap on everything impervious                                           |
| **TLA = MACA + USA**  | Hard + Soft = Whole Lot               | The simplest check — if your numbers do not add to 100%, you made an error |

**Chant this three times:** _"PSO plus TOSL is the lot. TOSL is ISA plus USA. MACA is PSO plus ISA."_

## Reason for the Law: Why These Rules Exist

Understanding the legislative intent makes the numbers unforgettable.

| Rule             | Why the Government Mandates It                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PSO cap**      | Prevents "wall-to-wall" buildings that block light and air to neighboring properties. Protects the right to natural ventilation and daylight — a public health concern in dense Metro Manila.              |
| **USA minimum**  | Ensures surface water percolation (rainwater soaking into the ground), aquifer recharge, heat absorption, and soil ventilation. Without this, Metro Manila floods worsen and urban heat islands intensify. |
| **ISA cap**      | Limits total impervious surface to reduce stormwater runoff. Paved areas generate runoff almost as aggressively as buildings.                                                                              |
| **TOSL minimum** | Guarantees a minimum quantum of "breathing room" — open sky above the lot — for light, ventilation, and emergency access.                                                                                  |
| **MACA cap**     | The umbrella control: even if you technically meet PSO and ISA individually, MACA ensures you cannot game the system by maximizing both.                                                                   |

**Emotional Anchor:** Every square meter of USA you protect is a square meter where rainwater can soak into the ground instead of flooding a family's home downstream. This is not abstract — this is Ondoy, Yolanda, and Carina.

### Pattern Recognition (Chunking)

Lock these patterns into memory:

- **PSO + TOSL always = 100%.** If you know one, you know the other.
- **PSO + ISA = MACA.** Always.
- **ISA + USA = TOSL.** Always.
- **Adding a firewall increases PSO** (you can build bigger) **but decreases or maintains USA** (you still need greenery).
- **Institutional has the lowest PSO (50%)** and the **highest USA (30%)** — schools and hospitals need the most open space.
- **Com-3 with firewall has the highest PSO (90%)** — dense CBD lots can be built almost wall-to-wall.

**Immediate Action:** Cover the table. From memory, write the Basic R-2 (without firewall) row: PSO **_, ISA _**, USA **_, TOSL _**. Check. Repeat until you get 55-30-15-45 without looking.

## High-Yield Exam Traps (Von Restorff Isolation)

These are the errors that cost passing candidates their license. Read each one aloud.

---

### TRAP 1: "Table VIII.1 = Inside Lot vs. Corner Lot"

**The Mistake:** Believing that Table VIII.1 gives different values for inside lots and corner lots.

**The Truth:** Table VIII.1 distinguishes by **firewall status** (with or without), NOT by lot type. The inside-lot vs. corner-lot distinction is governed by **Table VII.1** (PSO caps per occupancy and lot type) and **Table VIII.G.6** (TOSL requirements by lot type).

**Why It Matters:** When the exam gives you a corner lot, you do NOT simply look up "corner lot" in Table VIII.1. You check Table VIII.1 for firewall-based PSO, Table VII.1 for lot-type-based PSO, and Table VIII.G.6 for lot-type-based TOSL — then apply the **most restrictive**.

---

### TRAP 2: "TOSL = Front Setback"

**The Mistake:** Thinking TOSL only refers to the front yard.

**The Truth:** Per Sec. 803.3.b, TOSL includes "courts, yards, gardens, light wells, uncovered driveways, access roads and parking spaces" — the entire area outside the building footprint.

**Why It Matters:** An exam question may describe a building with a large front setback but tiny side/rear yards. The total must still meet or exceed the TOSL minimum.

---

### TRAP 3: Confusing MACA with AMBF

**The Mistake:** Thinking MACA is the building footprint.

**The Truth:** AMBF is the building footprint (in sq.m). MACA = PSO + ISA (in %) — it includes the building AND all paved surfaces outside. MACA is always larger than PSO.

**Mnemonic:** **M**ACA = **M**ore than just the building. **A**MBF = just the **A**ctual building.

---

### TRAP 4: Mixing Percentages with Square Meters

**The Mistake:** Writing "PSO = 275 sq.m" on the exam.

**The Truth:** PSO, ISA, USA, and TOSL are percentages. AMBF is the area in square meters. The conversion:

`AMBF (sq.m) = TLA (sq.m) × PSO (%)`

**Why It Matters:** Examiners design distractors that swap units. If the answer choices include both "55%" and "275 sq.m" (for a 500 sq.m lot), they are testing whether you know which term uses which unit.

---

### TRAP 5: Forgetting the "Most Restrictive" Rule

**The Mistake:** Using only Table VIII.1 to determine PSO.

**The Truth:** Per Rule VII, Sec. 704.4.a — if the values from Table VIII.1, Tables VIII.2/VIII.3 (setbacks), Table VII.1 (occupancy/lot-type PSO), and the Fire Code conflict, **the more stringent but applicable regulation shall be observed.**

**Example:** Table VIII.1 says Basic R-2 (with firewall) PSO = 60%. But if Table VII.1 says the inside-lot PSO for that building height is only 55%, you must use 55%.

## Worked Example (Full Calculation Chain)

**Problem:** A single-family dwelling on a Basic R-2 lot. TLA = 500 sq.m. No firewall. Calculate all six metrics.

| Step | Metric      | Formula                                    | Calculation | Result       |
| ---- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------- | ------------ |
| 1    | Max PSO     | From Table VIII.1 (Basic R-2, no firewall) | —           | **55%**      |
| 2    | AMBF        | TLA × PSO                                  | 500 × 0.55  | **275 sq.m** |
| 3    | Min TOSL    | 100% − PSO                                 | 100% − 55%  | **45%**      |
| 4    | TOSL (sq.m) | TLA × TOSL%                                | 500 × 0.45  | **225 sq.m** |
| 5    | Max ISA     | From Table VIII.1                          | —           | **30%**      |
| 6    | ISA (sq.m)  | TLA × ISA                                  | 500 × 0.30  | **150 sq.m** |
| 7    | Min USA     | From Table VIII.1                          | —           | **15%**      |
| 8    | USA (sq.m)  | TLA × USA                                  | 500 × 0.15  | **75 sq.m**  |
| 9    | MACA        | PSO + ISA                                  | 55% + 30%   | **85%**      |
| 10   | MACA (sq.m) | TLA × MACA                                 | 500 × 0.85  | **425 sq.m** |

**Self-Check:** Does AMBF (275) + ISA area (150) + USA area (75) = TLA (500)? → 275 + 150 + 75 = 500. Correct.

## Board Exam Practice Problem

**Question:** An architect is designing a single-family residential project on a 1,000 sq.m Basic R-2 lot without a firewall. If the architect maximizes the building footprint (AMBF) and also maximizes the allowed paved driveways and walkways (ISA), what is the exact square meter area that MUST be dedicated strictly to soil, lawns, or planting (USA)?

A) 100 sq.m  
B) 150 sq.m  
C) 300 sq.m  
D) 450 sq.m

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer and Explanation</b></summary>
<br>
<b>Correct Answer: B) 150 sq.m</b><br><br>
<b>Step-by-step:</b><br>
Step 1: Identify the zone → Basic R-2, no firewall.<br>
Step 2: From Table VIII.1 → PSO = 55%, ISA = 30%, USA = 15%.<br>
Step 3: The question asks for USA in square meters.<br>
Step 4: USA = 1,000 sq.m × 15% = <b>150 sq.m</b>.<br><br>
<b>Cross-check with MACA:</b> MACA = 55% + 30% = 85%. Therefore 15% must remain unpaved. 15% × 1,000 = 150 sq.m.<br><br>
<b>Why not 100 sq.m?</b> That answer (10%) would be correct for Basic R-2 <i>with firewall</i> — a common distractor that exploits confusion between the firewall and non-firewall rows of Table VIII.1.
</details>
