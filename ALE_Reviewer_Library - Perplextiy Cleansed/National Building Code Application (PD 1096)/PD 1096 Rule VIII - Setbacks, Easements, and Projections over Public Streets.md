## PART 1 — SETBACKS (YARDS)

### Source: Rule VIII, Section 804, 2004 Revised IRR

---

### 🎬 VISUALIZATION: WHY SETBACKS EXIST

> _You are standing at the front door of a newly built house in a subdivision. Look left — the neighbor's wall is barely a meter away. There is no sunlight. There is no airflow. If a fire starts, the flames jump the gap in seconds._

> _This is why PD 1096 mandates yards. A "yard" is the required open space between the outermost face of the building and the property line. The width of that yard IS the setback. Setbacks serve three critical purposes:_

1. **Light & Ventilation** — Natural air and sunlight must reach every habitable room.
2. **Fire Separation** — Distance slows fire spread between adjacent buildings.
3. **Public Safety & Aesthetics** — Uniform setbacks create orderly streetscapes and ensure emergency access.

> **Reason for the Law:** The Philippines is a tropical country with dense urban areas. Without mandated open space, buildings would consume entire lots, creating dark, unventilated fire traps — exactly the condition that PD 1096 was enacted to prevent.

---

### KEY DEFINITION

> **YARD** = the required open space left between the **outermost face** of the building/structure and the **property lines** (front, rear, right side, left side). **The width of the yard is the setback.**
> _(Rule VIII, Section 804, Subsection 3, 2004 Revised IRR)_

---

### TABLE 1: RESIDENTIAL SETBACKS — Table VIII.2 (2004 Revised IRR)

**Title: Minimum Setbacks for Residential Buildings/Structures**

| YARD      | R-1 (m)  | R-2 Basic (m) | R-2 Maximum (m) | R-3 Basic (m) | R-3 Maximum (m) | R-4 individual lot/unit (m) | R-5 (m) |
| --------- | -------- | ------------- | --------------- | ------------- | --------------- | --------------------------- | ------- |
| **Front** | **4.50** | 3.00          | 8.00 \*         | 3.00          | 8.00 \*         | 4.50                        | 6.00    |
| **Side**  | **2.00** | 2.00 \*\*     | 2.00 \*\*       | \*\*\*        | 2.00 (optional) | 2.00 (optional)             | 3.00    |
| **Rear**  | **2.00** | 2.00          | 2.00            | \*\*\*        | 2.00            | 2.00                        | 3.00    |

**NOTES (from the IRR — these are exam-critical):**

- **(a)** These setback requirements are for **newly-developed subdivisions**.
- **(\*)** Front setback = Total setback at grade only, i.e., 3.00 m + 5.00 m = **8.00 m** (to accommodate part of the minimum parking requirement outside the front yard). Second and upper floors comply with the minimum **3.00 m** setback.
- **(\*\*)** Setback required for only **one (1) side**. Setbacks on two sides shall be **optional**.
- **(\*\*\*)** R-3 Basic: Abutments on two sides and rear property lines may be allowed with conditions under Section 804, Subsection 10.
- **R-5:** Mixed-Use Buildings in R-5 lots shall be considered commercial if a substantial percentage (i.e., **55% of GFA**) is commercial.
- **(f)** Where yards/setbacks are impossible to attain (e.g., Open Market or Medium Cost Housing Projects), abutments on sides and rear may be allowed and **1.50 m** front yard is left open as transition area.

---

### ⚠️ TRAP ALERT #1 — The 4.50m R-1 Front Setback

> **The exam may ask:** "What is the minimum front setback for an R-1 residential lot?"
> **Answer: 4.50 meters.**
>
> Students frequently confuse this with the R-2/R-3 basic front setback of 3.00 m. Remember: **R-1 is the lowest density residential zone (single-detached dwelling). It gets the LARGEST default front yard because the lot is bigger and the intent is maximum open space.**
>
> Mnemonic: **"R-1 = 4 and a half, R-2/3 Basic = 3 flat"**

---

### TABLE 2: NON-RESIDENTIAL SETBACKS — Table VIII.3 (2004 Revised IRR)

**Title: Setbacks for Commercial, Industrial, Institutional, and Recreational Buildings**

| RROW Width (m)    | Front (m) | Side (m) | Rear (m) |
| ----------------- | --------- | -------- | -------- |
| **30.00 & above** | **8.00**  | 5.00     | 5.00     |
| 25.00 to 29.00    | 6.00      | 3.00     | 3.00     |
| 20.00 to 24.00    | 5.00      | 3.00     | 3.00     |
| 10.00 to 19.00    | 5.00      | 2.00     | 2.00     |
| Below 10.00       | 5.00      | 2.00     | 2.00     |

**CRITICAL NOTE:** These are for **newly-developed thoroughfares**. For highly built-up urban areas with duly established lines and grades reflecting proposed road widening, the Table VIII.3 requirements may **NOT** be imposed. The building face may abut on the side and/or rear property lines provided all requirements on open space, window opening, artificial ventilation, and **firewalls (Rule VII)** are first fully complied with. _(Section 804, Subsection 4, 2004 Revised IRR)_

---

### ⚠️ TRAP ALERT #2 — Residential vs. Non-Residential Setback Tables

> The exam may present a Commercial lot along a 20m RROW and ask for the front setback. Students who memorized only Table VIII.2 (Residential) will get it wrong.
>
> **Rule:** Residential setbacks are by **zoning density classification** (R-1 through R-5).
> Non-residential setbacks are by **RROW width**.
> **These are two completely different systems in two different tables.**

---

### ABUTMENT RULES (Section 804, Subsection 4c, 2004 Revised IRR)

Abutments (building walls built directly on the property line = zero setback) follow rules similar to firewalls under Rule VII:

| Lot Type | Abutments Allowed?       | Where?                                   |
| -------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| **R-1**  | **ABSOLUTELY NONE**      | No abutments at any property line        |
| **R-2**  | 1 side only              | No firewalls/abutments on front and rear |
| **R-3**  | 2 sides OR 1 side + rear | No abutments on front property line      |
| **R-4**  | 2 sides only             | No firewalls/abutments on front and rear |
| **R-5**  | 2 sides OR 1 side + rear | No abutments on front property line      |
| **C-3**  | 2 sides OR 1 side + rear | No abutments on front property line      |

> **Reason for the Law:** R-1 lots are single-detached dwellings — they must be free-standing on all sides, which is why firewalls are **absolutely prohibited** for R-1.

---

### INCREMENTAL SETBACK RULE (Section 804, Subsection 6)

> For buildings **more than 2 storeys** in height, the minimum width of the rear or side court shall be **increased at the rate of 300 mm for each additional storey** up to the **14th storey**.
>
> For buildings exceeding 14 storeys, the required court width is computed on the basis of **14 storeys** (i.e., the increment caps at 14).

**Worked Example:**

- A 10-storey commercial building, RROW = 25m
- Base side setback from Table VIII.3 = 3.00 m
- Additional storeys above 2 = 10 − 2 = 8
- Increment = 8 × 0.30 m = 2.40 m
- **Total side court width = 3.00 + 2.40 = 5.40 m**

> **Draw This:** Sketch a stepped building section — the upper floors step back from the property line like a wedding cake. This is the incremental setback shown in **Figure VIII.20** of the IRR.

---

### LOT TYPES (Figures VIII.2 through VIII.8)

The IRR identifies exactly **seven (7) lot types** and their respective locations:

| #   | Lot Type                                         | Description                                    | TOSL Required                                |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Interior Lot**                                 | Frontage on 1 street only                      | 50% (Groups A,B,C,D & J) / 20% (E,F,G,H & I) |
| 2   | **Inside Lot**                                   | Similar to Interior but smaller frontage ratio | 20% / 15%                                    |
| 3   | **Corner Lot**                                   | At intersection of 2 streets                   | 10% / 5%                                     |
| 4   | **Through Lot**                                  | Frontage on 2 parallel streets                 | 10% / 5%                                     |
| 5   | **Corner-Through Lot**                           | Corner + through (3 frontages)                 | 5% for all Groups                            |
| 6   | **End Lot**                                      | End of a row (1 street + 1 side exposed)       | 30-50% / 20-50%                              |
| 7   | **Corner Lot Abutting 3+ Streets/Alleys/Rivers** | Maximum exposure                               | 5% for all Groups                            |

> **Reason for these categories:** A corner lot has TWO streets providing light and ventilation — so it needs less open space on the lot itself. An interior lot with only ONE street frontage needs MORE open space within the lot to compensate.

---

### VARIANCE PROVISION (Section 803, Subsection 4e)

When lots are too narrow or too shallow such that the RROW/alley can adequately supply light and ventilation, TOSL requirements **may be waived**, provided:

- Lots abutting **1 public open space**: open space depth ≤ **8.00 m**
- Lots abutting **2 or more public open spaces**: open space depth ≤ **12.00 m**

---

### EASEMENTS — Quick Reference

Easements are NOT defined in PD 1096 itself but are commonly tested in the context of site planning:

| Easement Type                | Width                                                                     | Governing Law                 |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **River/Creek (3m+ wide)**   | **3.00 m** from bank on each side                                         | PD 1067 (Water Code), Art. 51 |
| **Estero/Small creek (<3m)** | **1.00 m** minimum on each side                                           | PD 1067, Art. 51              |
| **Shoreline (salvage zone)** | **20.00 m** from mean high tide (agricultural); **40.00 m** (forest land) | PD 1067, Art. 51              |
| **Power transmission lines** | Varies by voltage; consult NEA/NGCP                                       | RA 9136 (EPIRA)               |
| **Road Right-of-Way (RROW)** | Per local zoning ordinance / DPWH classification                          | PD 1096 + LGU ZO              |

> **Reason for Easement Laws:** Rivers flood. Power lines spark. The easement creates a buffer that protects lives and property AND preserves public access to waterways.

> **Exam Context:** The design problem in Area 3 frequently places a river or estero along one side of the lot. You MUST deduct the easement from your buildable area before computing AMBF. Forgetting this is an automatic point deduction.

## PART 2 — BUILDING PROJECTIONS OVER PUBLIC STREETS

### Source: PD 1096, Chapter 10 (Rule X), Sections 1001–1008

---

### 🎬 VISUALIZATION: WHY PROJECTION RULES EXIST

> _You are walking along a narrow Manila sidewalk. Above you, a balcony juts out over the street. Below you, unseen, a building footing extends past the property line into the public right-of-way. Above the shop entrance, a canopy shelters pedestrians from rain._

> _Every single one of these projections — the balcony overhead, the footing underground, the canopy above — is regulated by Chapter 10 of PD 1096. The law balances two competing needs: the building owner's desire to maximize usable space versus the public's right to safe, unobstructed streets._

> **Reason for the Law:** Metro Manila streets are narrow. Unregulated projections would create dark tunnels, trap vehicle exhaust at street level, obstruct emergency access, and endanger pedestrians. These rules create a vertical "envelope" within which projections are permitted.

---

### GENERAL RULE (Section 1001)

**(a)** No part of any building or structure or any of its appendages shall project **beyond the property line** of the building site, except as provided in this Code.

**(b)** The projection is measured **horizontally** from the property line to the outermost point of the projection.

---

### UNDERGROUND PROJECTIONS — Section 1002

**Section 1002(a):** No part of any structure or its appendage shall project into any alley or street, national road, or public highway except as provided in the Code.

---

#### ⚠️ CRITICAL — TWO SEPARATE Underground Provisions (NOT One Table)

The review material you may encounter often conflates these into one "depth progression table." They are **two distinct provisions** for **two different contexts**:

---

**PROVISION 1 — Section 1002(b): FOOTINGS along National Roads**

| Parameter               | Requirement                                       |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **What:**               | Footings (not foundations)                        |
| **Where:**              | Along national roads or public highways           |
| **Minimum depth:**      | At least **2.40 meters below grade**              |
| **Maximum projection:** | Not more than **300 mm** beyond the property line |

> _Think of this as: Deep footings on big roads — small projection allowed._

---

**PROVISION 2 — Section 1002(c): FOUNDATIONS into Public Sidewalks**

| Parameter                               | Requirement                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What:**                               | Foundations (broader than footings)                                                                                       |
| **Where:**                              | Into **public sidewalk areas**                                                                                            |
| **Maximum encroachment width:**         | Not exceeding **500 mm**                                                                                                  |
| **Minimum depth of top of foundation:** | Not less than **600 mm below established grade**                                                                          |
| **Additional condition:**               | Must not obstruct existing utilities (power, communication, gas, water, sewer). If it does, the owner pays for rerouting. |

> _Think of this as: Shallow foundations on sidewalks — wider projection allowed but must clear utilities._

---

### ⚠️ TRAP ALERT #3 — These Are NOT the Same Rule

> **WRONG (common exam error):** "Footings at 2.40m depth project 300mm; foundations at 600mm depth project 500mm" — presented as if this were a single graduated table.
>
> **RIGHT:** Section 1002(b) applies to **footings along national roads** at ≥2.40m depth (max 300mm projection). Section 1002(c) applies to **foundations into public sidewalks** (max 500mm width, top ≥600mm below grade). **Different building elements. Different locations. Different rules.**
>
> Mnemonic: **"F-3-2 / F-5-6"**
>
> - **F**ooting: **3**00mm projection, **2**.40m depth
> - **F**oundation: **5**00mm width, **6**00mm depth

---

### OVERHEAD PROJECTIONS — Sections 1003–1007

These are the elements that project ABOVE the street. Each has its own clearance requirement:

| Element                    | Section     | Minimum Vertical Clearance      | Notes                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Balconies & appendages** | **1003(b)** | **3.00 m** from grade/sidewalk  | Projection extent per Table 1003-A; must be uniform within a block                           |
| **Arcades**                | 1004(a)     | **3.00 m** from sidewalk grade  | Required by zoning when applicable                                                           |
| **Canopies (Marquees)**    | 1005(b)     | **3.00 m** from pavement/ground | Horizontal clearance from curb: ≥300mm; must be incombustible, ≥2-hr fire-resistive          |
| **Movable Awnings/Hoods**  | 1006        | **2.40 m** from pavement/ground | Horizontal clearance from curb: ≥300mm; must not block exits when collapsed                  |
| **Doors & Windows**        | 1007        | **2.40 m**                      | Doors/windows <2.40m above ground shall NOT project beyond property line (except fire exits) |

---

### ⚠️ TRAP ALERT #4 — Balcony Clearance is 3.00m, NOT 2.40m

> **This is the single most dangerous error in circulating review materials.**
>
> **WRONG:** "Balcony clearance = 2.40 m"
> **RIGHT:** **Balcony clearance = 3.00 m** (PD 1096, Section 1003(b))
>
> The **2.40 m** value applies to:
>
> - **Awnings** (Section 1006) — movable/retractable shelters
> - **Doors and windows** (Section 1007) — the threshold below which they cannot project
>
> **Why the confusion?** The 2.40m figure appears prominently in the Code for both awnings AND doors/windows, while the 3.00m figure appears once for balconies. Students who study quickly grab the more "common" number. The Board knows this and tests it.
>
> **Memory Trick:** A balcony is **permanent and structural** — it needs MORE clearance (3.00m). An awning is **lightweight and retractable** — it needs LESS clearance (2.40m). **Permanent = higher. Retractable = lower.**

---

### ARCADES — Section 1004

| Parameter             | Requirement                                                          |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **When required:**    | When existing building and zoning regulations require them           |
| **Minimum height:**   | **3.00 m** above established sidewalk grade                          |
| **Width and height:** | Uniform throughout the street                                        |
| **Construction:**     | Shall not obstruct access to standpipe connections or stairway exits |

> **Reason for the Law:** Arcades create covered pedestrian walkways on the ground floor — especially important in a tropical country with heavy rains and intense sun. They also serve as a public benefit in exchange for allowing the building to extend over the sidewalk.

---

### CANOPIES vs. AWNINGS — Know the Difference

| Feature                            | Canopy (Marquee) — Sec. 1005          | Awning — Sec. 1006                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Permanence**                     | Permanent, fixed                      | Movable, retractable, collapsible     |
| **Support**                        | Attached to and supported by building | Supported entirely from exterior wall |
| **Construction**                   | Incombustible, ≥2-hr fire-resistive   | No fire-rating requirement stated     |
| **Vertical clearance**             | **3.00 m**                            | **2.40 m**                            |
| **Horizontal clearance from curb** | ≥300 mm                               | ≥300 mm                               |
| **Drainage**                       | Must have drainage facility           | Not specified                         |

> **Exam Shortcut:** If the question says "permanent roofed structure," it's a **canopy** (3.00m). If it says "movable" or "retractable," it's an **awning** (2.40m).

---

### CORNER BUILDINGS — Section 1008 (Chaflans)

| Parameter          | Requirement                                                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **When required:** | Corner building or solid fence on a public street/alley **less than 3.60 m** wide                            |
| **What:**          | Corner must be truncated (chamfered)                                                                         |
| **Angle:**         | The face of the triangle formed is at **right angles to the bisector** of the intersection angle             |
| **Exemption:**     | If the building is **arcaded**, no chaflan is required regardless of street width (provided street < 12.00m) |

> **Reason for the Law:** At narrow street intersections, corner buildings without chaflans create dangerous blind spots for vehicles and pedestrians. The diagonal cut provides sight lines.

---

### RROW ANGLES & THE OFB (Outer Face of Building)

The 2004 Revised IRR introduced **angular plane diagrams** (Figures in the PRBOA interpretations) that determine how high a building can go based on the RROW width. Key setback values from these diagrams:

| RROW Width | R-2 (Max) Front Setback | R-4 Front Setback | R-5 Front Setback | C-1 Front Setback | C-2 Front Setback |
| ---------- | ----------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------- | ----------------- | ----------------- |
| 6.00 m     | —                       | —                 | —                 | —                 | —                 |
| 10.00 m    | —                       | —                 | —                 | —                 | —                 |
| 20.00 m    | 8.00 m                  | 4.50 m            | 6.00 m            | 5.00 m            | 6.00 m            |
| 26.00 m    | —                       | —                 | —                 | 6.00 m            | 6.00 m            |
| 40.00 m    | —                       | —                 | —                 | 8.00 m            | —                 |

> These values are drawn from the **PRBOA annotated RROW angle diagrams** (Initial Interpretations of the Application of the NBCP IRR-prescribed RROW Angles, www.architectureboard.ph). The angular plane concept determines the **OFB (Outer Face of Building)** — the maximum line beyond which no building wall or roof may extend.

> **Key Concept:** Above the **9th floor**, buildings must implement **incremental setbacks per storey** — each upper floor steps back further from the OFB line.

---

### TABLE 1003-A — Projection of Balconies and Appendages

Per Section 1003(a), the extent of projection over an alley or street shall be **uniform within a block** and shall conform to **Table 1003-A** in the Code (Annex B-2). The actual projection distances depend on RROW width. The key principle: the narrower the street, the smaller the allowed projection.

## PART 3 — PSO / TOSL INTEGRATION (Table VIII.1 Reference)

### ⚠️ TRAP ALERT #5 — Table VIII.1 Is NOT a Setback Table

> **Table VIII.1** provides the **Percentage of Site Occupancy (PSO)**, **ISA**, **USA**, and **TOSL** values for each land use classification. It does NOT contain setback dimensions. The setback dimensions are in **Table VIII.2** (Residential) and **Table VIII.3** (Commercial/Industrial/Institutional/Recreational).
>
> Students who confuse the three tables will misapply zoning data as setback data — a catastrophic error on the design exam.

### Quick PSO Reference (from Table VIII.1)

| Land Use             | PSO (without firewall) | PSO (with firewall) | TOSL    |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------- | ------- |
| Residential 1 (R-1)  | —                      | —                   | 50%     |
| Residential 2 (R-2)  | —                      | —                   | —       |
| Commercial 1 (Com-1) | 80                     | 80                  | 20      |
| Commercial 2 (Com-2) | 75 (e) / 85 (f)        | 80 (e) / 85 (f)     | 25 / 15 |
| Commercial 3 (Com-3) | 80 (e) / 90 (f)        | 70 (e)              | 20 / 10 |
| Industrial 1 (Ind-1) | 70 (e) / 80 (f)        | 70 (e) / 80 (f)     | 30 / 20 |
| Institutional        | 50 (e) / 60 (f)        | 50 (e) / 60 (f)     | 40 / 50 |

_(e) = without firewall; (f) = with firewall. Full table in Rule VIII, 2004 Revised IRR, pages 131–132.)_

> **Integration Principle:** PSO/TOSL tells you **how much** of the lot you can cover. Setback tables tell you **where** the open space must be located. You need BOTH to design a compliant building.

## PART 4 — SYNTHESIS: FROM LOT TO BUILDING ENVELOPE

### The Complete Design Workflow for ALE Area 3
```

Step 1: Identify LOT TYPE (7 types — Figures VIII.2 through VIII.8)
↓
Step 2: Check ZONING CLASSIFICATION → Get PSO, FLAR, BHL from Table VIII.1
↓
Step 3: Deduct EASEMENTS (creek, RROW, power lines)
↓
Step 4: Apply SETBACKS from Table VIII.2 (Residential) or Table VIII.3 (Non-Residential)
↓
Step 5: Compute AMBF = TLA × PSO
↓
Step 6: Check that TOSL = TLA − AMBF meets minimum %
↓
Step 7: Compute Total Allowable Floor Area = TLA × FLAR
↓
Step 8: Determine Max Floors = Total Floor Area ÷ AMBF
↓
Step 9: Apply BHL and INCREMENTAL SETBACKS above 2nd floor (300mm/storey, cap at 14th)
↓
Step 10: Check RROW ANGLES → OFB cannot exceed angular plane
↓
Step 11: Apply PROJECTION RULES for any balconies, canopies, footings over property line

```

### Key Mnemonics to Chant

Chant each mnemonic **3 times aloud** right now:

1. **"R-1 = 4 and a half, R-2/3 Basic = 3 flat"** → Front setback values
2. **"F-3-2 / F-5-6"** → Footing: 300mm/2.40m | Foundation: 500mm/600mm
3. **"Permanent 3, Retractable 2.4"** → Canopy/balcony vs. awning clearance
4. **"300 per storey, cap at 14"** → Incremental setback rule
5. **"R-1: Zero abutments, period."** → Strictest abutment rule
