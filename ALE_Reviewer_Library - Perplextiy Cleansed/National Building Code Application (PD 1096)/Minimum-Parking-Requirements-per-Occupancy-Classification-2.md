# ALE Mastery: Minimum Parking Requirements per Occupancy Classification

**Module Focus: Off-Street Parking Standards, Slot Dimensions & Occupancy Ratios**
**Authority: PD 1096 (National Building Code), 2004 Revised IRR, BP 344, DPWH DO 059 s. 2019**

---

## Schema Activation: The Basement That Kills the Budget

You are designing a 10-story condominium in Quezon City. The developer insists on 80 residential units at 48 sq.m. GFA each to maximize sales revenue. You start with two basement levels.

If you misapply the parking ratio — using the old table instead of the 2004 Revised IRR, or rounding down instead of up — you will either:

- **Under-provide** parking and fail to get a building permit, or
- **Over-provide** parking and waste millions on unnecessary basement excavation.

In the ALE, parking questions appear in nearly every sitting. The exam tests not just the ratios, but your ability to select the correct table, apply the correct bracket, and round correctly.

---

## Critical Warning: Two Tables Are in Circulation

Before memorizing any ratios, understand this fundamental fact: two sets of parking standards appear in ALE question banks and reviewers.

| Version | Source | When It Appears on the ALE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Old Summary Table"** | Pre-2004 simplified parking table, widely reproduced in older review materials and confirmed in some board exam question banks | Questions that cite "PD 1096" generically without specifying a table version, or that use the "low-income single-detached" framing |
| **"2004 Revised IRR Table VII.4"** | Current legally-binding standard (PRBoA-annotated, effective May 2005); amends and expands the original 1977 table | Questions referencing "highly urbanized areas," specific GFA brackets, jeepney slots, or Table VII.4 directly |

> **Reason for Two Versions:** The 2004 Revised IRR significantly updated parking requirements from the original 1977 PD 1096 — introducing urbanization tiers, vehicle-type-specific slots, and GFA-bracket differentiation. Many reviewers and exam question banks were written before or without reflecting the 2004 revision. The board exam draws from both pools. This module presents both versions side by side.

**Strategy:** Match your answer to how the question is framed. If it says "highly urbanized city" or cites Table VII.4, use the 2004 IRR. If it says "as per PD 1096" and uses "low-income single-detached" language, the Old Table is likely expected.

## Part 1: Parking Slot Dimensions

The ALE frequently tests exact physical dimensions of parking slots. These must be memorized with precision.

### Standard Slot Dimensions (Rule VII, Sec. 707.4, 2004 Revised IRR)

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — PARKING SLOT DIMENSION TABLE**

| Vehicle Type | Minimum Width | Minimum Length | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Car — Perpendicular or Diagonal** | **2.50 m** | **5.00 m** | Current standard per PRBoA-annotated 2004 Revised IRR; most frequently tested |
| **Car — Parallel** | **2.15 m** | **6.00 m** | Narrower but longer; extra length required for parallel entry/exit maneuver |
| **Jeepney / Shuttle** | **3.00 m** | **9.00 m** | Standard public utility jeepney or shuttle vehicle |
| **Standard Bus or Truck** | **3.60 m** | **12.00 m** | Tourist bus slots for hotels also use this dimension |
| **Articulated Truck** | **3.00 m** | **18.00 m** | Container van trailer with prime mover; note: some IRR editions cite 3.60 m width — the 3.00 m x 18.00 m figure reflects the specific container van trailer configuration in the PRBoA-annotated IRR |
| **PWD / Accessible Parking** | **3.70 m** | **5.00 m** | Per BP 344 (Accessibility Law); extra width accommodates wheelchair side-transfer zone |

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — TRAP #1: The Dimension Confusion**

| | Wrong | Right |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Perpendicular car slot | 2.00 m x 5.00 m or 2.40 m x 5.00 m | **2.50 m x 5.00 m** (Sec. 707.4, 2004 Revised IRR) |
| Parallel car slot | 2.50 m x 5.00 m | **2.15 m x 6.00 m** (narrower, longer) |
| Bus / Truck slot | 3.00 m x 12.00 m | **3.60 m x 12.00 m** |
| PWD slot | 2.50 m x 5.00 m | **3.70 m x 5.00 m** (extra 1.20 m for wheelchair) |

> **Reason for the Law:** The 2.50 m perpendicular width accommodates modern vehicle widths plus a minimum door-opening clearance. The parallel slot is longer (6.00 m vs. 5.00 m) because the arc of parallel entry requires additional fore-and-aft buffer. The PWD slot's extra 1.20 m width is a functional requirement for the wheelchair side-transfer zone — without it, a person transferring from a wheelchair to a vehicle cannot safely do so between parked cars.

---

**Additional Parking Location Rules (Sec. 707.4, 2004 Revised IRR):**

- All parking slots must be located **outside the Road Right-of-Way (RROW)**. The street is public domain — no private parking on it.
- Accessible (PWD) parking must be within **60 meters** of the building entrance it serves, measured along an accessible path (BP 344).

---

> **Mnemonic — Slot Dimensions: "2.5-5 / 2.15-6 / 3-9 / 3.6-12"**
> - Car perpendicular: **2.5 x 5** ("two-and-a-half by five")
> - Car parallel: **2.15 x 6** ("two-fifteen by six")
> - Jeepney: **3 x 9** ("three by nine")
> - Bus/Truck: **3.6 x 12** ("three-six by twelve")
> - PWD: **3.7 x 5** ("three-seven by five")

## Part 2: Residential Parking Ratios

### Old Summary Table (Frequently Tested As-Is)

Source: Pre-2004 simplified parking table; confirmed in board exam question banks.

| Occupancy Type | Condition | Minimum Required Parking |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Low-Income Single Detached** | Individual lots not more than 100 sq.m. | **1 pooled slot per 10 units** |
| **Multi-Family Dwelling** | Average living floor area up to 50 sq.m. | **1 slot per 8 units** |
| **Multi-Family Dwelling** | Average living floor area above 50 to 100 sq.m. | **1 slot per 4 units** |
| **Multi-Family Dwelling** | Average living floor area more than 100 sq.m. | **1 slot per 1 unit** |

---

### 2004 Revised IRR — Table VII.4 (Detailed Current Standard)

#### Group A, Division A-1: Single-Family and Multi-Family in Residential Subdivisions

| Lot Size and/or GFA | Minimum Required Parking |
| :--- | :--- |
| Lot: 32–72 sq.m. and/or GFA: 18–22 sq.m. | 1 pooled slot per **6 lots** |
| Lot: 50–96 sq.m. and/or GFA: 30–42 sq.m. | 1 pooled slot per **4 lots** |
| Lot: 100–120 sq.m. and/or GFA: 30–42 sq.m. | 1 slot per **each lot** |
| Lot: more than 120 sq.m. and/or GFA: more than 42 sq.m. | 1 slot per **each lot** |

#### Group A, Division A-2: Multi-Family in R-5 Residential Condominiums

> **As amended by DPWH Department Order No. 059, Series of 2019** (amends Table VII.4-1, Group A 1.2, Division A-2)

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — R-5 CONDOMINIUM PARKING TABLE (AMENDED)**

| GFA per Unit | Minimum Required Parking | "Or Fraction Thereof"? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **18.00 to 22.00 sq.m.** | 1 pooled slot per **8 units** | Yes — round up |
| **Up to 50.00 sq.m.** | 1 pooled slot per **6 units** | Yes — round up |
| **Above 50.00 to 100.00 sq.m.** | 1 pooled slot per **4 units** | Yes — round up |
| **More than 100.00 sq.m.** | **1 slot per unit** | 1:1 — no fraction |

> **"Or fraction thereof" explained:** If you have 25 units at 1 slot per 6 units, that is 25 / 6 = 4.17. Round UP to **5 slots required**. Any remainder above a whole number triggers an additional slot. Never round down.

> **Reason for the Law:** Studio apartments (18–22 sq.m.) are purchased primarily by young professionals or as investment units — statistically low car-ownership cohorts. Requiring 1:1 parking for every studio would price affordable housing out of the market. The graduated scale (8→6→4→1) reflects the empirical relationship between unit size and vehicle ownership.

---

> **Mnemonic — R-5 Condominium Ratios: "8-6-4-1"**
> - 18–22 sq.m. → 1 per **8** units (smallest units, fewest cars)
> - Up to 50 sq.m. → 1 per **6** units
> - 50–100 sq.m. → 1 per **4** units
> - Over 100 sq.m. → 1 per **1** unit (biggest units, 1:1)
> Pattern: Bigger unit = more cars = lower denominator = more parking.

---

#### Group A: Townhouses (R-4)

| GFA per Unit | Minimum Required Parking |
| :--- | :--- |

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — TRAP #2: Condominium vs. Subdivision vs. Townhouse**

## Part 3: Commercial, Institutional & Assembly Parking Ratios

### Side-by-Side Comparison: Old Table vs. 2004 Revised IRR Table VII.4

| Occupancy Type | Old Summary Table | 2004 Revised IRR (Table VII.4) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hotels — Highly Urbanized Cities** | 1 slot per **10 rooms** | 1 slot per **3 rooms** + **2 tourist bus slots** per hotel |
| **Hotels — All Other Areas** | 1 slot per **10 rooms** | 1 slot per **7 rooms** + **2 tourist bus slots** per hotel |
| **Residential Hotels / Apartels** | 1 slot per **5 units** | 1 slot per **5 units** + 1 bus slot per 60 rooms/units |
| **Motels** | 1 slot per **unit** | 1 slot per **unit** |
| **Pension / Boarding / Lodging Houses** | 1 slot per **20 beds** | 1 slot per **20 beds** |
| **Theaters / Cinemas / Auditoria** (Group H) | 1 slot per **50 sq.m.** of spectator area | 1 car slot + 1 jeepney/shuttle slot per **50 sq.m.** of spectator area; + 1 bus slot per **200 spectators** |
| **Markets** | 1 slot per **150 sq.m.** of market floor area | Varies by market classification |
| **Shopping Centers** | 1 slot per **100 sq.m.** of shopping floor area | 1 slot per **100 sq.m.** |
| **Restaurants / Fast Food / Bars** | 1 slot per **30 sq.m.** of customer area | 1 slot per **30 sq.m.** of customer area |
| **Nightclubs / Theater-Restaurants** | 1 slot per **20 sq.m.** of customer area | 1 slot per **20 sq.m.** of customer area |
| **Office Buildings** | 1 slot per **125 sq.m.** of GFA | 1 slot per **125 sq.m.** of GFA |
| **Elementary / Secondary / Vocational Schools** | 1 slot per **10 classrooms** | 1 slot per **10 classrooms** |
| **Colleges / Universities** | 1 slot per **5 classrooms** | 1 slot per **5 classrooms** |
| **Private Hospitals** | — | 1 slot per **12 beds** |
| **Public Hospitals** | — | 1 slot per **25 beds** |

**Reason for the Law:** Hotels in highly urbanized cities attract dramatically more vehicular traffic than comparable hotels in smaller towns — both from guests arriving by car and from restaurants, events, and banquet functions that draw non-guests. The 2004 IRR's urbanization-tier distinction for hotels reflects this empirical difference. The 2-tourist-bus-slot flat requirement ensures that tour operators always have a designated off-street zone regardless of hotel size.

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — TRAP #3: The Hotel Ratio Dual Answer**

| Question Framing | Expected Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| "As per PD 1096" — no further specification, "1 per 10 rooms" is a choice | **1 slot per 10 rooms** (Old Summary Table) |
| "In a highly urbanized city" or cites Table VII.4 explicitly | **1 slot per 3 rooms + 2 tourist bus slots** (2004 Revised IRR) |
| "In areas other than highly urbanized" per 2004 IRR | **1 slot per 7 rooms + 2 tourist bus slots** |

> **Mnemonic — Hotel Ratios: "3-7-10"**
> - Highly urbanized: 1 per **3** rooms
> - All other areas: 1 per **7** rooms
> - Old summary table: 1 per **10** rooms
> "**3**-star city / **7** provinces / **10** for the old exam"

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — TRAP #4: The Missing Jeepney and Bus Slots**

| | Wrong | Right |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Parking for a 500-seat cinema (Group H) | Count only car slots | **1 car + 1 jeepney/shuttle** per 50 sq.m. spectator area + **1 bus per 200 spectators** |
| Parking for a hotel in Manila | Car slots only, no bus consideration | Car slots per room ratio + **2 tourist bus slots** (flat requirement per hotel) |
| Bus slot dimensions | Same as car slot | **3.60 m x 12.00 m** — the standard bus/truck slot |

**Reason for the Law:** Assembly occupancies (theaters, sports facilities) generate large, simultaneous arrivals and departures of people using public transport. Jeepney and bus slots separate public utility vehicles from private cars, preventing traffic gridlock at building drop-off zones. Philippine urban mobility patterns — where a large percentage of patrons arrive by jeepney or bus — make this a functionally critical requirement.

## Part 4: PWD / Accessible Parking (BP 344 — Accessibility Law)

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — ACCESSIBLE PARKING TABLE**

| Condition | Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **50 to 150 total parking slots** in the building | Provide **1 accessible parking slot** |
| **Every additional 100 slots** beyond 150 | **1 additional accessible slot** |
| **Wheelchair transfer area** | 1 transfer area per 4 parking spaces designated for PWD use |
| **Maximum distance** from accessible parking to the entrance served | **60 meters**, measured along an accessible path |
| **Slot dimensions** | **3.70 m x 5.00 m** per slot |

> **Reason for the Law:** The 3.70 m width provides the extra 1.20 m lateral clearance needed for a wheelchair user to deploy and transfer. The 60 m maximum distance ensures that accessible slots are genuinely proximate — not technically compliant but functionally inaccessible. The ratio (1 for 50–150 total, +1 per additional 100) scales accessible provision proportionally with total parking supply.

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — TRAP #5: PWD Slot Placement**

| | Wrong | Right |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Accessible slots can be anywhere in the lot | Placed at whatever is convenient | Must be within **60 m** of the entrance served, along an accessible path (BP 344) |
| PWD slot dimensions | 2.50 m x 5.00 m (standard car slot) | **3.70 m x 5.00 m** — 1.20 m wider than standard |
| When to start providing PWD slots | When you have at least 1 parking slot | Starting at **50 total slots** |

## Part 5: Calculation Rules — The "Fraction Thereof" Rule

---

**PATTERN INTERRUPT — TRAP #6: Always Round UP**

| | Wrong | Right |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 25 units at 1:6 ratio = 4.17 slots | Round down to 4 slots | Round **up** to **5 slots** |
| 17 units at 1:8 ratio = 2.125 slots | Round down to 2 slots | Round **up** to **3 slots** |
| When to round down | When the fraction is 0.5 or below | **Never** — "or fraction thereof" means always round up |

> **Reason for the Law:** "Or fraction thereof" is a deliberate legislative choice to prevent under-provision through rounding. A fraction of a slot represents a real car that has no place to park. The law mandates that the developer absorb the cost of the additional slot rather than leaving one car without parking.

## Active Recall Challenges

### Question 1

An architect is designing an R-5 condominium with 25 residential units, each with a GFA of 45 sq.m. What is the minimum number of off-street parking slots required for the residential units under the 2004 Revised IRR (as amended by DPWH DO 059 s. 2019)?

**(A)** 25 slots
**(B)** 6 slots
**(C)** 5 slots
**(D)** 4 slots

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: C — 5 slots**

**Analysis:**

- Step 1: 45 sq.m. falls in the **"Up to 50.00 sq.m."** bracket under Division A-2 (R-5 Condominium), amended by DPWH DO 059 s. 2019.
- Step 2: Ratio = 1 pooled slot per **6 units** (or fraction thereof).
- Step 3: 25 units ÷ 6 = **4.167** → round UP to **5 slots**.
- Option D (4 slots) is the classic rounding-down error — the "fraction thereof" rule requires rounding up.
- Option B (6 slots) would be the result of applying the 1:4 ratio (50–100 sq.m. bracket) — wrong bracket.

</details>

### Question 2

A hotel in a highly urbanized city has 25 guest rooms. What is the minimum required parking under PD 1096 as specified in the 2004 Revised IRR?

**(A)** 3 car slots only
**(B)** 9 car slots + 2 bus slots
**(C)** 3 car slots + 2 bus slots
**(D)** 2 car slots + 2 bus slots

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B — 9 car slots + 2 bus slots**

**Analysis:**

- The 2004 Revised IRR specifies: 1 car slot per **3 rooms** (highly urbanized city) + **2 tourist bus slots** per hotel (flat requirement).
- Car slots: 25 ÷ 3 = 8.33 → round UP to **9 car slots** ("or fraction thereof").
- Bus slots: **2 tourist bus slots** — this is a flat per-hotel requirement regardless of room count.
- Option C (3 car slots) would represent 25 ÷ 3 rounded down — wrong direction.
- Option A omits the mandatory bus slots entirely.

</details>

---

### Question 3

Under the Old Summary Table of PD 1096, a low-income single-detached residential development has 47 units on individual lots of 90 sq.m. each. What is the minimum required pooled parking?

**(A)** 4 slots
**(B)** 5 slots
**(C)** 6 slots
**(D)** 8 slots

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B — 5 slots**

**Analysis:**

- The Old Summary Table specifies: 1 pooled slot per **10 units** for low-income single detached dwellings on individual lots not more than 100 sq.m.
- 47 ÷ 10 = **4.7** → round UP to **5 slots** ("or fraction thereof").
- Option A (4 slots) is the rounding-down error.
- The 2004 Revised IRR uses different brackets (lots 50–96 sq.m. = 1 per 4 lots), which would yield a different answer. This question uses the Old Table framing, so use the Old Table.

</details>

### Question 4

What are the minimum dimensions of a perpendicular automobile parking slot under the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096?

**(A)** 2.00 m x 5.00 m
**(B)** 2.15 m x 6.00 m
**(C)** 2.40 m x 5.00 m
**(D)** 2.50 m x 5.00 m

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: D — 2.50 m x 5.00 m**

**Analysis:**

- Per Rule VII, Sec. 707.4 of the PRBoA-annotated 2004 Revised IRR: the minimum size for a perpendicular or diagonal automobile parking slot is **2.50 m x 5.00 m**.
- Option A (2.00 x 5.00) appears in older pre-2004 references — not the current standard.
- Option B (2.15 x 6.00) is the **parallel** parking slot dimension — a deliberate distractor.
- Option C (2.40 x 5.00) appears in some unofficial reviewers — not the IRR figure.

</details>

---

### Question 5

A building has 180 total off-street parking slots. How many accessible (PWD) parking slots are required under BP 344?

**(A)** 1 slot
**(B)** 2 slots
**(C)** 3 slots
**(D)** 4 slots

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B — 2 slots**

**Analysis:**

BP 344 specifies:
- 1 accessible slot for **50 to 150 total slots**.
- 1 additional accessible slot for **every 100 slots** (or fraction thereof) beyond 150.

For 180 total slots:
- Base: 1 slot (covers 50–150)
- Beyond 150: 180 – 150 = 30 additional slots → 30 ÷ 100 = 0.30 → round UP = 1 additional slot
- Total: 1 + 1 = **2 accessible slots**

</details>

## Quick-Fire Mnemonics

**Slot Dimensions — "2.5-5 / 2.15-6 / 3-9 / 3.6-12 / 3.7-5"**

- Car perpendicular: **2.5 x 5**
- Car parallel: **2.15 x 6** (wider arc needs longer space)
- Jeepney: **3 x 9**
- Bus/Truck: **3.6 x 12**
- PWD: **3.7 x 5** (extra 1.20 m for wheelchair)

**R-5 Condominium Parking — "8-6-4-1"**

- 18–22 sq.m. → 1 per 8 units
- Up to 50 sq.m. → 1 per 6 units
- 50–100 sq.m. → 1 per 4 units
- Over 100 sq.m. → 1 per 1 unit
- Bigger unit = more cars = smaller denominator = more parking slots

**Hotel Ratios — "3-7-10"**

- Highly urbanized: 1 per **3** rooms
- All other areas: 1 per **7** rooms
- Old table: 1 per **10** rooms
- Always add **2 tourist bus slots** per hotel (2004 Revised IRR)

**PWD Provision — "1 at 50, +1 per 100"**

- First accessible slot triggers at **50 total slots**
- Covers up to 150 total slots
- Add 1 more accessible slot for every additional 100 (or fraction thereof) beyond 150
- Max distance: **60 meters** from slot to entrance, along accessible path

**The "Fraction Thereof" Rule — Always Round UP**

- Any decimal result → next whole number
- Never round down, no matter how small the fraction
