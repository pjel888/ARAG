# Architecture Board Exam (ALE) Mastery: Minimum Parking Requirements

**Module Focus: Off-Street Parking Standards & Occupancy Ratios (NBCP)**

---

## The "Why It Matters" Scenario (Schema Activation)

Imagine you are designing a medium-rise condominium in Quezon City. The developer wants to maximize saleable units, but you know that every unit you add changes the parking formula. If you miscalculate the required parking slots, the building will not get a permit, and redesigning the basement at the last minute will cost millions.

In the ALE, parking questions are guaranteed. The exam tests your ability to instantly recall specific ratios based on the **Occupancy Group** and the **Gross Floor Area (GFA)** of the units.

---

## CRITICAL WARNING: Two Tables in Circulation

> Before studying the ratios, you must understand this: There are **two versions** of parking standards that appear in ALE reviewers and board exam questions.

| Version                            | Source                                                                                                | Status                                  |
| :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------- |
| **"Old Summary Table"**            | Pre-2004 simplified parking table widely reproduced in review materials and some board exam questions | Still tested on the ALE                 |
| **"2004 Revised IRR Table VII.4"** | The current, legally-binding standard (PRBoA-annotated, effective May 2005)                           | Also tested on the ALE with more detail |

The board exam can draw from **either version** depending on how the question is framed. This module presents **both**, clearly labeled, so you never get caught off-guard. When a question says "as per PD 1096" without specifying "2004 Revised IRR," it often expects the old summary table answers. When a question is more detailed (referencing lot sizes, "highly urbanized areas," or specific GFA brackets), it expects the 2004 Revised IRR answer.

**Reason for the Two Versions:** The 2004 Revised IRR significantly expanded and updated the parking requirements from the original 1977 PD 1096. However, many review materials and some exam question banks were written before or without updating to the 2004 revision. Both sets of figures appear in the exam pool.

## Parking Slot Dimensions (Physical Footprint)

The ALE frequently tests these exact dimensions. These are consistent across both versions.

### 2004 Revised IRR Dimensions (Sec. 707.4, Rule VII)

| Vehicle Type                       | Minimum Dimensions   | Notes                                                                 |
| :--------------------------------- | :------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Car (Perpendicular / Diagonal)** | **2.50 m x 5.00 m**  | This is the current standard per the PRBoA-annotated 2004 Revised IRR |
| **Car (Parallel)**                 | **2.15 m x 6.00 m**  | Narrower but longer                                                   |
| **Jeepney / Shuttle**              | **3.00 m x 9.00 m**  | —                                                                     |
| **Standard Truck or Bus**          | **3.60 m x 12.00 m** | —                                                                     |
| **Articulated Truck**              | **3.00 m x 18.00 m** | Accommodates a 12.00 m container van plus long/hooded prime mover     |
| **PWD / Accessible Parking**       | **3.70 m x 5.00 m**  | Per BP 344 (Accessibility Law)                                        |

> **Exam Trap Alert:** Some older reviewers cite **2.40 m x 5.00 m** or even **2.00 m x 5.00 m** for perpendicular parking. The PRBoA-annotated 2004 Revised IRR states **2.50 m x 5.00 m**. When a board exam question offers both 2.0 x 5.0 and 2.5 x 5.0 as choices (as confirmed in actual exam question banks), the correct answer is **2.50 m x 5.00 m**.

**Reason for the Law:** Standardized slot dimensions ensure that every car can park, open its doors, and maneuver safely. The 2.50 m width accommodates modern vehicle widths plus door-opening clearance. The parallel slot is longer (6.00 m) because parallel maneuvering requires extra length for entry/exit.

All parking slots must be located **outside the Road Right-of-Way (RROW)** — the street is public domain and cannot be used for private parking.

## The Hard Data Cheat Sheet: Residential Parking

### Old Summary Table (Frequently Tested As-Is)

> Source: Pre-2004 simplified parking table. Appears in many ALE reviewers and confirmed in board exam question banks.

| Occupancy Type                 | Condition                                          | Required Minimum Parking       |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------- |
| **Low-Income Single Detached** | Individual lots **not more than 100 sq.m**         | **1 pooled slot per 10 units** |
| **Multi-Family Dwelling**      | Average living floor area **up to 50 sq.m**        | **1 slot per 8 units**         |
| **Multi-Family Dwelling**      | Average living floor area **above 50 to 100 sq.m** | **1 slot per 4 units**         |
| **Multi-Family Dwelling**      | Average living floor area **more than 100 sq.m**   | **1 slot per 1 unit**          |

### 2004 Revised IRR Table VII.4 (Detailed / Current Standard)

#### Group A, Division A-1: Single-Family & Multi-Family in Residential Subdivisions

| Lot Size / GFA                                        | Required Minimum Parking     |
| :---------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------- |
| Lot: 32-72 sq.m and/or GFA: 18-22 sq.m                | 1 pooled slot per **6 lots** |
| Lot: 50-96 sq.m and/or GFA: 30-42 sq.m                | 1 pooled slot per **4 lots** |
| Lot: 100-120 sq.m and/or GFA: 30-42 sq.m              | 1 slot per **each lot**      |
| Lot: more than 120 sq.m and/or GFA: more than 42 sq.m | 1 slot per **each lot**      |

#### Group A, Division A-2: Multi-Family in R-5 Residential Condominiums (as amended by DPWH DO 059 s. 2019)

| GFA per Unit                   | Required Minimum Parking                            |
| :----------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| **18.00 to 22.00 sq.m**        | 1 pooled slot per **8 units** (or fraction thereof) |
| **Up to 50.00 sq.m**           | 1 pooled slot per **6 units** (or fraction thereof) |
| **Above 50.00 to 100.00 sq.m** | 1 pooled slot per **4 units**                       |
| **More than 100.00 sq.m**      | **1 slot per unit**                                 |

> **"Or fraction thereof"** means: if you have more than 8 units but less than 16, you need 2 slots (not 1). The fraction always rounds UP.

#### Group A: Townhouses (R-4)

| GFA per Unit | Required Minimum Parking                            |
| :----------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| 50 sq.m      | 1 pooled slot per **2 units** (or fraction thereof) |

**Reason for the Law:** Smaller, more affordable units generate less car ownership statistically. Requiring 1:1 parking for studio apartments would make housing unaffordable. The graduated scale balances livability with economic reality.

### Additional Ratios (2004 Revised IRR Only)

| Occupancy Type                                               | Required Minimum Parking                                                                        |
| :----------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stadia / Sports Complexes / Convention Centers** (Group I) | 1 car slot + 1 jeepney/shuttle slot per 50 sq.m spectator area; + 1 bus slot per 200 spectators |
| **Industrial Buildings** (Group F)                           | 1 car slot per 1,000 sq.m GFA; + 1 bus slot per 100 workers                                     |
| **Clubhouses / Beach Houses**                                | 1 slot per **100 sq.m** of GFA                                                                  |
| **Places of Worship / Funeral Parlors**                      | 1 slot per **50 sq.m** of congregation area                                                     |
| **Bowling Alleys**                                           | 1 slot per **4 alleys**                                                                         |
| **Amusement Centers**                                        | 1 slot per **50 sq.m** of GFA                                                                   |

**Reason for the Law:** High-traffic commercial occupancies like hotels and shopping centers in urbanized areas generate far more vehicular traffic than the same building type in rural areas. The 2004 Revised IRR introduced the "highly urbanized" distinction for hotels to account for this reality.

## PWD / Accessible Parking (BP 344)

| Condition                         | Requirement                                                                                             |
| :-------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **50 to 150 total parking slots** | Provide **1 accessible parking slot**                                                                   |
| **Every 100 slots thereafter**    | **1 additional accessible slot**                                                                        |
| **Wheelchair transfer area**      | 1 for every **4 parking spaces** in buildings for PWD use                                               |
| **Maximum distance**              | Accessible parking must be within **60 meters** of the facility served (measured along accessible path) |

**Reason for the Law:** BP 344 (Accessibility Law) ensures that persons with disabilities have proximate, adequately-sized parking with enough clearance for wheelchair deployment. The 3.70 m width provides the extra 1.20 m needed for a side-transfer zone.

## High-Yield Exam Traps (Von Restorff Effect)

---

**TRAP #1: The Dimension Confusion**

- **Mistake:** Memorizing 2.40 m x 5.00 m or 2.00 m x 5.00 m for perpendicular parking.
- **Reality:** The 2004 Revised IRR (PRBoA-annotated) specifies **2.50 m x 5.00 m**. When the exam gives you both as options, choose 2.50 m. The 2.15 m x 6.00 m figure is for **parallel** parking only.

---

**TRAP #2: The Hotel Ratio Dual Answer**

- **Mistake:** Answering "1 slot per 3 rooms" when the question uses old-table framing, or answering "1 per 10 rooms" when the question specifies "highly urbanized."
- **Reality:** Read the question carefully. If it says "as per PD 1096" generically and offers "10 rooms" as a choice, that is likely the expected answer. If it specifies "highly urbanized areas" or references Table VII.4, the answer is **1 per 3 rooms**.

---

**TRAP #3: The Multi-Family Escalator**

- **Mistake:** Thinking all residential units require 1 slot per unit.
- **Reality:** Only units exceeding **100 sq.m** GFA require a 1:1 ratio. Smaller units are pooled (1:4, 1:6, or 1:8).

---

**TRAP #4: The "Fraction Thereof" Rule**

- **Mistake:** Calculating 4.2 slots and rounding down to 4.
- **Reality:** "Or a fraction thereof" means any result above a whole number rounds **UP**. 4.2 slots = **5 slots required**.

---

**TRAP #5: The Condominium vs. Subdivision Confusion**

- **Mistake:** Using the R-5 condominium ratios for a residential subdivision or vice versa.
- **Reality:** The 2004 Revised IRR has different tables for Division A-1 (subdivisions, which reference lot size AND GFA) and Division A-2 (condominiums, which reference only GFA per unit). A question about a "townhouse" uses the R-4 table (1:2 for 50 sq.m units).

---

**TRAP #6: Missing the Jeepney/Shuttle Slot**

- **Mistake:** Only counting car slots for theaters and assembly buildings.
- **Reality:** Under the 2004 Revised IRR, Group H and Group I occupancies require **BOTH** 1 car slot AND 1 jeepney/shuttle slot per 50 sq.m of spectator area, plus 1 bus slot per 200 spectators. A question may ask specifically about the jeepney component.

---

**TRAP #7: The "Tourist Bus" Requirement**

- **Mistake:** Forgetting that hotels have a mandatory tourist bus parking provision.
- **Reality:** Under the 2004 Revised IRR, every hotel must provide **2 tourist bus parking slots** in addition to the per-room car parking ratio. Bus slot = 3.60 m x 12.00 m minimum.

## Active Recall Challenges (Test Your Retention)

### Question 1

An architect is designing a multi-family condominium (R-5) with 24 residential units. Each unit has a Gross Floor Area of 45 sq.m. What is the minimum number of off-street parking slots required for the residential units?

**(A)** 24 slots
**(B)** 6 slots
**(C)** 4 slots
**(D)** 3 slots

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: C) 4 slots**

**Analysis:**

- Step 1: Identify the unit size bracket. 45 sq.m falls into **"Up to 50.00 sq.m"** under the R-5 condominium table.
- Step 2: Ratio = 1 pooled slot per 6 units.
- Step 3: 24 units / 6 = **4 slots exactly** (no fraction to round up).
- Option B (6 slots) is wrong — that would be 24/4, which is the ratio for the 50-100 sq.m bracket.

</details>

---

### Question 2

As per PD 1096, what is the minimum required parking for a hotel in a highly urbanized city with 25 guest rooms?

**(A)** 3 car slots + 2 bus slots
**(B)** 9 car slots + 2 bus slots
**(C)** 3 car slots only
**(D)** 2 car slots + 2 bus slots

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B) 9 car slots + 2 bus slots**

**Analysis:**

- The 2004 Revised IRR specifies: 1 car slot per 3 rooms (highly urbanized) + 2 tourist bus slots per hotel.
- Car slots: 25 rooms / 3 = 8.33 → round up to **9 car slots** (fraction-thereof rule).
- Bus slots: **2 tourist bus slots** (flat requirement per hotel).
- Total: 9 car slots + 2 bus slots.
- If the question had simply said "as per PD 1096" without specifying "highly urbanized," and offered "1 slot per 10 rooms" as a choice, that could be the expected answer under the old summary table.

</details>

---

### Question 3

For low-income single-detached living units with individual lots not more than 100 sq.m, what is the minimum pooled parking provision?

**(A)** 1 slot per 5 units
**(B)** 1 slot per 10 units
**(C)** 1 slot per 6 units
**(D)** 1 slot per 4 units

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B) 1 slot per 10 units**

**Analysis:**

- This question uses the exact framing of the old summary table: "low-income single-detached living units with individual lots not more than 100 sq.m."
- Under that table, the answer is **1 pooled slot per 10 units**.
- Note: The 2004 Revised IRR uses different brackets (lots 32-72 sq.m = 1 per 6 lots; lots 50-96 sq.m = 1 per 4 lots). If the question referenced those specific lot sizes, you would use the 2004 IRR ratios instead. Always match your answer to the framing of the question.

</details>

---

### Question 4

What are the minimum dimensions of a perpendicular automobile parking slot as per PD 1096?

**(A)** 2.00 m x 5.00 m
**(B)** 2.50 m x 5.00 m
**(C)** 2.15 m x 6.00 m
**(D)** 3.00 m x 5.00 m

<details>
<summary><b>Reveal Answer</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B) 2.50 m x 5.00 m**

**Analysis:**

- Per the PRBoA-annotated 2004 Revised IRR: "The size of an average automobile (car) parking slot must be computed at **2.50 meters by 5.00 meters** for perpendicular or diagonal parking."
- Option A (2.00 x 5.00) appears in some older references but is not the current standard.
- Option C (2.15 x 6.00) is the **parallel** parking dimension — a classic distractor.

</details>

## Quick-Fire Mnemonics

**Slot Dimensions — "2.5-5, 2.15-6, 3-9, 3.6-12"**

- Car perpendicular: **2.5** wide x **5** long ("two-and-a-half by five")
- Car parallel: **2.15** wide x **6** long ("two-fifteen by six")
- Jeepney: **3** wide x **9** long ("three by nine")
- Bus/Truck: **3.6** wide x **12** long ("three-six by twelve")

**Multi-Family Residential (R-5 Condominium) — "8-6-4-1"**

- 18-22 sq.m = 1 per **8** units
- Up to 50 sq.m = 1 per **6** units
- 50-100 sq.m = 1 per **4** units
- Over 100 sq.m = 1 per **1** unit
- Pattern: The ratios decrease (8→6→4→1) as units get bigger. Think: "Bigger unit, more cars."

**Hotel Ratios — "3-7-10"**

- Highly urbanized: 1 per **3** rooms
- All other areas: 1 per **7** rooms
- Old summary table: 1 per **10** rooms
- Mnemonic: "**3**-star city, **7** provinces, **10** for the old test"
