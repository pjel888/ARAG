# 🚪 Architecture Board Exam (ALE) Mastery: Egress, Corridors, & Occupant Loads

_Module Focus: PD 1096 Rule XII - The Geometry of Escape_

---

## 🧠 The "Why It Matters" Scenario (Schema Activation)

Imagine you are designing a massive call center (Group B) occupying an entire 2,000 sq.m floor plate. When the fire alarm triggers, 400 panicked employees will push their chairs back and run for the stairs.

If your corridor is too narrow, people will be crushed. If the doors swing the wrong way, the crowd will trap themselves against the exit. If you only provided one staircase because you thought "it's just one open room," you have created a death trap.

The board exam tests Rule XII rigorously because **Egress is the ultimate life-safety feature of architecture**. You must memorize the thresholds where a building "levels up" its escape requirements.

---

## 🏗️ Core Concepts (The "Flow" Framework)

To master Egress, think of the building as a plumbing system, but instead of water, you are moving people.

### 1. The Source: Occupant Load

- **Definition:** The maximum number of people legally allowed in a space. You don't guess this number; you calculate it by dividing the floor area by the "Unit Area" assigned to that specific use (Table XII.1).
- **Mental Hook:** _Bigger crowds require bigger pipes (corridors) and more valves (doors)._

### 2. The Valves: Exits & Doors

- **Definition:** The specific portals people pass through to escape.
- **The "Swing" Rule:** If a room holds **50 or more people**, the exit doors MUST swing _outward_ (in the direction of exit travel).

### 3. The Pipes: Corridors & Balconies

- **Definition:** The continuous, unobstructed path leading to the exit stair or public way.
- **The Bottleneck Rule:** A corridor can never be narrower than the doors leading into it, and its width must accommodate the total occupant load.

## 📊 The Hard Data Cheat Sheet (Optimized for Rapid Recall)

_Data strictly based on the Revised IRR of PD 1096 (Rule XII). Memorize these brackets._

### 1. Number of Exits Required (The Floor Brackets)

_How many exits must a floor have? It depends entirely on the Occupant Load._

| Occupant Load per Floor | Minimum Number of Exits | Exam Hook                                            |
| :---------------------- | :---------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| **1 to 9**              | **1 Exit**              | (Only allowed on the 1st storey or tiny mezzanines). |
| **10 to 499**           | **2 Exits**             | The standard for almost all regular floors.          |
| **500 to 999**          | **3 Exits**             | The crowd is getting dangerous.                      |
| **1,000 or more**       | **4 Exits**             | Stadium-level crowds.                                |

_🚨 Critical Note: ANY floor above the first storey that has an occupant load of **more than 10** must have at least TWO exits. Never design a 2nd floor with only one stair if it holds 11 people!_

### 2. Corridor Minimum Widths

_The original draft had hallucinations here. Here are the correct NBCP minimums._

| Corridor Usage                                        | Minimum Clear Width |
| :---------------------------------------------------- | :------------------ |
| **Standard Minimum** (Serving >= 10 occupants)        | **1.10 meters**     |
| **Educational** (Group C / Schools)                   | **1.80 meters**     |
| **Institutional** (Group D / Hospitals - Bed Traffic) | **2.40 meters**     |

### 3. Door Dimensions

- **Maximum Leaf Width:** **1.20 meters**. (If a door is wider than 1.20m, it must be split into double doors. A massive, heavy single door is too hard for a person to push open in a panic).
- **Minimum Clear Width:** **900 mm** (for standard egress).
- **Minimum Height:** **2.00 meters**.

## ⚠️ High-Yield Exam Traps (Von Restorff Effect)

Your brain wants to simplify the rules. Examiners know this and design traps around the exceptions.

- 🚨 **TRAP #1: The "1/2 Diagonal" Separation Trap.**
  - _Mistake:_ Placing two required exits right next to each other.
  - _Reality Check:_ If two exits are required, they must be placed a distance apart equal to **not less than 1/2 of the length of the maximum overall diagonal dimension** of the building or area to be served. (If they are too close, a single fire blocks both).
- 🚨 **TRAP #2: The Dead End Corridor.**
  - _Mistake:_ Designing a long hallway with an exit only at one end.
  - _Reality Check:_ The code strictly limits "dead ends" (where a person has no choice but to turn back if fire blocks the path) to a maximum of **6.00 meters**.
- 🚨 **TRAP #3: The Change in Elevation (Steps vs. Ramps).**
  - _Mistake:_ Putting a single 200mm step in a hallway to handle a floor level change.
  - _Reality Check:_ Changes in elevation of **less than 300 mm** along an exit corridor must be made by a **RAMP**, not a step. (Exception: Group A Residential can use steps). People trip on small steps in a panic.

## 🔄 Active Recall Challenge (Test Your Retention)

**Question:** You are calculating the egress requirements for a 2nd-floor corporate office. The total floor area is 600 sq.m. According to Table XII.1, the Unit Area for an office is 9.30 sq.m per occupant. Based on PD 1096, what is the minimum number of exits required for this floor, and which way must the doors swing?

A) 1 Exit; doors can swing inward.
B) 2 Exits; doors must swing outward (direction of travel).
C) 3 Exits; doors must swing outward.
D) 2 Exits; doors can swing either way.

<details>
  <summary><b>👉 Click here to reveal the answer and explanation</b></summary>
  <br>
  <b>Correct Answer: B</b><br><br>
  <b>Why?</b> <br>
  Step 1: Calculate Occupant Load. 600 sq.m ÷ 9.30 sq.m/occupant = <b>64.5</b> (Round up to 65 occupants).<br>
  Step 2: Determine Number of Exits. A load of 65 falls into the "10 to 499" bracket. Furthermore, it is above the 1st storey and has more than 10 people. Therefore, it requires <b>2 Exits</b>.<br>
  Step 3: Determine Door Swing. Because the occupant load is <b>50 or more</b>, the code mandates that exit doors must swing in the direction of exit travel (outward).
</details>
