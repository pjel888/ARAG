# PD 1096 Rule XII: General Requirements for Egress, Corridors, and Occupant Loads

_An Immersive ALE Review Module (Exam‑Optimized)_

## The Real‑World Scenario (The Hook)

Imagine you are designing a **multi‑storey office building** with **open‑plan floors** and several meeting rooms. Each floor has:

- 200–500+ people working.
- Long corridors connecting workstations to the exit stair.
- Thresholds and small changes in level at exits.

Your design must comply with **PD 1096 Rule XII**, which asks:

- “How many exits per floor?”
- “How wide should the corridor be?”
- “How far can someone walk from the farthest desk to the nearest exit?”
- “At a 250 mm‑high threshold, must it be a ramp or a step?”

This module is crafted using **educational psychology** (chunking, mental imagery, “enemy‑traps”, spaced‑repetition cues) and **behavioral “habit loops”** (exam‑style question patterns, step‑by‑step workflows) so that **any student reading it walks away with both the big picture and the exam‑critical details**.

---

## Core Concepts (With Mental Anchors)

### 1. **Egress (“The Escape Path”)**

**Definition**:  
The **continuous, unobstructed path** from any occupied space to the **outside public way** (street, open area), including:

- Exit doors.
- Exit corridors.
- Exit balconies.
- Exit stairways.

**Mental anchor**:

> “**Egress = the ‘running lane’ from any seat to the street when the fire alarm goes off.**”

**Rule XII emphasis**:

- **Number of exits**, **location**, and **capacity**.
- **Minimum corridor width** (1.10 m).
- **Travel distance** from the farthest point to the nearest exit.

---

### 2. **Corridor Widths (Flow‑Width Logic)**

**Definition**:  
The **minimum width of corridors and exit balconies** depends on **occupant load** and **building height**.

**Mental anchor**:

> “**Corridor width = how many people can walk shoulder‑to‑shoulder safely.**”

**Rule XII basics**:

- **Every corridor or exit balcony** must be **at least 1.10 m wide**.
- Higher occupant load → **wider** corridor (1.50 m, 1.80 m, etc.).

---

### 3. **Occupant Loads (How Many People?)**

**Definition**:  
The **maximum number of people** a space is expected to hold at any time, calculated as:

\[
\text{Occupant load} = \frac{\text{Floor area}}{\text{Unit area per occupant}}
\]

where **unit area per occupant** comes from **Table XII.1** (PD 1096 RIRR).

**Mental anchor**:

> “**Occupant load = the code’s ‘people‑count’ for your floor plan.**”

**Code‑style logic**:

- **Higher occupant load** → **more exits** and **wider corridors**.
- Exits must be **remotely located** if the load is ≥50.

---

### 4. **Stair / Riser‑to‑Ramp Transitions (Safety of Steps vs Ramps)**

**Rule XII idea**:  
For **any exit path** with **occupant load ≥10**:

- A **change in elevation ≤300 mm** must be by **ramp**, not step.
- **Exception**: **Group A (dwellings)** may use **step** instead of ramp.

**Mental anchor**:

> “**If ≥10 people use this exit and the bump is ≤300 mm, it must be a ramp.**”

**Why it matters**:

- Prevents tripping in dark, smoke‑filled, or crowded conditions.
- Acts like a **proto‑accessibility** rule.

## The Hard Data (Cheat Sheet – Code‑Anchor Table)

### 1. Occupant Load and Unit Area (Table XII.1, 2004 RIRR)

| Use or Occupancy                        | Typical Unit Area per Occupant (sq.m) | Exam‑Style Note                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Dwellings (Group A)**                 | 28.00                                 | Very low density; egress usually less strict. |
| **Offices (Group B)**                   | 10.00                                 | Common for offices; higher occupant density.  |
| **Classrooms (Group C, educational)**   | 1.80–2.50                             | High density; strict egress rules.            |
| **Assembly (Group E, theaters, halls)** | 0.50–0.80                             | Very high density; 3–4 exits often required.  |

> **Rule**:
>
> - **Occupant load = floor area ÷ unit area per occupant**.
> - Higher load → **more exits, wider corridors, stricter separation**.

---

### 2. Minimum Exits by Occupant Load (Rule XII, 191–194)

| Occupant Load (per floor) | Minimum Number of Exits                  |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **1–10**                  | 1                                        |
| **11–299**                | 2                                        |
| **300–500**               | 2–3 (depends on travel distance, layout) |
| **500–999**               | 3                                        |
| **1000 or more**          | 4                                        |

> **Exam‑critical notes**:
>
> - **Floors above the first storey** with **>10 occupants** → **at least 2 exits**.
> - **≥500 occupants** → **3 exits**.
> - **≥1000 occupants** → **4 exits**.
> - If **occupant load >50**, exits must be **remotely located** (travel distance between exits ≥ ½ the max room dimension, conceptually).

---

### 3. Corridor and Exit Balcony Widths

| Service Condition            | Minimum Width (m) | Exam‑style Remark                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Serving <50 occupants**    | **1.10**          | Standard minimum for small offices, small clinics.           |
| **Serving 50–500 occupants** | **1.10–1.50**     | 1.10 m is code‑minimum; 1.50 m often in local ordinances.    |
| **Serving >500 occupants**   | **≥1.80**         | Code‑based capacity approach; exam may use “1.80 m or more”. |

> **Rule XII basics**:
>
> - **Every corridor or exit balcony** ≥ **1.10 m wide**.
> - For **high‑occupant floors**, **1.50–1.80 m** corridors are typical.

---

### 4. Stair / Riser‑to‑Ramp Transitions (Rule XII, 194)

- **For any exit path with occupant load ≥10**:
  - A **change in elevation ≤300 mm** must be by **ramp**.
  - **Exception**: Group A (dwellings) may use **step** instead of ramp.

> **Why it matters (exam‑style)**:
>
> - Prevents tripping at thresholds.
> - Encourages **accessible** evacuation, even before full‑accessibility rules.

---

### 5. Exit Door Requirements (Rule XII, 191–194)

| Requirement               | Detail                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Exit door leaf width**  | **≤1.20 m** per leaf. Multi‑leaf doors must follow same rule.                                                                                         |
| **Prohibited exit types** | **Revolving, sliding, overhead** doors **cannot** be used as **required exits**.                                                                      |
| **Swing direction**       | For **exit enclosures** and **occupant load ≥50**, doors must swing **in the direction of egress**.                                                   |
| **Clear opening**         | Must be **≥ J1.10 m** (conceptually, enough to pass a stretcher / crowd). Many exams assume **≥0.70–1.10 m clear width** depending on capacity logic. |

> **Mental anchor**:
>
> - “**Exit door leaf ≤1.20 m; no revolving/sliding/overhead doors as exits.**”

## Step‑by‑Step (Conceptual, Not Pure Computation)

### Step 1 – Determine Occupant Load (Critical First Step)

**Given**:

- Floor area = 300 sq.m.
- Use = **Office (Group B)** → **10.00 sq.m per occupant**.

**Calculation**:
\[
\text{Occupant load} = \frac{300}{10} = 30 \text{ occupants}
\]

**Rule‑based result**:

- 30 occupants → **11–299 bracket** → **2 exits required**.
- **If above first storey** → even 11 occupants → **2 exits**.

**Mental script for exam**:

> “Always compute **occupant load first**, then match to the **1–10, 11–299, 500–999, 1000+** bands.”

---

### Step 2 – Check Corridor Width

- **Corridor serving 30 occupants (<50)** → **minimum 1.10 m**.
- If corridor serves **600 occupants** (e.g., large assembly hall):
  - Occupant load ≥500 → **3 exits (or 4 if ≥1000)**.
  - Corridor width → **≥1.80 m** (capacity‑based).

**Behavioral fix**:

- Highlight **1.10 m** as the **absolute minimum**; many students draw **0.90–1.00 m** corridors and lose points.

---

### Step 3 – Check Ramps vs Steps at Thresholds

- **Scenario**:
  - Corridor approaching exit has a **250 mm drop** at threshold.
  - **Occ−load on corridor ≥10** → **must be a ramp**.
- **Exception**:
  - If the building is **Group A (dwelling)** → **step** is allowed.

**Mental‑image cue**:

> “If the bump is ≤300 mm and ≥10 people use it, must be a **smooth‑slope ramp**, not a step.”

## ALE Exam Traps and “Enemy List” (With Cognitive Fixes)

### 1. **Confusing Group A with Other Groups on Ramps**

**Trap**:  
Students assume **all buildings** must use **ramps** for ≤300 mm changes, forgetting that **Group A** (dwellings) may use **steps**.

**Rule‑based fix**:

- **Group A** (A‑1, A‑2, A‑3, A‑4) → **step allowed** at ≤300 mm.
- **All other groups** → **ramp required** if ≥10 occupants.

**Mental hook**:

> “**If it’s a house/dorm/hotel‑type residential, ≤300 mm can be a step; if it’s office, theatre, school, must be a ramp.**”

---

### 2. **Ignoring the 500–999 (3 Exits) Band**

**Trap**:  
Students remember:

- 1–10 → 1 exit.
- 11–299 → 2 exits.  
  But forget:
- **500–999 → 3 exits**.
- **1000+ → 4 exits**.

**Mnemonic chain**:

> “**1–10: 1. 11–299: 2. 500–999: 3. 1000+: 4.**”  
> Say it out loud 3 times.

---

### 3. **Using 900 mm Corridors**

**Trap**:  
Drawing **0.90 m corridors** for 20–30 occupant floors and thinking “it’s enough.”

**Rule‑based fix**:

- **Minimum 1.10 m** for **any** corridor serving even **10 occupants**.
- **1.50 m** for 50–500.
- **1.80 m** for >500.

**Exam‑style catchphrase**:

> “**If the corridor serves ≥10 occupants, 1.10 m is the code‑minimum, not 0.90 m.**”

---

### 4. **Using Prohibited Doors as Exits**

**Trap**:

- Drawing a **revolving door** as the **only or required** exit.
- Using **sliding** or **overhead doors** as main egress.

**Rule‑based fix**:

- **Required exits**:
  - **No revolving doors.**
  - **No sliding doors.**
  - **No overhead doors.**
- Those may be used **elsewhere**, but **not** as the **mandatory egress**.

**Mental visual**:

> “Exit path must be a **normal swinging door**, not a revolving one.”

---

### 5. **Travel Distance and Remote‑Exit Calculations**

**Trap**:

- Students **only** check **number of exits**, ignoring:
  - **Travel distance** from the **farthest point** to the **nearest exit**.
  - **Distance between two exits** (must be **remotely located** if >50 occupants).

**Rule‑based logic**:

- For **>50 occupants**, two exits must be **separated enough** so that if one is blocked, the other is still usable.
- Typical ALE‑style rule (conceptually):
  - Distance between exit‑doorways ≥ **½ the maximum room dimension**.

**Exam‑style safeguard**:

- In any floor plan question, **draw two lines**:
  - From **farthest desk** to **nearest exit**.
  - Between **two exits**.

## Active Recall Question (With Deep‑Thinking Cues)

**Question**:  
What is the **minimum number of exits required** from a **floor above the first storey** with an **occupant load of 25 people**?

A) One  
B) Two  
C) Three

> **Answer: B) Two**
>
> **Explanation with mental‑retention hooks**:
>
> - Rule XII states that **floors above the first storey having an occupant load of more than ten (10)** must have **at least two exits**.
> - 25 occupants falls in **11–299** → **2 exits**.
> - **1–10** → 1 exit.
> - **500–999** → 3 exits.
> - **1000+** → 4 exits.
>
> **Why A and C are wrong**:
>
> - A → 1 exit only for 1–10 occupants.
> - C → 3 exits only for ≥500 occupants.
>
> **Spaced‑repetition drill**:
>
> - After reading this, **close your eyes** and repeat 3 times:
>   - “Floors above first storey, occupant load >10 → 2 exits.”
> - Then sketch mentally:
>   - A **25‑person floor** with **two exits at opposite sides**.

## Additional Details (Deep‑Structure, Long‑Term Retention)

### 1. General Egress Requirements (Rule XII Core)

- **Minimum exits per floor**:
  - 1–10 → 1.
  - 11–299 → 2.
  - 300–500 → 2–3.
  - 500–999 → 3.
  - 1000+ → 4.
- **Separation of exits**:
  - For **>50 occupants**, exits must be **remotely located** (distance between doorways ≥ ½ maximum room dimension).
- **Exit stairways**:
  - Width scaled to occupant load (conceptually **1.10 m** minimum, more for high‑load floors).
- **Exterior exit balconies**:
  - Count as **required exits** if connected to exit stair.
  - Must be **≥1.10 m wide**, **fire‑resistive**, and **free of storage**.

    300 ÷ 0.6 = 500 → 3 \text{ exits required (if 500–999)}.
