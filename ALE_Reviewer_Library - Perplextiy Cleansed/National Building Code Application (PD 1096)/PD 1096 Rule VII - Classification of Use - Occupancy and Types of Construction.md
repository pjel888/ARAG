## Core Concepts (With Brain‑Friendly Anchors)

### 1. **Classification of Use/Occupancy (Groups A–I)**

**Definition**:  
Rule VII, Section 701, divides all buildings into **occupancy groups A–I** according to **what the building is used for**—not how it looks or how tall it is.

Each group carries **assumed fire‑risk, egress requirement, and materials**.  
For example:

- **A (Residential)** → Lower fire‑load, sleep‑sensitive occupants.
- **H (Hazardous)** → Very high fire‑load, explosion/chemical risk.

**Mental anchor**:

> “**Groups A–I = ‘Purpose Tags’ for the whole building.**"

**Key exam‑style reminder**:

- **Mixed‑use buildings** (e.g., residential + retail) often fall under **Group I**, or must satisfy the **most restrictive** of the involved groups.

---

### 2. **Types of Construction (Types I–V)**

**Definition**:  
Rule VII, Sections 702–703, defines **types of construction** (I–V) based on:

- **Combustibility** of structural elements.
- **Fire‑resistance rating** of walls, floors, and roofs.

Occupancy group + height → **required minimum type of construction**.  
Higher hazard or more storeys = **more fire‑resistant construction type**.

**Mental anchor**:

> “**Types I–V = ‘How much fire it can eat before it collapses’.**”

> “**A–E (ordinary buildings) → III–V.**”

## Step‑by‑Step (Not Computation, but “Matching Logic”)

### Step 1 – Identify Principal Uses (Top‑Down Mind Map)

On an exam question, you often see:

- “Residential condominium tower with ground‑floor retail and rooftop garden.”
- “Hospital with medical office annex and parking basement.”
- “Factory with storage warehouse adjacent.”

**Your 5‑second brain script**:

1. **List all main uses**:
   - Condo + retail + rooftop garden → **A + C (+E? no, if not large‑crowd‑gathering)** → **Group I (mixed use)**.
   - Hospital (G) + parking (D) → **G (dominant, high‑risk)**.
2. **Ask**:
   - “Which use is **most hazardous** or **most vulnerable**?”
   - That group’s rules **dominate** the design (exit requirements, fire‑resistance, construction type).

---

### Step 2 – Match Occupancy to Construction Type (AE‑Style Logic)

**Ask yourself exam‑preset questions**:

1. **“Given a 10‑storey hospital (Group G), what is the minimum type of construction?”**
   - Answer: **Type I (fire‑resistive)**, because:
     - High‑rise.
     - High‑vulnerability occupants.
     - High occupant‑load for egress.

2. **“Can a Group H hazardous warehouse use Type V wood‑frame construction?”**
   - Answer: **Typically NO.**
   - H‑risk + combustible construction = very dangerous.
   - Code usually requires **Type I–II** for high‑hazard or flammable stores.

---

### Step 3 – Common “Matching” Patterns to Memorize

| Scenario (ALE‑Style)                    | Occupancy Group | Likely Construction Type |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------ |
| 2‑storey house (A‑1)                    | A               | III or V                 |
| 10‑storey office tower (B, mixed‑use I) | B / I           | I                        |
| 5‑storey hospital (G)                   | G               | I                        |
| Small factory (F)                       | F               | II–III                   |
| Fuel station (H)                        | H               | I–II                     |
| Low‑rise school (G)                     | G               | II–III                   |

**Mnemonic**:

> “**A–E → III–V; G–H–I‑high‑rise → I–II**."

## ALE Exam Traps and “Enemy List” (With Cognitive Fixes)

### 1. **Swapping B (Business) and C (Mercantile)**

**Trap**:  
Students see “mall with office above” and say “Group B” because “office = B”, ignoring that the **main use** is **retail/merchandise selling**.

**Rule of thumb**:

- If **goods are sold to the public in significant area** → **Group C (mercantile)**.
- If **mostly desks, computers, documents** → **Group B (business)**.

**Mental cue**:

> “**B = Brain‑work; C = Cash‑and‑shopping**."

---

### 2. **Over‑Simplifying Group A (“Everything Residential = A”)**

**Trap**:  
Students think “A = all residential, no need to subdivide”, missing that the exam can test **A‑1, A‑2, A‑3, A‑4** explicitly.

**Sub‑divisions (Table 701, 2004 RIRR)**:

| Subdivision | Example (for ALE)                                                           | Exam‑style clue                                   |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **A‑1**     | Single‑family detached houses, townhouses.                                  | “Nuclear family dwellings, subdivision lots.”     |
| **A‑2**     | Multi‑family (condos, apartments, apartments with shared corridors).        | “High‑rise condo, row of townhouses.”             |
| **A‑3**     | Dormitories, worker‑housing, boarding houses with minimal services.         | “Student dorm, labor camp dormitory.”             |
| **A‑4**     | Residential hotels, lodging houses, serviced apartments with full services. | “Serviced apartment hotel, hotel‑type residence.” |

**Memory hook**:

> “**A‑1 = 1 family; A‑2 = 2+ families; A‑3 = dorms; A‑4 = hotels**."

---

### 3. **Ignoring Group I – Mixed Use**

**Trap**:  
Students classify a **residential‑commercial building** as “A” or “C” only, missing **Group I**.

**Exam‑style cue**:  
Questions often say:

> “buildings containing two or more principal uses” or “residential towers with ground‑floor retail”.

**Rule**:

- If **two or more principal uses** coexist and none is minor (e.g., small office in a huge mall doesn’t make it “I”), the building is **Group I (mixed use)**.
- Compliance must satisfy **all principal uses** or be governed by the **most restrictive** (highest hazard, strictest egress, highest construction type).

---

### 4. **Confusing Types of Construction with Use/Occupancy**

**Trap**:  
Students say:

> “This is Group A, so it must be Type V.”

**Reality**:

- **Occupancy group** (A–I) = **Purpose**.
- **Type of construction** (I–V) = **Material/fire‑resistance**.
- You match them **together** with **height** and **hazard**.

**Exam‑style pattern**:

- **Low‑rise residential** (A‑1, A‑2, small scale) → **Type III or V** (more common).
- **High‑rise residential** (tall condo) → **Type I or II** (higher safety).
- **High‑hazard industrial/H** → **Type I–II** (never Type V in hazardous storage).

**Mnemonic**:

> “**A–E (normal) = III–V; G–H–I‑high‑rise = I–II**."

---

### 5. **Assuming “One‑Size‑Fits‑All” for Construction Type**

**Trap**:  
Students memorize:

> “Hospital = Type I; House = Type V,”  
> then apply it blindly without checking height or local rules.

**Rule VII nuance**:

- **Type depends on:**
  - Occupancy **group**.
  - **Height**.
  - **Local fire‑hazard classification** (e.g., BP‑220, NFPA‑type references).

**Behavioral fix**:

- Add a phrase to your exam‑thinking:
  > “OK, it’s Hospital (G), but how many storeys? What’s the zoning?”

## Active Recall Question (With Deep‑Thinking Hints)

**Question**:  
Under PD 1096 Rule VII, in which group are **residential buildings** classified?

A) Group A  
B) Group C  
C) Group E

> **Answer: A) Group A**  
> **Explanation**:
>
> - In **PD 1096 Rule VII, Section 701**, **residential buildings (houses, apartments, condos, dorms, worker‑housing, serviced apartments)** are classified under **Group A – Residential**.
> - **Group C** is for **mercantile (retail, shops, malls)**.
> - **Group E** is for **assembly (theaters, cinemas, halls, churches, stadiums)**.
>
> **Memory hooks**:
>
> - “**A = Apartment, A = all homes**.”
> - “**C = Cash‑counter; E = Everyone‑gathering**.”
>
> **Exam‑style drill**:  
> After reading this, close your eyes and repeat 3 times:
>
> - “Residential = Group A. Apartment, condo, dorm, house → all A.”
> - “Shopping → C. Crowd‑hall/church/theater → E.”

## Additional Details (Deep‑Structure, Long‑Term Retention)

### 1. **Sub‑Division “Mental Drill” (For A‑1, A‑2, A‑3, A‑4)**

Every time you see “residential building” in a question, force your brain to ask:

1. **Is it single‑family, detached/attached, townhouse?** → **A‑1**.
2. **Is it multi‑family, condo, aparment block?** → **A‑2**.
3. **Is it a dormitory or worker‑dorm type?** → **A‑3**.
4. **Is it residentail hotel / serviced apartments with full services?** → **A‑4**.

**Quick‑check mnemonics**:

- **A‑1 = 1 family.**
- **A‑2 = 2+ families.**
- **A‑3 = Dorm.**
- **A‑4 = Hotel/Service.**

---

### 2. **Mixed‑Use (Group I) “Flowchart”**

When you see:

- Residential above retail.
- Office + cinema in one tower.
- Hotel + shops.

**Mental script**:

1. **Are there two or more principal uses?**
   - Yes → **Group I (mixed use)**.
2. **If one use is more hazardous or restrictive?**
   - e.g., **G (hospital)** or **H (fuel)** dominant → design follows that group’s stricter rules.
   - e.g., **E (theater)** dominant → focus on egress and travel‑distance rules.
