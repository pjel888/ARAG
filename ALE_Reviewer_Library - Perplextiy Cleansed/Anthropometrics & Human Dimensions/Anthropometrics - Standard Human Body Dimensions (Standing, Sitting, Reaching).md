# Anthropometrics: Standard Human Body Dimensions (Standing, Sitting, Reaching)

_An Immersive ALE Review Module – Maximized for Board Exam Retention_

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)

Picture yourself as the lead architect for a new Quezon City community center. An elderly user in a wheelchair can't reach the light switch, a child topples from a too-high bench, and tall adults bump their heads on door frames. **Your job: Use anthropometrics to design spaces that fit _everyone_.** This prevents lawsuits, ensures safety, and aces your ALE board exam.

**Quick Win**: Anthropometrics = "Human-fit design." Master it to score high on accessibility questions.

## Core Concepts (Locked In with Mnemonics)

- **Anthropometrics**: Science of human body measurements for design (Mnemonic: _Anthro_ = human + _metrics_ = measures).
- **PD 1096 RIRR (2008) Mandate**: Designs _must_ consider varying person sizes, **standing/sitting reaches**, and **lines of sight** (Rule IV, Sec. 404.1.6; NB: Updated from 2004 to 2008 version).
- **Key Positions**:
  | Position | Why It Matters | Exam Focus |
  |----------|---------------|------------|
  | **Standing** | Door heights, shelf reaches | Max reach ~2.2m (95th percentile male) |
  | **Sitting** | Desk heights, counters | Elbow height ~0.7-0.8m; eye level ~1.1-1.2m |
  | **Reaching** | Switches, controls | Standing: 1.8-2.2m; Seated: 1.2-1.5m |

_Fact-Check_: PD 1096 doesn't list _exact_ numbers (that's the trap!). It requires **consideration** of 5th-95th percentile data from sources like NASA or Philippine standards. Use general values for exams.

## ALE Exam Traps & Mistakes (Avoid These!)

1. **Trap #1**: Picking exact numbers PD 1096 doesn't give → Wrong! Answer: "Varying sizes & reaches."
2. **Trap #2**: Ignoring seated/wheelchair users → Fails accessibility (ADA-inspired rules).
3. **Trap #3**: Confusing 5th/95th percentiles → 5th = smallest (design MIN clearance); 95th = largest (design MAX space).
   _Pro Tip_: Exams test **principles**, not memorizing mm. Phrase answers: "Per PD 1096, consider anthropometric data for inclusivity."

## Active Recall Drill (Test Yourself – No Peeking!)

**Q1**: PD 1096 RIRR requires designs to account for what 3 anthropometric factors?  
**A**: Varying sizes, reaches (standing/sitting), lines of sight.

**Q2**: True/False: PD 1096 provides exact standing reach heights.  
**A**: **False** – It mandates _consideration_, not specifics.

**Q3**: For a counter, what's the safe elbow height range (sitting)?  
**A**: 0.65m - 0.85m (adjust for 5th-95th percentile).

## Step-by-Step Design Application

1. **Identify Users**: Kids (5th %), adults (50th %), tall males (95th %), wheelchair.
2. **Select Data**: Standing reach max = 2.2m → Place switches ≤2.0m.
3. **Verify PD 1096**: Ensure "varying reaches" covered (Sec. 404).
4. **Iterate**: Test with sketch → Adjust for safety.

**Example Calc**: Door header for 95th male (1.85m + 0.2m clearance) = **2.05m min**.

## Verified Sources (Exam-Proof)
