# The Bauhaus and the International Style: Form Follows Function
*An Immersive ALE Review Module*

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)
Imagine you're an architect tasked to design a new building that adheres strictly to modernist principles. You must decide whether your approach will be influenced by the minimalist, functional ethos of the Bauhaus or the more streamlined and rational International Style.

## Core Concepts
- **Bauhaus**: A German art school founded in 1919 emphasizing the integration of fine arts with crafts.
- **International Style**: An architectural style that emerged in the early 20th century characterized by simplicity, functionality, and minimal ornamentation.
- **Form Follows Function (F3)**: The principle that design should be based on function rather than aesthetics.

## ALE Exam Traps and Common Mistakes
Common mistakes include confusing the principles of Bauhaus with those of International Style or misunderstanding that form follows function means purely aesthetic design without regard for practicality.

## Active Recall Question
Which architectural style emphasizes minimal ornamentation, simplicity, and functionality?
A) The Bauhaus  
B) Form Follows Function (F3)  
C) The International Style  

<blockquote>
Answer: C) The International Style. Explanation: While both the Bauhaus and F3 emphasize function over form, the International Style is specifically known for its minimal ornamentation and streamlined design.
</blockquote>

## Additional Details

The Bauhaus was a German art school operational from 1919 to 1933 that greatly influenced modern design. It emphasized functionalism, rejecting ornamental details for simplicity and practicality in architecture, furniture, graphic design, interior design, typography, and industrial design.

The International Style is an architectural style developed in the 1920s and 1930s by architects such as Walter Gropius, Le Corbusier, and Ludwig Mies van der Rohe. It emphasizes simplicity, functionality, and minimal ornamentation with a focus on modern materials like glass and steel.

Form Follows Function is a principle in architecture that suggests the shape of a building or object should primarily relate to its intended function or purpose. This concept was popularized by American architect Louis Sullivan and became central to Modernist architectural theory.
