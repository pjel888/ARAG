# PD 1096 Rule XII: General Requirements for Egress, Corridors, and Occupant Loads
*An Immersive ALE Review Module*

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)
Imagine you are designing an office building with multiple floors. Each floor has a varying number of occupants based on the type of use. You need to ensure that your design complies with PD 1096 Rule XII, which sets out specific requirements for egress routes and corridors.

## Core Concepts
- **Egress**: The means by which people exit from a building in case of emergency.
- **Corridor Widths**: Minimum widths required based on the number of occupants served.
- **Occupant Loads**: Determining how many people are expected to occupy an area at any given time, influencing egress requirements.

## Additional Details

**PD 1096**: The National Building Code of the Philippines, as revised by PD 1096 RIRR 2004.

**Rule XII - General Design and Construction Requirements**: This rule outlines general requirements for building design and construction including safety standards, material quality, methods of construction, type of occupancy classification, and supplementary guidance from applicable referral codes. 

### General Requirements for Egress
According to PD 1096 RIRR 2004:
- Floors above the first storey with more than ten occupants must have at least two exits.
- Changes in floor elevations less than 300 millimeters along any exit serving a tributary occupant load of ten or more shall be by means of ramp, except for Group A Occupancies.

### Corridors
Corridor and exterior exit balcony requirements include:
- Width: Every corridor or exit balcony must not be less than 1.10 meters in width.
- Construction: Walls and ceilings should have at least one-hour fire-resistive construction (exceptions apply).

### Occupant Loads
Occupant load determination is based on dividing the floor area assigned to a use by the unit area allowed per occupant as shown in Table XII.1, determined by the Secretary of Public Works and Highways.
- Exit requirements are dictated by the number of occupants:
  - Floors with an occupant load between 500 to 999 require at least three exits.
  - Floors with an occupant load of 1000 or more must have a minimum of four exits.

These provisions ensure safe and efficient egress in buildings, adhering strictly to the guidelines set forth by PD 1096 RIRR 2004.
