# BP 344 vs PD 1096: Resolving Conflicting Ramp and Corridor Width Requirements
*An Immersive ALE Review Module*

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)
Imagine you are designing an accessible building entrance that must comply with both Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344) and Presidential Decree No. 1096 (PD 1096). You need to ensure the ramp width meets BP 344's accessibility standards while also adhering to PD 1096’s general building regulations.

## Core Concepts
- **Accessible Ramps**: Minimum clear widths, gradient limits, and landing requirements.
- **Corridors**: Widths for required exits serving more than ten occupants.
- **Conflict Resolution**: Determining which regulation takes precedence when rules conflict.

## The Hard Data (Cheat Sheet)
| Regulation | Requirement |
|------------|-------------|
| BP 344     | Accessible ramps must have a minimum clear width of 1200 mm. <br> Gradient not steeper than 1:20; special cases allow up to 1:15 with specific length and rise limits (Table A.1.1).<br> Intermediate landings required for ramps longer than 10.50 m, each at least 1800 mm.<br> Level areas of 1800 mm at both ends of ramps. |
| PD 1096    | Corridor width minimum is 1.10 meters for required exits serving more than ten occupants.<br>Ramps for entrances/exits must not exceed a slope of 1/10. |

## ALE Exam Traps and Common Mistakes
- **Trap**: Assuming PD 1096's corridor width requirement overrides BP 344’s ramp requirements.
- **Mistake**: Failing to provide intermediate landings for ramps longer than the specified length in BP 344.

## Active Recall Question
Which regulation takes precedence when designing an accessible building entrance that must comply with both Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344) and Presidential Decree No. 1096 (PD 1096)?

A) BP 344, because it specifies wider ramps for accessibility needs.
B) PD 1096, as it sets the general building regulations.

> **Answer:** A) BP 344 takes precedence due to its specific focus on accessibility requirements.

## Additional Details

- **PD 1096** specifies that ramps for entrances/exits must not exceed a slope of 1:10, aligning closely with BP 344's requirement but providing broader coverage. Additionally, PD 1096 mandates that sidewalks should be level where possible and allows slopes up to 1:8 in special cases.
- **BP 344** requires accessible ramps to have a minimum clear width of 1200 mm with gradients not steeper than 1:20. Steeper gradients are allowed under specific conditions, as detailed in Table A.1.1 for lengths and rises up to 700mm.
- **PD 1096** mandates that corridors must be at least 1.10 meters wide when serving an occupant load of more than ten individuals, ensuring sufficient space for movement and safety.
- Both documents emphasize the importance of unobstructed widths in corridors and exterior exit balconies to ensure accessibility and ease of passage. Trim handrails and doors should not reduce corridor width by more than 200 millimeters or half its required width when fully opened.
- **BP 344** further specifies that ramps longer than 10.50 meters must have intermediate landings at least 1800 mm long, ensuring rest areas for individuals using the ramp.
- Both regulations highlight the necessity of level areas measuring at least 1800 mm at both ends of accessible ramps to facilitate safe entry and exit points.
- **PD 1096** also addresses driveway connections with sidewalks, requiring a maximum slope not exceeding 1:8 when connecting driveways are joined by means of a ramp.
