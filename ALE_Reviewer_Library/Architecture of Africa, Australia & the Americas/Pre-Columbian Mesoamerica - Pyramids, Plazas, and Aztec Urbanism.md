# Pre-Columbian Mesoamerica: Pyramids, Plazas, and Aztec Urbanism
*An Immersive ALE Review Module*

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)
Imagine you are an architect tasked with designing a modern public space inspired by the ancient plazas of Tenochtitlán. How would you incorporate elements from Mesoamerican urban planning into your design?

## Core Concepts
- **Pyramids**: Large religious structures, often serving as temples.
- **Plazas**: Open spaces used for communal activities and gatherings.
- **Aztec Urbanism**: The grid-based city layout and construction techniques of the Aztecs.

## The Hard Data (Cheat Sheet)
| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Pyramids | Religious structures, often serving as temples. Examples include Teotihuacán's Sun Pyramid and Moon Pyramid. |
| Plazas | Public spaces used for communal activities such as markets or religious ceremonies. |
| Aztec Urbanism | Grid-based city layout with use of adobe bricks and volcanic stone like tezontle. |

## ALE Exam Traps and Common Mistakes
1. **Misidentifying Pyramids**: Be careful not to confuse the significance of pyramids in Mesoamerican architecture with other ancient civilizations.
2. **Overlooking Plazas' Functions**: Remember that plazas were multifunctional spaces, often serving as marketplaces or religious centers.

## Active Recall Question
Which material was commonly used by Aztec builders for construction?
A) Limestone  
B) Tezontle (volcanic rock)  
C) Marble  

<details>
  <summary>Answer and Explanation</summary>
  The correct answer is B: Tezontle. This porous volcanic stone, ranging in color from black to crimson, was widely used by Aztec builders due to its availability and workability.
</details>
