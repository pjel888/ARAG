# Pre-Colonial Vernacular: The Anatomy, Joinery, and Evolution of the Bahay Kubo
*An Immersive ALE Review Module*

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)
Imagine you are tasked with designing a modern interpretation of a traditional Filipino house that adheres to pre-colonial vernacular principles. Understanding the anatomy and evolution of the bahay kubo is crucial for capturing its essence while incorporating contemporary needs.

## Core Concepts
- **Pre-Colonial Vernacular**: The bahay kubo, literally "cube house," refers to an indigenous dwelling common in Philippine lowlands.
- **Anatomy**: It consists of a raised floor on stilts and typically includes living spaces above ground level with open sides for ventilation.
- **Joinery**: Traditional joinery techniques used natural materials like bamboo or wood without nails.

## ALE Exam Traps and Common Mistakes
1. **Misunderstanding the Anatomy**: Failing to recognize that the bahay kubo is raised on stilts for ventilation and protection from flooding.
2. **Joinery Techniques**: Assuming modern construction methods were used instead of traditional joinery without nails or metal fasteners.

## Active Recall Question
What are two key features distinguishing a pre-colonial Bahay Kubo from its evolved form, the Bahay na Bato?

A) Use of stone and wood vs bamboo and nipa leaves  
B) Raised floor on stilts vs ground-level construction  
C) Open sides for ventilation vs enclosed walls  

**Answer: A)**

The bahay kubo is traditionally constructed using natural materials like bamboo or cogon grass, while the Bahay na Bato incorporates stone and wood elements during Spanish colonial times.

## Additional Details

The construction techniques and materials used in joinery for Bahay Kubo are traditionally crafted using natural fibers such as abaca or rattan to bind bamboo poles together. This method ensures flexibility during earthquakes while maintaining structural integrity.

Regarding the evolution timeline from Bahay Kubo to Bahay na Bato, it began around the 16th century with Spanish colonization and continued through subsequent centuries, incorporating stone foundations and walls alongside wooden elements for increased durability against natural disasters and improved aesthetics influenced by colonial architectural styles.
