## What You Need to Know for the ALE

**Scenario:** On the ALE, you may encounter a problem like: "A building entrance has a ramp leading up to it. The ramp rises 0.70 m over a horizontal distance of 12 m. According to BP 344 (2024 IRR), what tactile warning surface must be installed at the top and bottom of this ramp?" Many candidates incorrectly assume standard floor tiles suffice without specific tactile requirements.[file:1][web:12]

To master this topic, memorise:

- **Tactile warning surfaces** (truncated domes) required at top/bottom of ramps, stairs, escalators
- **Braille signage**: Grade 2 Braille with raised letters (0.75 mm), height 16-50 mm
- **Visual alarms**: Visible from seated/standing positions in emergency areas
- **Luminance contrast**: Minimum **50%** between tactile blocks and surrounding floor [file:1][web:12]

## Core Concept

**Historical/Legal Context:** BP 344 2024 IRR updates enhance PWD navigation through standardized tactile, Braille, and visual warning systems aligned with international accessibility standards.[web:12]

**Definitions:**

- **Tactile Warning Surface:** Textured floor blocks (truncated domes) alerting visually impaired users to hazards.
- **Braille Signage:** Tactile signs with Grade 2 Braille for public/safety areas.
- **Visual Alarm System:** Flashing lights for hearing-impaired emergency notification.[web:17]

**Specific Requirements (2024 IRR):**

- **Tactile warning blocks**: Required at top/bottom of **all ramps ≥0.70m rise**, stairs, escalators, platform edges, vehicle-pedestrian crossings
- **Braille signage**: Letters/numerals raised **0.75 mm**, height **16-50 mm**; Grade 2 Braille mandatory
- **Visual alarms**: **Visible from seated (1.2m) and standing (1.7m)** heights in fire exits, assembly areas
- **Luminance contrast**: **≥50%** between tactile blocks and adjacent flooring [file:1][web:12]

**Analogies:**

1. **Tactile blocks as Braille speed bumps**: Alert visually impaired to elevation changes
2. **Braille signs as 3D maps**: Provide touch-readable directions
3. **Visual alarms as silent sirens**: Emergency notification without sound [file:1]

**Practical Design Implications:** In Manila's crowded walkways, tactile surfaces must not create trip hazards while maintaining **50% contrast** for visibility.[web:12]

## How It's Tested on the ALE

**Pattern 1: Tactile Location**
**Question:** "0.70m ramp requires tactile warnings where?"
**Answer:** **Top AND bottom** of ramp [web:12]

**Pattern 2: Braille Dimensions**
**Question:** "Braille signage letter requirements?"
**Answer:** **16-50mm height**, **0.75mm raised**, Grade 2 [file:1]

**Pattern 3: Slope + Tactile**
**Question:** "12m run, 0.70m rise ramp needs what warnings?"
**Answer:** **1:17 slope** → **truncated dome blocks** top/bottom [file:1]

## Common Mistakes

1. **Tactile placement**: Only stairs (WRONG) → **All ramps ≥0.70m**
2. **Braille specs**: Wrong height (16-50mm required)
3. **Visual alarms**: Single height → **Dual seated/standing visibility**
4. **No contrast**: Tactile blocks must have **50% luminance contrast**
5. **Ramp exemption**: All accessible ramps need warnings [file:1][web:12]

## Key Terms

- **Truncated domes**: Standard tactile warning pattern
- **Grade 2 Braille**: Contracted Braille system
- **Luminance contrast**: **≥50%** required
- **Detectable warnings**: Tactile + visual floor alerts [web:12]
