## What You Need to Know for the ALE

**Scenario:** On the ALE, you may encounter a problem like: “A proposed ramp has a total rise of 0.70 m and must comply with BP 344 (2024 IRR). If the site allows a maximum run of 12 m, what slope tier should be used?” Many candidates incorrectly assume that all ramps can use the most lenient slope tier without considering length limits. To master this topic, memorise:

- Maximum gradient tiers: **1:20** (standard), **1:15** (up to 10.5 m length, 700 mm rise), limited use of steeper slopes like 1:12 in special cases
- Run = Rise × slope ratio (e.g., for 1:15, Run = Rise × 15)
- Intermediate landings: every 10-12 m for longer runs, depending on slope
- Minimum width: **1.20 m**; landing length: **1.50 m** [file:1]

## Core Concept

**Historical/Legal Context:** BP 344 (Accessibility Law) was updated in 2024 to align with international standards, emphasizing accessibility for persons with disabilities. The 2024 IRR provides stricter ramp guidelines under PD 1096 for safety and ease of use [file:1].

**Definitions:**

- **Ramp:** A sloped surface used as an alternative to stairs.
- **Gradient (slope):** Ratio of vertical rise to horizontal run (e.g., 1:20 means 1 unit rise for every 20 units run).
- **Run Length:** Horizontal distance covered by the ramp.
- **Landing:** Level platform at top, bottom, and intermediate points [file:1].

**Specific Dimensions and Formulas (2024 IRR):**

- Maximum gradient tiers:
  - **1:20** for ramps longer than 6 m (standard maximum).
  - **1:15** allowed up to **10.5 m** length with max rise **0.70 m** (700 mm).
  - Steeper slopes (e.g., 1:12) not standard; permitted only in special cases with shorter runs and landings every ~8 m.
- \( \text{Run} = \text{Rise} \times \text{slope ratio} \)
  - For 1:12: \( \text{Run} = \text{Rise} \times 12 \)
  - For 1:15: \( \text{Run} = \text{Rise} \times 15 \)
  - For 1:20: \( \text{Run} = \text{Rise} \times 20 \) [file:1]
- Maximum rise per run (for steeper slopes): **0.70 m**
- Minimum ramp width: **1.20 m**
- Landing length (minimum): **1.50 m**; width **1.80 m** at top/bottom
- Intermediate landings required every **10.5 m+** for applicable slopes [web:3][web:4]

**Analogies:**

1. **Ramp as a gentle hill:** Flatter slopes (1:20) are easier for wheelchairs.
2. **Slope ratio as a ruler:** 1:20 requires 20 m run per 1 m rise.
3. **Landings as rest stops:** Provide space to pause and turn [file:1].

**Practical Design Implications:** In urban areas like Manila (Quezon City), space limits favor steeper slopes, but 2024 IRR prioritizes 1:20 with exceptions for shorter runs to ensure PWD accessibility [file:1].

## How It’s Tested on the ALE

### Question Patterns and Strategies

**Pattern 1: Slope Tier Selection**

- **Question:** "Ramp rise 0.70 m, max run 12 m. What slope?"
- **Strategy:** Check runs: 1:12=8.4 m, 1:15=10.5 m, 1:20=14 m. Use 1:15 (fits site) [file:1].

**Pattern 2: Max Rise Calculation**

- **Question:** "1:15 gradient, max run 10 m. Max rise?"
- **Strategy:** Rise = Run / 15 = 10/15 ≈ 0.67 m (<0.70 m limit).

**Pattern 3: Landings Needed**

- **Question:** "1:15 ramp, total run 20 m. Intermediate landings?"
- **Strategy:** Every 10.5 m → 1 landing.

**Pattern 4: Compliance Check**

- **Question:** "1:20 gradient, rise 0.70 m compliant?"
- **Strategy:** Yes, if run ≤ site limits.

**Sample Problem:** Ramp rise 0.70 m, max run 12 m. Slope tier?

- 1:12 run=8.4 m ✓
- 1:15 run=10.5 m ✓
- 1:20 run=14 m ✗
- **Use 1:15** (steepest fitting) [file:1].

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1:** Using 1:12 universally (not standard).

- **Correct:** 1:20 default; 1:15 limited [file:1].

**Mistake 2:** Wrong run formula (divide instead of multiply).

- **Correct:** Run = Rise × ratio.

**Mistake 3:** Ignoring length limits for 1:15.

- **Correct:** Max 10.5 m [file:1].

**Mistake 4:** Exceeding 0.70 m rise.

- **Correct:** Cap at 700 mm.

**Mistake 5:** Omitting landings.

- **Correct:** Every 10+ m [web:3].

## Key Terms

- **Ramp Gradient:** 1:20 standard, 1:15 up to 10.5 m.
- **Max Rise:** 0.70 m per run.
- **Width:** 1.20 m min.
- **Landings:** 1.50 m length [file:1].
