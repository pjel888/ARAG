## Core Concept
### Historical/Legal Context
The Building Code of the Philippines (BP) 344 was updated in 2024 to align with international standards for accessibility. The new provisions under BP 344, specifically PD 1096, mandate stricter guidelines for ramp design to ensure ease of movement and safety for individuals with disabilities.

### Definitions
- **Ramp:** A sloped surface used as an alternative to stairs.
- **Gradient:** The slope of the ramp expressed as a ratio (e.g., 1:20).
- **Run Length:** The horizontal distance covered by the ramp.
- **Maximum Rise:** The vertical height that can be accommodated in one continuous run.

### Specific Dimensions and Formulas
- Maximum gradient tiers:
  - \( \text{Gradient} = 1:20 \) for ramps longer than 6 meters.
  - \( \text{Gradient} = 1:15 \) allowed up to a maximum length of 10.5 meters with a rise not exceeding 700 mm.
  - \( \text{Gradient} = 1:12 \) for shorter ramps, but must be accompanied by intermediate landings every 8 meters.

- Minimum width:
  - \( \text{Minimum Width} = 1.20\,m \)

- Landing dimensions:
  - \( \text{Landing Length} = 1.50\,m \)
  - \( \text{Landing Width} = 1.80\,m \) at top and bottom of ramp

### Analogies
1. **Ramp as a Gentle Slope:** Think of a ramp like a gentle hill in a park that allows people to walk up without difficulty.
2. **Gradient as a Ratio:** Imagine the gradient as a ratio where 1:20 means for every meter you go horizontally, you rise by 5 cm vertically.
3. **Landings as Rest Stops:** Landings are akin to rest stops on highways, providing a place to pause and regain energy before continuing.

### Practical Design Implications
In Manila, site constraints often necessitate the use of ramps in tight spaces or where stairs would be impractical due to height restrictions. The 2024 IRR updates ensure that these ramps are safe and accessible for all users, particularly those with mobility aids like wheelchairs.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Ramp Gradients with Stairway Requirements:**
   - Misunderstanding that ramp gradients are different from stairway requirements.
   - **Correct Understanding:** Ramps have specific gradient tiers (1:20, 1:15, 1:12) based on length.

2. **Omitting Intermediate Landings:**
   - Forgetting to include intermediate landings every 8 meters for ramps with a gradient of 1:12.
   - **Correct Understanding:** Ensure compliance with BP 344 (2024 IRR).

3. **Incorrect Calculation of Maximum Rise:**
   - Miscalculating the maximum rise based on given length and gradient.
   - **Correct Understanding:** Use specific formulas provided in the code.

4. **Neglecting Minimum Width Requirements:**
   - Ignoring minimum width requirements for ramps.
   - **Correct Understanding:** Ensure ramp widths meet specified dimensions (1.20 m).

5. **Misinterpreting Landing Dimensions:**
   - Incorrectly applying landing length and width requirements.
   - **Correct Understanding:** Refer to specific code sections detailing landing dimensions.
