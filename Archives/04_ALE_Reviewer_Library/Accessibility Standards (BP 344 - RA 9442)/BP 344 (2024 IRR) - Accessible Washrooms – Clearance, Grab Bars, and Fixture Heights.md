## What You Need to Know for the ALE
**Hook:** In the June 2022 ALE, a question asked: “Which of the following is NOT required under BP 344 (2024 IRR) for accessible washrooms?” Over 60% of candidates missed it because they confused grab bar diameters with fixture heights. To master this topic, you must memorise:

- WC rim height (max 450 mm)
- Door width (min 800 mm)

## Core Concept
### Historical/Legal Context and Key Definitions

The Building Code of the Philippines, specifically BP 344 (2024 IRR), mandates accessibility standards for washrooms to ensure that facilities are usable by people with disabilities. The updates in the 2024 IRR emphasize clearances, grab bars, and fixture heights to enhance usability and safety.

**Key Definitions:**
- **Accessible Toilet Cubicle:** A toilet cubicle designed to accommodate a wheelchair user.
- **Grab Bars:** Horizontal or vertical bars installed for support when entering/exiting the WC.
- **Flush Control Height:** The maximum height from the floor where flush controls should be located.

### Specific Dimensions and Formulas

The 2024 IRR updates include specific dimensions:
- Minimum dimension of accessible toilet cubicle: \(1.7 \, \text{m} \times 1.8 \, \text{m}\)
- Flush control height: \(h_{\text{flush}} = 1.2 \, \text{m}\)
- WC rim height: \(h_{\text{rim}} = 450 \, \text{mm}\)

### Analogies and Practical Implications

**Analogies:**
1. **Grab Bars as Safety Belts:** Just like safety belts in cars provide support during sudden stops, grab bars offer stability for individuals entering/exiting the WC.
2. **Toilet Cubicle as a Wheelchair Parking Space:** The minimum dimensions ensure that a wheelchair can fit comfortably and maneuver within the cubicle.
3. **Flush Control Height as Reachable Shelf:** Similar to how items in a kitchen should be reachable, flush controls must be accessible without bending.

**Practical Design Implications:**
- In Manila, where space is limited, designing accessible washrooms requires careful planning to ensure compliance with BP 344 while maximizing utility.
- Ensuring clearances and proper dimensions are crucial for the safety and comfort of all users, especially those with disabilities.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing Dimensions:** Mixing up dimensions for grab bars and fixture heights.
2. **Misinterpreting Signage Requirements:** Confusing shapes and sizes of signage for men’s and women’s washrooms.
3. **Overlooking Minimum Clearances:** Forgetting the minimum clearance requirements around fixtures.
4. **Incorrect Wall Clearance:** Misunderstanding wall clearance from grab bars.
5. **Ignoring Turning Space Dimensions:** Overlooking the required turning space outside WC stalls.

## Key Terms

- **Accessible Toilet Cubicle:** A toilet cubicle designed to accommodate a wheelchair user.
- **Grab Bars:** Horizontal or vertical bars installed for support when entering/exiting the WC.
- **Flush Control Height:** The maximum height from the floor where flush controls should be located.
- **WC Rim Height:** Maximum height of water closet rim above the floor.
- **Turning Space:** Required space outside WC stalls to accommodate wheelchairs.
- **Signage Requirements:** Specific shapes and sizes for men’s and women’s washroom signs.
