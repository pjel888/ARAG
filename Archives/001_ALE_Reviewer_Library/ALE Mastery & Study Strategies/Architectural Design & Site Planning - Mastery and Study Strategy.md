## ALE Mastery Strategy for Philippine Architecture Licensure Examination

### 1. The "Big Picture" & ALE Focus
**Architectural Design and Site Planning** is not merely a subject; it is the **core competency** of the Architectural Licensure Examination (ALE). As noted in the provided context, this segment carries a **massive 40% weight** of the total examination. 

This subject bridges the gap between abstract theory (Day 1) and applied problem-solving (Day 2). The Board is not testing your ability to memorize history; they are testing your ability to **organize space logically** and **ensure safety legally**. 

Think of this subject as the **constitution of the building**. Just as a constitution dictates the rights and duties of citizens, the National Building Code (PD 1096) and Fire Code (RA 9514) dictate the rights and duties of the building's occupants. Your design must respect these "rights" (setbacks, egress, fire lanes) to be valid.

---

### 2. Narrative Logic & First Principles
Why do we analyze a site before drawing a line? Because **nature dictates form**. 

*   **The Doctor Analogy:** Before a surgeon operates, they diagnose the patient. Before an architect designs, they diagnose the site.
*   **The Puzzle Analogy:** Site planning is a 3D puzzle. You have fixed pieces (topography, existing structures, utilities) and you must fit the new pieces (building footprint) without breaking the rules (setbacks, fire codes).
*   **The Legal Shield:** Your design is a shield against liability. If you ignore a fire lane, you are not just designing poorly; you are designing dangerously.

**First Principles of Site Planning:**
1.  **Safety First:** Life safety (egress, fire protection) > Aesthetics.
2.  **Efficiency:** Every square meter must serve a purpose (GFA vs. Floor Area).
3.  **Compliance:** PD 1096 and BP 220 are the law. Ignorance is not a defense.

### 3. Technical Specifications & Spatial Data
To master this subject, you must internalize the **Philippine Standard Units** and **Legal Limits**. Memorize these values; they are the "constants" of the exam.

| Parameter | Standard / Code | Value / Limit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Minimum Lot Width** | PD 1096 | 6 meters (for residential) |
| **Minimum Lot Area** | PD 1096 | 120 sqm (for residential) |
| **Setback (Front)** | PD 1096 | 3 meters (residential) |
| **Setback (Side)** | PD 1096 | 1 meter (residential) |
| **Fire Lane Width** | RA 9514 | 3 meters minimum |
| **Fire Escape Width** | PD 1096 | 1.2 meters minimum |
| **GFA Calculation** | PD 1096 | Sum of all floor areas (excluding open spaces) |

**LaTeX Formula for GFA:**
$$ GFA = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (L_i \times W_i) - \text{Open Spaces} - \text{Non-habitable Areas} $$

### 4. Computation & Application Patterns
The ALE often presents a scenario where you must calculate compliance. Here is a typical problem pattern:

**Problem:** A lot has a frontage of 10 meters and a depth of 20 meters. The building is to be a 2-story residential structure. Calculate the maximum allowable GFA if the setbacks are 3m (front), 1m (sides), and 1m (rear).

**Solution:**
1.  **Lot Area:** $10 \text{ m} \times 20 \text{ m} = 200 \text{ m}^2$
2.  **Building Footprint:**
    *   Front: $10 - 3 = 7 \text{ m}$
    *   Sides: $20 - 1 - 1 = 18 \text{ m}$ (Wait, side setbacks are usually measured from the lot line).
    *   *Correction:* Side setbacks are typically 1m each.
    *   Width available: $10 - 1 - 1 = 8 \text{ m}$
    *   Depth available: $20 - 3 - 1 = 16 \text{ m}$
    *   Footprint Area: $8 \text{ m} \times 16 \text{ m} = 128 \text{ m}^2$
3.  **GFA:** $128 \text{ m}^2 \times 2 \text{ floors} = 256 \text{ m}^2$

**Tip:** Always check if the lot is a **corner lot**. Corner lots often have different setback requirements (e.g., 3m front, 3m side, 3m rear).

### 5. The Examiner's Mindset (Trap Analysis)
The Board loves to test your attention to detail. Here are the common traps:

*   **Trap 1: Fixture vs. Personal Property.**
    *   *Question:* "Which is not considered a fixture?"
    *   *Options:* Water Closet, Bidet, Shower Tray, Lavatory Mixer.
    *   *Answer:* **Shower Tray** (often considered a fixture, but sometimes removable depending on attachment). However, in the context of the provided snippet, the question implies a distinction. Usually, **Lavatory Mixer** (if not permanently attached) might be the trick, but standard plumbing fixtures (WC, Bidet, Shower) are fixtures. *Note: The provided text snippet suggests a specific question where the answer key is critical.*
    *   *Examiner Logic:* They want to see if you know the **Civil Code** definition of a fixture (attached to the land).

*   **Trap 2: Landscape Architecture Licensure.**
    *   *Question:* "All Architects can practice landscape architecture unconditionally."
    *   *Answer:* **No.**
    *   *Reasoning:* Landscape Architecture is a **separate profession** requiring its own licensure (RA 11823 or similar). An architect cannot practice landscape architecture without the specific license.

*   **Trap 3: Fire Code Principles.**
    *   *Question:* "What is the active principle of burning?"
    *   *Answer:* **Heat** (or Fire Triangle: Fuel, Oxygen, Heat).
    *   *Examiner Logic:* They want to ensure you understand the **Fire Triangle**. Removing any one element stops the fire.
