# Masterclass Study Module: Hydrology in Site Planning
## Topic: Stormwater Runoff and Drainage Systems
## Context: Philippine Architecture Licensure Examination (ALE)

### 1. The "Big Picture" & ALE Focus
**The Core Concept:**
Water is the Philippines' greatest resource and its most dangerous adversary. In site planning, hydrology is not merely a utility calculation; it is a **Life Safety** and **Environmental Compliance** issue.

**Why This Matters for the ALE:**
The National Building Code (PD 1096) and the Philippine Environmental Code (RA 9266) mandate that architects must ensure a structure does not exacerbate flooding or erosion.
*   **The Trap:** Many candidates treat drainage as a "Civil Engineering problem."
*   **The Architect's Role:** You are the **integrator**. You must ensure the building orientation, façade, and circulation systems work *with* the water, not against it.
*   **Exam Weight:** Questions often appear in the **Site Planning** and **Environmental Impact** sections.

---

### 2. Narrative Logic & First Principles
**The "Why" Behind the Rules:**
The provided text highlights a critical shift in philosophy:
> *"Detention basins may be designed to meet a predeveloped storm rate of discharge, but the nature of a basin is to concentrate the flow... and to extend the time of discharge to account for the increased volume from the developed site."*

**First Principles of Philippine Site Hydrology:**
1.  **Pre-Development Baseline:** You must know the *pre-development* storm rate. Development increases runoff volume (impermeable surfaces).
2.  **Concentration vs. Dispersion:** A basin concentrates flow to a single outlet. This increases velocity. If not managed, this causes erosion downstream.
3.  **Existing Patterns:** *"Site designers must study existing drainage patterns and pathways to identify opportunities that exist on the site."*
    *   **Philippine Context:** In Metro Manila and coastal areas, existing drainage paths are often clogged. Do not obliterate them. Enhance them.
4.  **Construction Phase:** *"To prevent water pollution, site designers must now address storm water management practices not only for the finished development but for the construction site as well."*
    *   **SWPPP:** You must implement a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan during construction.

### 4. Computation & Application Patterns
**The Rational Method (Simplified for ALE):**
While complex hydrologic models exist, the ALE often tests the **Rational Method** for small drainage areas.

$$Q = C \cdot i \cdot A$$

Where:
*   $Q$ = Peak discharge rate ($m^3/s$)
*   $C$ = Runoff coefficient (dimensionless)
*   $i$ = Intensity of rainfall ($mm/hr$)
*   $A$ = Drainage area ($ha$)

**Application Pattern:**
1.  **Identify the Watershed:** Determine the area contributing to the specific outlet.
2.  **Determine $C$:** Based on land use (e.g., parking lot vs. garden).
3.  **Select $i$:** Use Philippine Intensity-Duration-Frequency (IDF) curves from **PAGASA**.
4.  **Calculate $Q$:** Compare with the pre-development rate.
5.  **Design Mitigation:** If $Q_{developed} > Q_{pre-development}$, design a detention basin to offset the increase.

**Critical Design Decision:**
> *"Clearly the best design solution is to locate the basin with careful thought and analysis if it is to serve its purpose within a watershed."*
*   **Exam Tip:** Do not just place a basin anywhere. It must be located to manage the *entire* watershed, not just the site.

### 5. The Examiner's Mindset (Trap Analysis)
**What the ALE Examiner is Looking For:**
The examiner wants to see if you understand the **consequences** of poor drainage.

**Common Traps:**
1.  **Confusing Retention vs. Detention:**
    *   *Retention:* Water stays there (wet pond).
    *   *Detention:* Water is released slowly (dry pond).
    *   *Trap:* Choosing a retention basin in a flood-prone area where the goal is temporary storage.
2.  **Ignoring Construction Runoff:**
    *   *Trap:* Designing for the finished building but ignoring the construction phase where soil is disturbed and vegetation removed.
    *   *Consequence:* Increased velocity and volume of runoff during construction.
3.  **Obliterating Existing Paths:**
    *   *Trap:* Designing a new path that cuts across existing drainage swales.
    *   *Consequence:* Erosion and flooding downstream.
4.  **Shallow Water Tables:**
    *   *Trap:* Building foundations on sites with water tables as shallow as 20cm (8 inches).
    *   *Consequence:* Structural instability and flooding.

### 6. How It's Tested on the ALE (Question Patterns)
**Pattern 1: Scenario-Based Flood Risk**
*   **Question:** "A site is located in a flood-prone area with a shallow water table. Which of the following is the most appropriate site design strategy?"
*   **Options:**
    *   A) Build on the highest ground and use a detention basin.
    *   B) Build on the lowest ground to maximize infiltration.
    *   C) Ignore the water table and use deep foundations.
    *   D) Use a retention basin only.
*   **Correct Answer:** A.
*   **Reasoning:** High ground + Detention Basin = Safety.

**Pattern 2: Runoff Coefficient Selection**
*   **Question:** "Calculate the peak runoff for a 2-hectare parking lot (Concrete) in a 24-hour storm with an intensity of 50mm/hr."
*   **Calculation:** $Q = 0.9 \times 50 \times 2 = 90$ (units adjusted for PAGASA standards).
*   **Reasoning:** Tests knowledge of $C$ values and the Rational Method.

**Pattern 3: SWPPP Compliance**
*   **Question:** "Which of the following is NOT a requirement for a construction site?"
*   **Options:**
    *   A) SWPPP implementation.
    *   B) Erosion control measures.
    *   C) Permanent wet pond.
    *   D) Sediment traps.
*   **Correct Answer:** C.
*   **Reasoning:** A permanent wet pond is a *retention* basin, not a requirement for *construction* phase pollution prevention.

### 7. The "Philippine Architect" Persona
**The Voice of the Professional:**
> *"In the Philippines, we do not just build on land; we build on water. The soil is often laterite or volcanic ash, which can be unstable when saturated. We must respect the 'existing drainage patterns' that have evolved over centuries. A detention basin is not just a concrete hole; it is a tool to protect the community from the very storms PAGASA warns us about."*

**Key Takeaway:**
*   **Respect the Land:** Do not fight the water.
*   **Protect the Community:** Your design must not increase flood risk for neighbors.
*   **Sustainability:** Use green infrastructure (rain gardens, permeable pavements) to reduce runoff.

### 9. Key Terms & Definitions
*   **Runoff Coefficient ($C$):** Ratio of runoff to rainfall. Concrete: 0.9, Grass: 0.2.
*   **Time of Concentration ($T_c$):** Time for water to travel from the furthest point to the outlet.
*   **Detention Basin:** Temporary storage; releases water slowly to meet pre-development rates.
*   **Retention Basin:** Permanent storage (wet pond); used for water quality treatment.
*   **SWPPP:** Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan; required during construction.
*   **Pre-Developed Storm Rate:** The discharge rate before the site was built; the target for mitigation.
*   **Existing Drainage Patterns:** Natural or man-made paths water takes; must be identified and preserved.
*   **Flood Hazard Zones:** Areas prone to flooding; must be mapped and avoided if possible.
*   **Aquifer Recharge:** Areas where groundwater discharges to the surface; important for water quality.
*   **Shallow Water Table:** Groundwater level close to the surface (e.g., 20cm); requires special foundation design.
