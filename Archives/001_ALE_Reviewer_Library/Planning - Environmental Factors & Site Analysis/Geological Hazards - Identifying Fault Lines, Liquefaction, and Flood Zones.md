# Masterclass Study Module: Geological Hazards (Faults, Liquefaction, Floods)
**Target:** Philippine Architectural Licensure Examination (ALE)
**Subject:** Site Planning & Structural Safety
**Reference:** PD 1096, NSCP, RA 10121, PHILVOLCS Guidelines

---

### 1. The "Big Picture" & ALE Focus
**The Hook:** Imagine the Philippine archipelago as a giant, trembling chessboard. Every move of the tectonic plates is a potential checkmate for a building project. In the ALE, you are not just designing walls; you are designing survival.

**ALE Relevance:**
*   **Life Safety:** The primary mandate of the Architect under **RA 9266** (Philippine Architecture Act of 2004) is public welfare. Ignoring geological hazards violates this mandate.
*   **Site Suitability:** A building on a fault line or liquefaction zone is structurally unsound regardless of its design.
*   **Compliance:** You must adhere to **PD 1096** (National Building Code) and **RA 10121** (National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act).

**The Core Question:** *Can this site support a structure without catastrophic failure during a seismic event or flood?*

---

### 2. Narrative Logic & First Principles
**Why This Matters:**
The Philippines sits on the **Pacific Ring of Fire**. We are not just "in" a seismic zone; we are *on* the fault lines.
*   **Fault Lines:** These are fractures where rocks slip. If a fault is "active" (moved in the last 11,000 years), it is a red flag.
*   **Liquefaction:** Think of shaking a jar of water and sand. If the sand is saturated and shaken hard enough, it turns to liquid. The building sinks.
*   **Flood Zones:** Often secondary to geological shifts (subsidence) or hydrological changes.

**The Logic Chain:**
1.  **Geology:** Identify the soil type and fault proximity.
2.  **Hydrology:** Check water tables and drainage.
3.  **Design:** Adjust structural systems (e.g., base isolation) or refuse the site.
4.  **Regulation:** Ensure compliance with Local Government Unit (LGU) ordinances and PD 1096.

**Analogy:** Building on a fault line without mitigation is like building a house on a cliff edge during a typhoon. You can reinforce the walls, but the foundation is compromised.

---

### 3. Technical Specs & Philippine Standards
**Key Codes & Standards:**
*   **PD 1096 (National Building Code):**
    *   **Section 1001:** Seismic provisions.
    *   **Seismic Zones:** The Philippines is divided into zones (Zone 1 to Zone 4) based on seismic intensity.
    *   **Active Faults:** Must be avoided or protected by specific setbacks.
*   **NSCP (National Structural Code of Philippines):**
    *   **Seismic Design Category:** Determines the base shear force ($V$).
    *   **Soil Classification:** Uses SPT (Standard Penetration Test) values ($N$-value).
*   **PHILVOLCS (Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology):**
    *   Provides maps of active faults and seismic zones.
*   **RA 10121:** Mandates DRRM plans for all projects.

**Technical Thresholds:**
*   **Active Fault:** Defined as a fault that has moved in the last **11,000 years**.
*   **Liquefaction Susceptibility:**
    *   **Soil:** Saturated, loose, sandy soils (low SPT $N$-value).
    *   **Depth:** Groundwater table must be shallow (less than 2-3 meters below surface).
*   **Floodplain:** Areas with a recurrence interval of 100 years or less.

### 4. Computation & Design
**Seismic Force Calculation (Simplified):**
The base shear force ($V$) is calculated to ensure the structure can resist ground shaking.
$$ V = W \cdot I \cdot S_a / R $$
Where:
*   $V$ = Base shear force (Newtons)
*   $W$ = Seismic weight of the structure (Newtons)
*   $I$ = Importance factor (e.g., 1.2 for hospitals)
*   $S_a$ = Design spectral acceleration (based on seismic zone)
*   $R$ = Response modification factor (ductility)

**Liquefaction Factor of Safety ($FoS$):**
To prevent liquefaction, the Factor of Safety must be $\ge 1.0$ (often $\ge 1.5$ for critical structures).
$$ FoS = \frac{\text{Resisting Stress}}{\text{Driving Stress}} $$
*   **Resisting Stress:** Friction between soil particles.
*   **Driving Stress:** Pore water pressure generated by shaking.
*   **Mitigation:** If $FoS < 1.0$, you must improve the soil (compaction, stone columns) or relocate.

**Flood Continuity Equation:**
$$ Q = A \cdot V $$
Where:
*   $Q$ = Discharge (flow rate)
*   $A$ = Cross-sectional area of the river
*   $V$ = Velocity of the water
*   **Application:** If $Q$ exceeds the channel capacity, flooding occurs.

### 5. Examiner's Mindset & ALE Strategy
**The Traps:**
*   **Trap 1: Ignoring Topography.** A flat site might look safe, but it could be in a river basin or a landslide-prone slope.
*   **Trap 2: Assuming Soil Safety.** Just because the surface looks hard doesn't mean the subsurface isn't liquefiable.
*   **Trap 3: Overlooking Fault Setbacks.** PD 1096 requires specific setbacks from active faults.

**The Key Strategy:**
*   **Always Check the SIR (Site Investigation Report):** The ALE loves questions about the SIR. If the SIR is missing, the design is incomplete.
*   **Look for "Red Flags":**
    *   "Saturated sandy soil" $\rightarrow$ Liquefaction risk.
    *   "Proximity to Manila Fault" $\rightarrow$ Seismic risk.
    *   "Low-lying coastal area" $\rightarrow$ Flood risk.
*   **Mitigation:** If a hazard cannot be avoided, propose mitigation (e.g., deep foundations, base isolation, flood barriers).

**Sample Question Pattern:**
*   *Question:* "A site is located 50 meters from an active fault. The soil is clayey. Is this site suitable?"
*   *Answer:* **No.** Even if the soil is clayey (less liquefiable), the proximity to an active fault requires a specific setback (usually 100m or more, depending on fault width and slip rate).

### 6. Question Patterns & Sample Questions
**Pattern 1: Site Suitability**
*   *Question:* "Which of the following sites is most susceptible to liquefaction?"
    *   A. Dry, rocky hillside.
    *   B. Saturated, loose sandy soil near a river.
    *   C. Dense clay soil in a mountain.
    *   D. Concrete foundation on bedrock.
    *   *Correct Answer:* **B.** (Saturated, loose sand is the recipe for liquefaction).

**Pattern 2: Hazard Identification**
*   *Question:* "What is the primary hazard for a building located on the Manila Fault?"
    *   A. Landslide.
    *   B. Ground rupture.
    *   C. Flood.
    *   D. Wind.
    *   *Correct Answer:* **B.** (Ground rupture occurs directly on the fault line).

**Pattern 3: Regulatory Compliance**
*   *Question:* "According to PD 1096, what is the minimum setback from an active fault?"
    *   A. 10 meters.
    *   B. 50 meters.
    *   C. 100 meters (or more, depending on fault width).
    *   D. No setback required.
    *   *Correct Answer:* **C.** (Setbacks are critical for safety).

### 7. Active Recall & Memorization
**Mnemonic: "F.L.O.W."**
*   **F**ault: Check for active faults (PHILVOLCS maps).
*   **L**iquefaction: Check for saturated sand (SPT values).
*   **O**verland Flow: Check for flood zones (FEMA/LGU maps).
*   **W**etlands: Avoid building on wetlands (environmental hazard).

**Flashcard: PD 1096 Seismic Zones**
*   **Zone 1:** Low seismicity (e.g., parts of Mindanao).
*   **Zone 2:** Moderate seismicity (e.g., parts of Luzon).
*   **Zone 3:** High seismicity (e.g., Metro Manila, Cebu).
*   **Zone 4:** Very high seismicity (e.g., Cotabato, parts of Mindanao).
*   *Note:* Always verify the specific zone for your project location.

### 9. Key Terms & Glossary
*   **Liquefaction:** The process where saturated soil loses strength and behaves like a liquid during an earthquake.
*   **Active Fault:** A fault that has moved in the last 11,000 years.
*   **Seismic Zone:** A region classified by its seismic intensity (Zone 1-4).
*   **Floodplain:** An area of land adjacent to a river that is prone to flooding.
*   **SPT (Standard Penetration Test):** A test to determine soil density (N-value).
*   **Setback:** The required distance from a hazard (e.g., fault line) to build a structure.
