### 1. The "Big Picture" & ALE Focus
**The Hook:** Imagine a building not as a static box, but as a vessel navigating the currents of the tropics. In the Philippines, the sun and wind are not merely weather conditions; they are active agents that dictate the thermal comfort and energy efficiency of a structure.

**ALE Relevance:**
*   **Building Technology:** Understanding how to mitigate heat gain (overheating) while maximizing daylighting.
*   **Site Planning:** Orienting the building to catch prevailing winds for natural ventilation.
*   **Sustainable Design:** Reducing reliance on HVAC systems through passive strategies (PD 1096 compliance).
*   **Exam Weight:** High priority in Environmental Control and Site Planning questions.

**The Core Challenge:** How do we balance the need for winter solar heat gain (rare in the tropics but relevant for high-altitude areas like Baguio) against the intense summer solar load and monsoon winds?

---

### 2. Narrative Logic & First Principles
**Why Orientation Matters:**
The text provided by Szokolay notes that "Solar control utilizes beneficial sunshine for passive heating and for daylighting and minimizes liabilities of overheating." In the Philippine context, this translates to:
1.  **Solar Access:** We generally want to *avoid* direct summer sun (June-August) on living spaces to prevent overheating.
2.  **Wind Capture:** We want to *invite* the prevailing winds (Amihan/Habagat) to cool the building.

**Historical Context:**
The provided text references a "Solar and Astronomical Observatory. Jaipur, India. circa 1710." While ancient, the principle remains: humans have always tracked the sun. Traditional Filipino houses (*Bahay Kubo*) were oriented to catch the breeze. Modern high-rises in Metro Manila must replicate this logic using overhangs and fenestration.

**The Strategy:**
*   **Winter (Dec-Feb):** The sun is lower. We want it to enter.
*   **Summer (Jun-Aug):** The sun is higher. We want to block it.
*   **Monsoon:**
    *   **Amihan (Dec-Mar):** Northeast winds. Cool.
    *   **Habagat (Jun-Sep):** Southwest winds. Hot and humid.

### 3. Technical Specifications & Spatial Data
**Wind Patterns (Philippine Monsoon):**
*   **Amihan:** Prevails from **December to March**. Direction: **Northeast**.
    *   *Effect:* Cooler, drier air. Ideal for ventilation.
    *   *Design:* Openings on the Northeast side.
*   **Habagat:** Prevails from **June to September**. Direction: **Southwest**.
    *   *Effect:* Hot, humid, often brings typhoons.
    *   *Design:* Shading devices required. Openings on the Southwest side should be minimized or protected.

**Solar Geometry (Metro Manila Approx. 14.5°N):**
*   **Winter Solstice (Dec 21):** Sun is lowest.
    *   *Goal:* Maximize penetration.
    *   *Shading:* Minimal overhang needed.
*   **Summer Solstice (Jun 21):** Sun is highest.
    *   *Goal:* Minimize penetration.
    *   *Shading:* Deep horizontal overhangs required.

**Units:**
*   **Distance:** Meters (m).
*   **Angle:** Degrees (°).
*   **Temperature:** Celsius (°C).
*   **Wind Speed:** Meters per second (m/s) or km/h.

### 4. Computation & Formulas
**Solar Shading Device Calculation:**
To design a horizontal overhang (eaves) that blocks the summer sun but lets in the winter sun:

$$ L = H \times \cot(\alpha) $$

*   **$L$**: Length of the overhang (meters).
*   **$H$**: Height of the window (meters).
*   **$\alpha$**: Solar altitude angle.

*   **For Summer (June):** Use the maximum solar altitude angle for the latitude.
*   **For Winter (Dec):** Use the minimum solar altitude angle.

**Wind Speed Adjustment:**
$$ V = V_{base} \times K $$
*   **$V_{base}$**: Base wind speed from maps (m/s).
*   **$K$**: Corridor factor (openings vs. solid walls).

**Solar Angle (Simplified for ALE):**
$$ \sin(\delta) $$
*   **$\delta$**: Solar declination.
*   *Note:* For ALE, focus on the *result* (shading needed) rather than complex trigonometry unless specified.

### 5. Examiner's Mindset & Question Patterns
**The "Trap" Questions:**
*   **Trap 1:** "Which orientation is best for a house in Cebu?"
    *   *Wrong Answer:* "North" (In the tropics, North is often the hottest side due to the sun's path).
    *   *Correct Logic:* South-facing windows need heavy shading for summer. North-facing windows get less direct sun but need protection from rain.
*   **Trap 2:** "Ignoring Habagat."
    *   *Examiner Thought:* If you design a house with large openings on the Southwest side without shading, you fail the "Thermal Comfort" criteria.
*   **Trap 3:** "Overlooking Wind Corridors."
    *   *Examiner Thought:* A building that blocks the Amihan path will fail the "Ventilation" requirement of PD 1096.

**Key Insight:** The examiner wants to see if you understand that **South-facing windows in the tropics require shading**, unlike in temperate zones where North is preferred for passive solar.

### 6. Question Patterns (ALE Style)
**Multiple Choice:**
*   "Which orientation minimizes cooling load in Metro Manila?"
    *   A) East-facing
    *   B) South-facing (with shading)
    *   C) West-facing
    *   D) North-facing
    *   *Answer:* **B** (With proper shading, South is viable; West is the worst due to afternoon heat).

**Short Answer:**
*   "Explain the role of Amihan in ventilation."
    *   *Answer:* "The Amihan (Northeast wind) provides cool, dry air during the dry season. Designing openings on the Northeast side allows this air to enter and cross-ventilate the building, reducing the need for mechanical cooling."

**Scenario:**
*   "Designing a house in Cebu vs. Manila."
    *   *Answer:* "Cebu is closer to the equator (higher solar angles). Manila is slightly north. Both require shading for the Habagat, but Cebu may need deeper overhangs due to higher sun angles."
