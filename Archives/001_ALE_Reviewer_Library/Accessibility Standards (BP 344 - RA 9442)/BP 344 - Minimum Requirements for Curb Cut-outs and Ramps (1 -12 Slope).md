## Section 1: The Big Picture – Why This Matters
Welcome to the **Accessibility Standards Module**. In the Philippine Board Exam, accessibility is not just a "nice-to-have" detail; it is a **legal requirement** and a **measure of social responsibility**.

*   **The Law:** While **BP 344** (Building Code of the Philippines) sets the general framework, specific accessibility mandates are often reinforced by **RA 7279 (Magna Carta for Disabled Persons)** and **PD 1096 (National Building Code)**.
*   **The Reality:** A building is only complete when it is usable by everyone. A wheelchair user cannot enter a building with a 1:12 slope ramp if the width is less than 900mm.
*   **The Exam Strategy:** The Board loves to test your ability to convert **imperial to metric** (e.g., 4ft to 1200mm) and apply **safety factors** (e.g., landing widths). This module bridges the gap between US standards (ADA) and Philippine metric practice.

## Section 4: Computation – The Math Behind the Ramp
The Board Exam often asks you to calculate the **length of a ramp** based on a given height.

**Formula:**
$$ \text{Run (Length)} = \text{Rise (Height)} \times 12 $$
*(Since the slope is 1:12, for every 1 unit of height, you need 12 units of horizontal length)*

**Example Problem:**
*   **Scenario:** A building entrance has a step height of **150mm**.
*   **Question:** What is the minimum horizontal length of the ramp required?
*   **Solution:**
    1.  Identify the Rise: $150\text{mm}$.
    2.  Apply the Slope: $150\text{mm} \times 12$.
    3.  Calculate: $1,800\text{mm}$ (or $1.80\text{m}$).
*   **Answer:** The ramp must be at least **1.80m** long.

**Landing Calculation:**
*   If the ramp length exceeds **9m**, you must add a landing every **9m**.
*   If the ramp has a turn, you must add a landing at the turn.

## Section 5: Examiner's Mindset – What the Board Looks For
When you sit for the licensure exam, the reviewers are looking for three things:

1.  **Metric Consistency:** Do not mix feet and meters. If the question gives you meters, answer in meters. (e.g., $900\text{mm} = 0.90\text{m}$).
2.  **Safety First:** If a choice between 1:10 and 1:12 is given for a ramp, choose **1:12**. Steeper is dangerous.
3.  **Minimums vs. Preferreds:** The Board tests the **Minimums**.
    *   *Preferred:* 1200mm width.
    *   *Minimum:* 900mm width.
    *   *Exam Answer:* Always choose the **Minimum** unless the question asks for the "Preferred" or "Ideal" condition.

**Common Trap:**
*   *Question:* "What is the minimum width for a ramp?"
*   *Distractor:* 1200mm (This is for two people).
*   *Correct Answer:* 900mm (This is for one person).

## Section 6: Question Patterns – Typical MCQs
Here are the types of questions you will encounter:

**Pattern A: Slope Conversion**
*   *Q:* "A ramp has a rise of 200mm. What is the minimum run?"
*   *A:* $200 \times 12 = 2,400\text{mm}$ (2.40m).

**Pattern B: Width Selection**
*   *Q:* "A single wheelchair user needs to enter a building. What is the minimum ramp width?"
*   *A:* 900mm.

**Pattern C: Landing Placement**
*   *Q:* "A ramp is 10m long. How many landings are required?"
*   *A:* One landing (since 10m > 9m).

**Pattern D: Door Width**
*   *Q:* "What is the minimum clear width for an accessible door?"
*   *A:* 800mm.

## Section 8: Web of Knowledge – Connecting the Dots
*   **BP 344:** The Building Code that mandates these standards.
*   **RA 7279:** The law that gives disabled persons the right to access.
*   **PD 1096:** The National Building Code that defines structural safety.
*   **ADA:** US standards that often serve as a reference for metric conversions (e.g., 4ft = 1200mm).
*   **Universal Design:** The philosophy that buildings should be usable by all people, to the greatest extent possible, without the need for adaptation.

---

## Section 9: Key Terms & Glossary
*   **Curb Cut-out:** A ramp at the edge of a sidewalk to allow wheelchair access.
*   **Flared Side Slope:** The slope of the ground on the sides of the ramp (usually 1:10).
*   **Clear Width:** The unobstructed width of a ramp or door.
*   **Landing:** A flat area at the top, bottom, or turn of a ramp.
*   **Rise:** The vertical height of a step or ramp.
*   **Run:** The horizontal length of a ramp.
*   **Slope Ratio:** The relationship between rise and run (e.g., 1:12).
