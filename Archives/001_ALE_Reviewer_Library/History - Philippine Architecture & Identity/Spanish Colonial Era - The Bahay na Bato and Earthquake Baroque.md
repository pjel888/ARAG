### 2. Narrative Logic & First Principles
**Why Stone Base, Wood Roof?**
The Spanish Colonial architecture in the Philippines was a hybrid.
*   **The Stone Base (Bahay na Bato):** Stone was used for the ground floor to resist moisture, pests, and provide a heavy, stable foundation. It acts as a counterweight.
*   **The Wood Upper Floor:** Wood was used for the upper floor because it is lighter and flexible. In an earthquake, the stone base absorbs the shock, while the wood frame sways without collapsing.
*   **The Sawali Ceiling:** The woven nipa palm ceiling allows heat to escape and provides a lightweight roof that reduces seismic mass.

**The Earthquake Baroque Logic:**
*   **Context:** The 1755 Lisbon earthquake destroyed many stone churches.
*   **Response:** Architects adopted a "heavy" facade (Baroque) for visual grandeur but kept the structural plan simple and robust.
*   **Philippine Adaptation:** In Rizal and Iloilo, the heavy Baroque facade was applied to stone structures, but the structural logic remained rooted in the *Bahay na Bato* tradition of using flexible materials for the upper levels.

### 3. Technical Specifications & Spatial Data
**Table 1: Material Properties & Seismic Behavior**

| Component | Material | Function | Seismic Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ground Floor** | Stone / Brick | Foundation, Storage, Livestock | High Mass, High Compression Strength |
| **Upper Floor** | Wood / Bamboo | Living Quarters | High Flexibility, Low Mass |
| **Roof** | Nipa / Thatch | Weather Protection | Lightweight, Reduces Seismic Force |
| **Columns** | Stone / Wood | Vertical Support | Stone = Rigid; Wood = Flexible |

**Table 2: Key Dimensions (Typical Bahay na Bato)**
*   **Height:** 2.5m - 3.5m (Ground Floor)
*   **Elevation:** 1.5m - 2.0m (Above Ground)
*   **Floor Area:** 40m² - 100m² (Typical Family Unit)

**Formula for Seismic Force (Simplified):**
$$F = m \cdot a$$
Where:
*   $F$ = Seismic Force (Newtons)
*   $m$ = Mass of the structure (kg)
*   $a$ = Ground acceleration (m/s²)

*Note: Reducing mass ($m$) by using wood for the upper floor reduces $F$.*

### 4. Computation & Application Patterns
**Problem:** Calculate the reduction in seismic force if a Bahay na Bato replaces a full stone house.
**Given:**
*   Mass of full stone house ($m_{stone}$) = 50,000 kg
*   Mass of Bahay na Bato ($m_{wood}$) = 25,000 kg (Wood is lighter)
*   Ground acceleration ($a$) = 0.5 m/s² (Moderate quake)

**Solution:**
1.  **Force of Stone House:** $F_{stone} = 50,000 \cdot 0.5 = 25,000 \text{ N}$
2.  **Force of Bahay na Bato:** $F_{wood} = 25,000 \cdot 0.5 = 12,500 \text{ N}$
3.  **Reduction:** $25,000 - 12,500 = 12,500 \text{ N}$

**Conclusion:** The Bahay na Bato design reduces seismic force by 50% compared to a full stone structure. This is why it survived the 19th-century earthquakes in the Philippines.

### 5. Common Traps & Misconceptions
**Trap 1: Confusing "Spanish Gothic" with "Spanish Colonial"**
*   **Trap:** Thinking that "Spanish Gothic" (pointed arches, ribbed vaults) is the same as "Bahay na Bato".
*   **Reality:** Spanish Gothic is a European style (e.g., Seville). The Bahay na Bato is a *Philippine* adaptation. The text mentions "Spanish Gothic" as a distractor.
*   **Correction:** The Bahay na Bato is *not* Gothic. It is a vernacular style adapted by Spanish colonizers.

**Trap 2: Confusing "Saracen" with "Moorish"**
*   **Trap:** Thinking "Saracen" refers to Islamic architecture in the Philippines.
*   **Reality:** "Saracen" is often used loosely to describe Moorish influence (e.g., in Spain). In the Philippines, the Spanish Colonial style is distinct from Islamic architecture.
*   **Correction:** The Bahay na Bato is *not* Saracen. It is a hybrid of Spanish and Filipino.

**Trap 3: Confusing "Latin American Modernism" with "Spanish Colonial"**
*   **Trap:** Thinking that "Latin American Modernism" (e.g., Le Corbusier) is the same as "Bahay na Bato".
*   **Reality:** Latin American Modernism is a 20th-century style. The Bahay na Bato is 16th-19th century.
*   **Correction:** The text mentions "Latin American Modernism" as a contrast. The Bahay na Bato is *not* Modernism.
