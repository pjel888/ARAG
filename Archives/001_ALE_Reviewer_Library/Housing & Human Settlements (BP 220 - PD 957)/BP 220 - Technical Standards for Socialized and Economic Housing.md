# MASTERCLASS MODULE: BP 220: Technical Standards for Economic and Socialized Housing

## 1. The "Big Picture" & ALE Focus
**The Hook:** Imagine a family earning ₱15,000 a month. If they spend 30% of that on rent or mortgage, they are living in the "affordable" zone defined by the state. If they spend more, they are in the "market" zone. **BP 220** is the legal framework that ensures the government and developers don't exploit this vulnerable group.

**Why This Matters for the ALE:**
The Board does not just test your ability to draw a house; they test your ability to **regulate**.
*   **Competency:** You must distinguish between *Economic Housing* (Shell) and *Socialized Housing* (Complete).
*   **Legal Compliance:** You must know that BP 220 overrides general market standards for these specific housing types.
*   **Social Responsibility:** You are an architect of dignity. Your designs must ensure that low-income earners get a home that is safe, sanitary, and affordable, not just a "shell" that collapses.

**ALE Relevance:**
*   **High Priority:** This is a "Must-Know" topic for the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) Board Exams.
*   **Trap:** Confusing the *National Building Code (PD 1096)* setbacks with *BP 220* specific allowances.
*   **Key Concept:** The "Level of Completion" is the single most important variable in BP 220 questions.

## 2. Narrative Logic & First Principles
**The "Why" Behind the Law:**
BP 220 was born from the realization that the private market would not voluntarily build for the poor. The government had to intervene to ensure that housing for the poor was not just "shacks" but "homes."

**The Logic of "Shell" vs. "Complete":**
*   **Economic Housing (Shell):** Why "Shell"? Because the government wants to encourage private participation. The developer builds the structure (walls, roof, floors) but leaves the finishing (paint, fixtures, appliances) to the buyer. This reduces the developer's cost, making the unit affordable.
*   **Socialized Housing (Complete):** Why "Complete"? Because the government is directly providing housing for the *very* poor (often with government subsidies). The state ensures that the unit is fully habitable upon delivery.

**The Logic of "30%":**
*   **Affordability:** The law defines "affordable" as spending no more than 30% of the gross family income on housing. This is a global standard (World Bank) adopted by the Philippines.
*   **The Math:** If a family earns ₱30,000/month, their housing cost must be ≤ ₱9,000/month.
*   **The Trap:** If the housing cost is 40%, it is *not* Economic or Socialized Housing. It is Market Housing.

**The Authority:**
*   **HLURB (Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board):** Now known as the **Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development (DHSUD)**. They are the enforcers of BP 220.
*   **PD 957:** The *Subdivision and Condominium Buyers' Protective Decree*. BP 220 is the *Implementing Rules and Regulations* (IRR) for housing standards under PD 957.

## 3. Technical Standards & Specifications (BP 220)
**The "Must-Know" Table:**

| Feature | Economic Housing (ESH) | Socialized Housing (SH) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level of Completion** | **Shell** (Structure only) | **Complete** (Fully habitable) |
| **Affordability** | ≤ 30% of Gross Family Income | ≤ 30% of Gross Family Income |
| **Authority** | HLURB / DHSUD | HLURB / DHSUD |
| **Setbacks** | Refer to **PD 1096 (NBC)** | Refer to **PD 1096 (NBC)** |
| **Lot Size** | Follows **PD 1096** (e.g., 120 sqm for 1 story) | Follows **PD 1096** |
| **Finishing** | Buyer completes (paint, fixtures) | Developer completes |
| **Purpose** | Private sector participation | Government/NGO participation |

**Key Distinction:**
*   **Economic Housing:** The developer builds the "Shell". The buyer finishes it.
*   **Socialized Housing:** The developer builds the "Complete" unit. The buyer moves in.

**The "Shell" Definition:**
*   **Structure:** Walls, roof, floors, windows, doors.
*   **Excluded:** Paint, electrical fixtures, plumbing fixtures, appliances, landscaping.
*   **Why?** To reduce the initial cost for the buyer.

**The "Complete" Definition:**
*   **Structure:** All of the above.
*   **Included:** Paint, electrical fixtures, plumbing fixtures, appliances, landscaping.
*   **Why?** To ensure immediate habitability for the very poor.

## 4. Computation & Application
**The "30% Rule" Example:**
*   **Family Income:** ₱30,000/month.
*   **Maximum Housing Cost:** 30% of ₱30,000 = **₱9,000/month**.
*   **Scenario:** A developer offers a unit with a monthly amortization of ₱10,000.
*   **Conclusion:** This is **NOT** Economic or Socialized Housing. It is Market Housing.

**The "Level of Completion" Example:**
*   **Scenario:** A developer wants to build a 1-story house for a family earning ₱15,000/month.
*   **Requirement:** The unit must be Economic Housing (Shell).
*   **Action:** The developer must ensure the unit is delivered as a "Shell" (no paint, no fixtures).
*   **Trap:** If the developer delivers a "Complete" unit, the cost will exceed the 30% affordability threshold.

**The "Setback" Example:**
*   **Scenario:** A developer wants to build a 1-story Economic Housing unit on a 120 sqm lot.
*   **Requirement:** The setbacks must follow **PD 1096 (NBC)**.
*   **Action:** The developer must check the NBC setbacks (e.g., 3m front, 1.5m side, etc.).
*   **Trap:** BP 220 does *not* override the NBC setbacks. It only overrides the *Level of Completion*.

## 5. Examiner Mindset & Common Traps
**The "Trap" Questions:**
*   **Trap 1:** "What is the setback for Economic Housing?"
    *   *Answer:* Refer to **PD 1096 (NBC)**. BP 220 does not set specific setbacks; it refers to the NBC.
*   **Trap 2:** "What is the Level of Completion for Socialized Housing?"
    *   *Answer:* **Complete**.
*   **Trap 3:** "What is the Level of Completion for Economic Housing?"
    *   *Answer:* **Shell**.
*   **Trap 4:** "Who regulates Economic Housing?"
    *   *Answer:* **HLURB (DHSUD)**.

**The "Trap" Scenarios:**
*   **Scenario:** A developer builds a "Complete" unit for a family earning ₱15,000/month.
    *   *Question:* Is this Economic Housing?
    *   *Answer:* **No**. It is Market Housing. The cost will exceed the 30% affordability threshold.
*   **Scenario:** A developer builds a "Shell" unit for a family earning ₱15,000/month.
    *   *Question:* Is this Economic Housing?
    *   *Answer:* **Yes**. The cost is within the 30% affordability threshold.

**The "Trap" Math:**
*   **Scenario:** A family earns ₱30,000/month.
    *   *Question:* What is the maximum housing cost?
    *   *Answer:* **₱9,000/month**.
*   **Scenario:** A family earns ₱50,000/month.
    *   *Question:* What is the maximum housing cost?
    *   *Answer:* **₱15,000/month**.
