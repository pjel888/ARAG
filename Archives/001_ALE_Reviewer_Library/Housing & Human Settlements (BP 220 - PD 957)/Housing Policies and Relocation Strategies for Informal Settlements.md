# Masterclass Module: Housing Policies and Relocation Strategies for Informal Settlements

## 1. The "Big Picture" & ALE Focus
**The Hook:** Imagine a city as a living organism. Formal housing is the skeleton; informal settlements are the immune system's first response to infection—rapid, organic, and often chaotic. In the Philippine context, the **Architectural Licensure Exam (ALE)** does not just test your ability to draw a house; it tests your ability to understand *where* that house belongs and *why* it must be there.

**ALE Competency Focus:**
*   **Urban Planning:** Understanding the hierarchy of needs (Water > Movement > Drainage).
*   **Legal Compliance:** Mastery of **RA 7279 (LUPA)** and **PD 1096 (NBC)**.
*   **Social Responsibility:** Recognizing that "Relocation" is not just moving a house; it is moving a community's livelihood and identity.

**The Core Question:** *How do we balance the legal requirement for safety (hazard-prone areas) with the social reality of community identity and self-selection?*

---

## 2. Narrative Logic & First Principles (The "Why")
**The Intent:**
The primary intent of housing policy in the Philippines is **Tenure Security** and **Safety**. However, the *method* of achieving this has evolved from forced relocation to **In-City Resettlement** and **In-Situ Upgrading**.

**The Logic Chain:**
1.  **Problem:** Informal settlements often exist in hazard-prone areas (flood plains, steep slopes).
2.  **Policy:** RA 7279 mandates that relocation sites must be **safe** and **affordable**.
3.  **Reality:** Informal settlers often possess "identity" in their environment (self-built infrastructure, community spaces).
4.  **Solution:** Relocation strategies must respect **autonomous growth** and **continuous development** (Self-Selection Process).

**The "Self-Selection" Principle:**
As noted in the provided text, informal settlements demonstrate that users can control their environment.
*   **Autonomous Growth:** The community builds incrementally.
*   **Continuous Development:** Infrastructure improves over time.
*   **ALE Insight:** When designing for relocation, do not impose rigid formal standards that destroy this organic growth. Allow for "Basic Needs" first, then "Identity" later.

## 3. Technical Specifications & Spatial Data (The "What")
**The Hierarchy of Basic Needs:**
According to the provided text and RA 7279, the provision of basic needs follows a descending order of priority. This is crucial for ALE questions regarding site planning.

| Priority | Basic Need | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Water** | Access to potable water (essential for life). |
| **2** | **Movement & Circulation** | Access to roads, pathways, and public transport. |
| **3** | **Storm Drainage** | Prevention of flooding and waterlogging. |
| **4** | **Solid & Liquid Waste Disposal** | Sanitation and hygiene. |
| **5** | **Park/Playground** | Recreational space (often deferred). |
| **6** | **Power** | Electricity (often considered a luxury in basic shelter). |

**Relocation Site Criteria (RA 7279):**
*   **Location:** Outside potential hazard-prone areas (flood, fire, landslide).
*   **Affordability:** Based on the financial capability of beneficiaries.
*   **Participation:** Must involve LGUs, CSOs, NGOs, and communities.

**Minimum Lot Sizes (BP 220 & PD 1096):**
*   **Economic Housing:** Minimum lot area is often reduced compared to standard residential zones to ensure affordability.
*   **Setbacks:** Must comply with PD 1096 (National Building Code) unless specific exemptions are granted for informal settlements.

## 4. Computation & Application Patterns (The "How")
**Problem:**
Calculate the **Affordable Cost** of a housing project for a specific beneficiary group, considering the **Basic Needs Hierarchy**.

**Given:**
*   Target Market: Low-income families (Program Beneficiaries).
*   Land Cost: ₱50,000 per lot.
*   Construction Cost (Basic): ₱150,000 per unit.
*   Subsidy Available: ₱200,000 per unit.
*   **Constraint:** The site must be outside hazard-prone areas (RA 7279).

**Solution:**
1.  **Determine Total Cost:**
    $$ \text{Total Cost} = \text{Land Cost} + \text{Construction Cost} $$
    $$ \text{Total Cost} = 50,000 + 150,000 = 200,000 $$
2.  **Apply Subsidy:**
    $$ \text{Affordable Cost} = \text{Total Cost} - \text{Subsidy} $$
    $$ \text{Affordable Cost} = 200,000 - 200,000 = 0 $$
    *(Note: In reality, the subsidy covers the gap, but the ALE tests your ability to identify if the cost is within the "Affordable" range defined by the government.)*

3.  **Check Hazard Compliance:**
    *   Is the site in a flood plain? **No.** (If Yes, relocation is mandatory).
    *   Is the site in a steep slope? **No.** (If Yes, relocation is mandatory).

**ALE Tip:**
Always check if the computed cost exceeds the **Affordable Housing** threshold. If it does, the project is not compliant with RA 7279.

## 5. Examiner Mindset & Traps (The "Gotchas")
**The Trap:**
*   **Ignoring Hazard-Prone Areas:** RA 7279 Section 20.4 explicitly states that relocation sites must be outside hazard-prone areas. If you design a house in a flood plain, you fail.
*   **Assuming All Need Relocation:** Not all informal settlers need relocation. Some prefer **In-Situ Upgrading**. The ALE tests your ability to distinguish between "Relocation" and "Upgrading."
*   **Ignoring Identity:** The text mentions that informal settlements have "identity" in their environment. Forcing a rigid formal layout without considering community participation is a common error.

**The "Self-Selection" Trap:**
*   **Trap:** Assuming the government decides the location.
*   **Reality:** The **Self-Selection Process** allows communities to choose their preferred location (within safety limits). Ignoring this violates the principle of **Community Participation**.

## 6. Question Patterns & ALE Strategy
**Pattern 1: "Which law governs..."**
*   **Question:** "Which law mandates that relocation sites must be outside hazard-prone areas?"
*   **Answer:** **RA 7279 (LUPA)**.
*   **Strategy:** Memorize the acronyms. LUPA = Land Use Planning Act.

**Pattern 2: "What is the minimum setback for..."**
*   **Question:** "What is the minimum setback for a house in a hazard-prone area?"
*   **Answer:** **Zero** (if the area is deemed safe after upgrading) or **Relocation** (if unsafe).
*   **Strategy:** Always default to **Relocation** if the area is hazard-prone.

**Pattern 3: "Identify the hazard-prone area..."**
*   **Question:** "Which of the following is NOT a hazard-prone area?"
*   **Answer:** A site with stable soil and no flood history.
*   **Strategy:** Look for keywords like "flood," "fire," "landslide," "steep slope."

## 8. Web of Knowledge (Connections)
*   **RA 7279 (LUPA)** $\rightarrow$ **RA 9266 (Housing Act of 2004)** $\rightarrow$ **PD 1096 (NBC)**.
    *   *Connection:* RA 7279 focuses on *land use* and *relocation*. RA 9266 focuses on *housing production*. PD 1096 focuses on *building safety*.
*   **Relocation** $\rightarrow$ **Community Participation** $\rightarrow$ **Tenure Security**.
    *   *Connection:* Relocation without community participation leads to lack of tenure security.
*   **In-City Resettlement** $\rightarrow$ **Out-of-City Resettlement**.
    *   *Connection:* In-City is preferred to maintain livelihoods. Out-of-City is used when In-City is not feasible.

---

## 9. Key Terms & Vocabulary
*   **In-City Resettlement:** Relocation within the city limits to maintain access to livelihoods.
*   **Hazard-Prone Area:** Areas susceptible to natural disasters (flood, fire, landslide).
*   **Self-Selection:** The process where communities choose their preferred relocation site.
*   **Tenure Security:** The assurance that a person or group has the right to occupy a property.
*   **Affordability:** The ability of the beneficiary to pay for the housing without compromising other basic needs.

---

## 10. Final Summary & ALE Strategy
**The Takeaway:**
The ALE is not just about drawing lines; it is about understanding the **human context** of housing.
*   **Safety First:** Always prioritize hazard-prone areas.
*   **Community Second:** Always prioritize community participation and self-selection.
*   **Needs Third:** Always prioritize the hierarchy of basic needs (Water > Movement > Drainage).

**Final Advice:**
When answering ALE questions, always ask: *"Does this solution respect the rights of the informal settler and ensure their safety?"* If the answer is no, it is likely incorrect.
