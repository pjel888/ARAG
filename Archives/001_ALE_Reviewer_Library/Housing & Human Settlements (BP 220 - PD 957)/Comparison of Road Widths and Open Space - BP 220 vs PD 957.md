# Masterclass Module: Comparison of Road Widths and Open Space (BP 220 vs. PD 957)

## Part 1: Big Picture — The "Street Width Dilemma"

Imagine you are a developer in the Philippines. You have a 10,000 sqm lot. You want to build a residential subdivision. You need to decide:
1.  **How wide should the roads be?** (To allow fire trucks, cars, and pedestrians).
2.  **How much open space do I need?** (To comply with the law and ensure livability).

You have two main laws to consider:
*   **BP 220 (1972):** The National Building Code. It sets the *general* standards for setbacks and open space.
*   **PD 957 (1977):** The Subdivision and Condominium Buyers' Protective Decree. It sets the *specific* standards for subdivisions (road widths, drainage, etc.).

**The Global Context (Provided Text):**
The provided text discusses USA standards (NAHB, 1988) where streets are often narrower (e.g., 6.00m vs. 7.20m) but still meet safety standards. It highlights that narrower streets can be safe *if* designed correctly.
*   **Key Insight:** While the USA might allow narrower streets (6.00m) in some contexts, Philippine law (PD 957) mandates specific widths (4m for residential, 6m for commercial) to ensure *emergency access* and *parking*.
*   **Why it matters:** The provided text argues against making streets too narrow (emergency access, parking). This aligns with PD 957, which mandates wider roads (6m for main roads) to ensure fire trucks can pass.

**The Core Question:**
*   **BP 220:** Focuses on the *building* (setbacks, open space).
*   **PD 957:** Focuses on the *subdivision* (road widths, infrastructure).
*   **Conflict:** Sometimes local ordinances differ. The exam often asks about the *minimum* road width for a specific zone (e.g., residential vs. commercial).

## Part 4: Computation — Open Space & Road Width

Let's compute the open space and road width requirements based on lot size.

### 1. Open Space Computation (BP 220)
*   **Formula:**
    $$ \text{Open Space} = \text{Lot Area} \times \text{Open Space Ratio} $$
*   **Example:**
    *   **Lot Area:** 10,000 sqm
    *   **Open Space Ratio:** 15% (0.15)
    *   **Calculation:**
        $$ \text{Open Space} = 10,000 \times 0.15 = 1,500 \text{ sqm} $$
    *   **Result:** Juan needs 1,500 sqm of open space.

### 2. Road Width Computation (PD 957)
*   **Formula:**
    $$ \text{Road Width} = \text{Minimum Required Width} $$
*   **Example:**
    *   **Zone:** Residential
    *   **Minimum Width:** 4m
    *   **Result:** Juan needs a 4m wide road.

### 3. Combined Computation (BP 220 + PD 957)
*   **Scenario:** Juan has a 10,000 sqm lot. He wants to build a residential subdivision.
*   **Open Space:** 1,500 sqm (15% of 10,000 sqm).
*   **Road Width:** 4m (Residential) or 6m (Commercial).
*   **Total Area:**
    $$ \text{Total Area} = \text{Lot Area} - \text{Open Space} - \text{Road Width} $$
    $$ \text{Total Area} = 10,000 - 1,500 - \text{Road Width} $$
    $$ \text{Total Area} = 8,500 - \text{Road Width} $$
*   **Result:** Juan has 8,500 sqm for buildings and other uses.
