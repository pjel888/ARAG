# MASTERCLASS MODULE: Residential Clearances (Kitchen & Bedroom)

### 1. The Big Picture
**Topic:** Residential Clearances: Kitchen Work Triangles and Bedroom Layouts  
**Context:** Philippine Architectural Licensure Examination (ALE)  
**Core Concept:** The efficiency of a kitchen is defined by the **Work Triangle**, while the safety of a bedroom is defined by **Egress and Ventilation**.  
**Why it Matters:** For the ALE, you must demonstrate that a design is not just aesthetic but functional and code-compliant. A kitchen that interrupts the work flow wastes energy and increases accident risk. A bedroom that lacks proper clearance violates fire safety codes (PD 1096).  
**Philippine Standard:** While international texts (like the Michigan State Housing Development Authority source provided) use feet/inches, Philippine practice (NBC 2015/2018) requires metric precision. You must be able to convert and apply both.

### 2. Narrative Logic
**The Flow of Work (Kitchen):**  
Cooking is a sequence: Storage $\rightarrow$ Prep $\rightarrow$ Cooking $\rightarrow$ Serving $\rightarrow$ Cleaning.  
*   **Logic:** If a door or passageway cuts through the triangle, the cook must step out of the "zone" every time they grab an item. This breaks the "continuity of kitchen activities."  
*   **The Rule:** The triangle should not be broken by full-height fitments (tall cabinets) or passageways.  
*   **The Bedroom Logic:** A bedroom is a sanctuary. Layouts must ensure that in an emergency (fire), the path to the exit is clear. No furniture should block the door swing or the path to the window.

**The Flow of People (Bedroom):**  
*   **Logic:** Bedrooms must accommodate furniture (bed, wardrobe, nightstand) without blocking the door.  
*   **The Rule:** Minimum clear width for a door is typically 900mm (36 inches) for accessibility.  
*   **The Connection:** Both spaces require "clearance" to ensure safety and efficiency.

### 3. Technical Specs (The Numbers)
**Kitchen Work Triangle Dimensions:**
*   **Perimeter (Sum of Sides):** $3660\text{ mm} \le \text{Total Length} \le 6705\text{ mm}$ (Approx. $12' \text{ to } 22'$).  
    *   *Note:* The provided text suggests $5500\text{--}6000\text{ mm}$ for normal families. The ALE often accepts the broader range ($12' \text{--} 22'$) as the standard.
*   **Sink to Range:** Maximum $1800\text{ mm}$ ($60"$).  
    *   *Reason:* Prevents splashing and allows safe movement.
*   **Through Circulation:** Avoid in work area.  
    *   *Reason:* Prevents accidents (hot oil, sharp knives).
*   **Clearances:**
    *   **Appliances:** $1200\text{ mm}$ ($48"$) working space.
    *   **Refrigerator Side:** $1500\text{ mm}$ ($60"$) for side-by-side or double-door units.
*   **Bedroom Minimums (NBC 2015/2018):**
    *   **Door Width:** Minimum $900\text{ mm}$ ($36"$) for accessibility.
    *   **Egress Width:** Minimum $1000\text{ mm}$ ($40"$) for fire safety.
    *   **Ventilation:** Required for habitable rooms (NBC 2015).

### 4. Computation
**Calculating the Work Triangle Perimeter:**
The work triangle is formed by connecting the **Sink**, **Range**, and **Refrigerator**.
$$L_{total} = L_{sink-range} + L_{range-fridge} + L_{fridge-sink}$$

**Example Calculation:**
*   $L_{sink-range} = 1500\text{ mm}$
*   $L_{range-fridge} = 1800\text{ mm}$
*   $L_{fridge-sink} = 1800\text{ mm}$
*   **Total:** $1500 + 1800 + 1800 = 5100\text{ mm}$

**Analysis:**
*   Is $5100\text{ mm}$ within the range ($3660\text{--}6705\text{ mm}$)? **Yes.**
*   Is any side $> 2600\text{ mm}$? **No.** (If one side is too long, the triangle is inefficient).
*   **Conclusion:** The layout is efficient.

### 5. Examiner's Mindset
**What the Board Looks For:**
1.  **Code Violations:** Does the bedroom door width meet the minimum ($900\text{ mm}$)? Is the kitchen triangle interrupted by a door?
2.  **Safety Hazards:** Is the sink too close to the range ($> 1800\text{ mm}$)? Is the refrigerator side clearance insufficient ($< 1500\text{ mm}$)?
3.  **Accessibility:** Are the clearances wide enough for a wheelchair or stretcher (if applicable)?
4.  **Ventilation:** Does the bedroom have a window or mechanical ventilation?
5.  **Metric Conversion:** Can you convert $12'$ to $3658\text{ mm}$ accurately?

### 6. Question Patterns
**Multiple Choice (MCQ):**
*   "Which of the following violates the work triangle principle?"
    *   A) Sink to Range distance is $1500\text{ mm}$.
    *   B) Total perimeter is $4000\text{ mm}$.
    *   C) A door passes directly through the triangle.
    *   D) Refrigerator side clearance is $1500\text{ mm}$.
    *   **Answer:** C (Through circulation is prohibited).

**Case Study:**
*   "A client wants a kitchen with a sink and range $2000\text{ mm}$ apart. Is this acceptable?"
    *   **Analysis:** No, it exceeds the maximum $1800\text{ mm}$. This increases the risk of burns and splashing.

### 8. Web of Knowledge
**Related Topics:**
*   **HVAC:** Ventilation requirements for kitchens (extract fans) and bedrooms (fresh air).
*   **Structural:** Load-bearing walls in bedrooms (cannot be removed without structural analysis).
*   **Fire Safety:** Egress paths in bedrooms (NBC 2015).
*   **Ergonomics:** Counter heights ($900\text{--}1050\text{ mm}$) and appliance clearances.

### 9. Key Terms
*   **Work Triangle:** The imaginary triangle connecting the sink, range, and refrigerator.
*   **Through Circulation:** A path that cuts through the work area (prohibited).
*   **Egress:** The path of exit from a building or room (fire safety).
*   **Fitments:** Cabinets, shelves, or built-in appliances.
*   **Habitable Room:** A room used for living, sleeping, or dining (must meet NBC 2015).
*   **Clearance:** The unobstructed space around an appliance or door.
