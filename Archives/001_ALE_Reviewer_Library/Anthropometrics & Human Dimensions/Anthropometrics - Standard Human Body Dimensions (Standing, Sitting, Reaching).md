### Part 1: The Big Picture
**The Golden Ratio of the Filipino Home**

Imagine you are designing a kitchen in a subdivision in Quezon City. You choose a countertop height of 850mm. You install a sink. You invite a neighbor to cook. The neighbor, who is taller than the average Filipino adult, cannot reach the faucet comfortably. The neighbor, who is shorter, cannot see the stove.

This is not just about aesthetics; it is about **Anthropometrics**.

Anthropometrics is the science of measuring the human body. In the Philippine context, it is the bridge between **PD 1096 (National Building Code)** and the lived reality of the Filipino family. While the Code mandates minimum clearances for safety, anthropometrics dictates *comfort* and *functionality*.

**The Core Thesis:**
Architecture is not built for a statue; it is built for a moving, breathing, reaching human. The dimensions you choose today determine the safety and usability of the space tomorrow.

---

### Part 2: Narrative Logic
**The Story of the "Average" Filipino**

**The Hook:**
Every architect starts with a sketch. But before the pencil touches the paper, the architect must ask: *Who is this space for?*

**The Flow:**
1.  **Standing:** We measure the vertical reach. This dictates door heights, light switch placement, and shelf heights.
2.  **Sitting:** We measure the seated posture. This dictates chair height, desk height, and knee clearance.
3.  **Reaching:** We measure the horizontal and vertical reach. This dictates cabinet depth and appliance placement.

**The Conflict:**
The text warns us: *"Average dimensions must always be treated with caution since variations from the norm will always exist."*
In the Philippines, we have a diverse population. Using the "average" (often based on Western data) without adjustment can lead to exclusion.

**The Resolution:**
We must design for the **range**, not just the mean. If you design for the 50th percentile, you exclude the 10th and the 90th. Universal Design requires us to accommodate the extremes within the minimum safety standards.

### Part 4: Computation
**The Math of Comfort**

Architects must calculate clearances to ensure safety and usability.

**Formula 1: Clearance Calculation**
$$ \text{Clearance} = \text{Furniture Width} + \text{Passage Width} $$
*Example:* A dining table is 1200mm wide. The user needs 600mm of clearance on each side to pull a chair out.
$$ \text{Total Width Required} = 1200 + (600 \times 2) = 2400 \text{mm} $$

**Formula 2: Reach Calculation**
$$ \text{Reachable Height} = \text{Standing Height} \times \text{Factor} $$
*Example:* If a user is 1700mm tall, the maximum comfortable reach is often 70% of their height.
$$ \text{Reach} = 1700 \times 0.7 = 1190 \text{mm} $$

**Formula 3: Table Height vs. Chair Height**
$$ \text{Table Height} = \text{Seat Height} + \text{Knee Clearance} $$
*Standard:* Seat Height = 450mm. Knee Clearance = 250mm.
$$ \text{Table Height} = 450 + 250 = 700 \text{mm} \text{ (Standard is 740mm)} $$

### Part 5: Examiner's Mindset
**What the BOA Wants**

The Board of Architecture (BOA) does not want a building that looks good but is unusable. They want **Safety** and **Accessibility**.

**The Trap:**
Using "average" dimensions for everyone.
*   *Scenario:* You design a door width of 700mm because it fits the "average" male.
*   *Reality:* This excludes wheelchairs and strollers. PD 1096 and Accessibility Guidelines often require a minimum of **800mm** for accessible doors.

**The Trap:**
Confusing structural dimensions with functional dimensions.
*   *Example:* A wall might be 200mm thick (structural), but the *clearance* for a door is 800mm (functional).

**The Golden Rule:**
Always prioritize the **minimum clearance** required by code over the "comfortable" dimension unless the project is specifically for a high-end residential unit where comfort is the selling point.

### Part 6: How It's Tested
**Question Types & Scenarios**

**1. Multiple Choice (MCQ):**
*   *Question:* "What is the minimum clear width for a door to accommodate a wheelchair?"
*   *Answer:* 800mm (32 inches).

**2. Case Study:**
*   *Scenario:* A client wants a kitchen with a 900mm counter. The client is 1600mm tall.
*   *Analysis:* Is this safe? Is it comfortable? Does it meet the reach factor?

**3. Design Review:**
*   *Question:* "Identify the error in the following plan: A hallway is 800mm wide with a door swing of 900mm."
*   *Answer:* The door swing (900mm) exceeds the hallway width (800mm), blocking passage.
