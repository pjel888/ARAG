## Core Concept  
Housing policies and relocation strategies are critical components of urban planning, particularly in addressing informal settlements. The **1987 Philippine Constitution** mandates a continuing program of urban land reform and housing under Article XIII, Section 9. This framework is further detailed in **BP 344 (The Housing Act of 1992)** and its 2024 IRR updates, as well as **PD 1096**, which governs land use and development.

### Historical / Legal Context  
- BP 344 was enacted in 1992 but underwent significant revisions with the 2024 IRR. Key changes include stricter compliance with environmental regulations and enhanced community participation in relocation planning.
- PD 1096, issued in 1978, remains foundational for land use policies but has been updated to align with modern housing standards.

### Definitions  
- **Informal Settler Families (ISF):** Households living on public or private land without legal claim, often in危险 areas like esteros or riverbanks.
- **In-City Resettlement:** Relocation within the same local government jurisdiction. 
- **Outside City Relocation:** Relocation to other municipalities or provinces.

### Dimensions / Formulas  
- Housing density: \( \text{Density} = \frac{\text{Total Population}}{\text{Housing Units}} \).
- Land allocation for relocation sites: \( \text{Area} = (\text{Number of Families}) \times 100 \, \text{sqm per family} \).

### Analogies  
1. Relocation planning is like urban acupuncture—targeting specific areas to create balanced growth.
2. Informal settlements are the city’s “unofficial neighborhoods,” reflecting unmet housing demand.
3. In-City Resettlement is akin to interior design—rearranging spaces within existing boundaries.

### Practical Implications  
- Relocation must ensure access to basic services (water, electricity) and livelihood opportunities.
- Challenges include balancing land use with environmental conservation and addressing resistance from resettled families.
