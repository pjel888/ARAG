## Core Concept  
### Historical / Legal Context  
BP 344, enacted in 1987, governs building construction standards, including road design requirements. PD 1096 (1975) amended BP 344 to prioritize public safety and accessibility. The **2024 IRR** further updated these laws, introducing stricter guidelines for carriageway widths and open spaces in urban areas.  

BP 220, passed in 1989, focuses on housing development standards, emphasizing the relationship between road width, density, and public space. While BP 344 (PD 1096) is more infrastructure-focused, BP 220 addresses community planning and sustainability.  

### Definitions  
- **Carriageway Width**: The total width of a road intended for vehicular traffic.  
- **Open Space**: Areas designated for public use, such as parks or plazas, to ensure pedestrian safety and social interaction.  
- **Setback Requirements**: Distances from property lines to buildings or roads to allow for infrastructure expansion.  

### Specific Dimensions and Formulas  
The 2024 IRR mandates:  
- Minimum carriageway width for residential streets under BP 344 (PD 1096): \( \text{Width} = 5.5 \, \text{m} \) for two-way traffic.  
- Open space requirement per housing unit under BP 220: \( \text{Area} = 0.05 \, \text{ha/unit} \).  

### Analogies  
1. **Carriageway Width**: Like a hallway in a building, it must be wide enough to accommodate traffic without congestion.  
2. **Open Space**: Similar to a courtyard, it provides breathing room for communities and enhances quality of life.  
3. **Setback Requirements**: Comparable to buffer zones between buildings and roads, ensuring safety and functionality.  

### Practical Implications  
The 2024 IRR updates have narrowed carriageway widths in urban areas (from \(5.5 \, \text{m}\) to \(4.8 \, \text{m}\)) to prioritize pedestrian-friendly designs. However, this creates challenges for fire engines and large vehicles, requiring wider turning radii (\(12 \, \text{m}\)).
