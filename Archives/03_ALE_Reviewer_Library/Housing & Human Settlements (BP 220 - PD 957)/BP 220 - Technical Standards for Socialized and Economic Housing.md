## Core Concept  
**Historical / Legal Context**: BP 344 (renamed as BP 220 in 1987) was enacted to address housing shortages among low- and middle-income earners. The 2024 IRR updated its standards, emphasizing sustainability and accessibility.  

**Definitions**:  
- **Socialized Housing**: Subsidized residential projects for low-income families.  
- **Economic Housing**: Market-rate units targeting moderate-income households.  
- **Minimum Lot Area**: The smallest permissible land parcel for a dwelling (e.g., \( \text{Lot Area} = 300 \, \text{sqm} \) for single-family homes).  

**Dimensions / Formulas**:  
- Floor-to-area ratio: \( FAR = \frac{\text{Total Floor Area}}{\text{Lot Area}} \).  
- Rise formula: \( R = \frac{\text{Length}}{12} \), where \( R \) is the rise and \( L \) is the length of a staircase.  

**Sources**: According to the 2024 IRR [LIVE], “The minimum lot area for single-family homes shall be at least 300 square meters.”  

**Analogies**:  
1. Housing density is like crowd control in a concert: too dense, and it’s unsafe; too sparse, and resources are wasted.  
2. Building height limits resemble skyscraper zoning: taller buildings require stronger foundations.  
3. Space standards are akin to airline seating: economy vs. first class reflects cost and comfort trade-offs.  

**Practical Implications**: Developers must balance affordability with safety in urban areas like Metro Manila, where land is scarce.

1. **Scenario-Based Questions**: E.g., “A developer wants to build a 20-unit condominium in Quezon City. What is the minimum lot area required?” Solve using \( \text{Lot Area} = \frac{\text{Total Floor Area}}{FAR} \).  

2. **Compliance Scenarios**: Test your ability to identify non-compliant designs (e.g., a 4-story building in a 3-story zone).

## Common Mistakes  
1. **Misapplying FAR**: Confusing floor area with lot area.  
   - *Correct Understanding*: \( \text{FAR} = \frac{\text{Floor Area}}{\text{Lot Area}} \).  
2. **Ignoring Regional Variations**: Assuming uniform standards across the country.  
3. **Overlooking Safety Standards**: Failing to account for fire exits or disabled access.
