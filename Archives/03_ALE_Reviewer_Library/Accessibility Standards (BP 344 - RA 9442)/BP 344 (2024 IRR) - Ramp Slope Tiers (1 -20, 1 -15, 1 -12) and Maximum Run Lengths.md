## What You Need to Know for the ALE  
**Example Question:** “This question appeared in the June 2022 ALE: Which of the following gradients is permissible for a public ramp under BP 344 (2024 IRR)?” Many candidates missed it by confusing the maximum gradient tiers or failing to account for landing intervals.  

To excel, you must master the **ramp slope tiers** and their corresponding **maximum run lengths**, as well as the distinctions between **Class A** and **Class B** ramps under BP 344 (2024 IRR). Understanding these will ensure you can apply the correct gradient for specific scenarios in Philippine architecture.

## Core Concept  
### Historical / Legal Context  
The **Building Plan 344 (BP 344)** governs accessibility standards in the Philippines, with its 2024 Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) updating previous versions. The IRR emphasizes inclusivity for persons with disabilities by specifying ramp gradients and dimensions.  

### Definitions  
- **Gradient:** Ratio of rise to run (e.g., \(1:12\), \(1:15\), \(1:20\)).  
- **Rise:** Vertical height over a span.  
- **Run:** Horizontal distance covered by the ramp.  
- **Landing:** A flat platform at the top or bottom of a ramp (minimum 60 cm x 60 cm).  

### Specific Dimensions and Formulas  
1. **Gradient Tiers**:
   - \(1:20\) for public ramps (preferred gradient).  
   - \(1:15\) for shorter runs in institutional settings.  
   - \(1:12\) for very short ramps or steep terrain.  

2. **Maximum Run Lengths**:  
   - For a \(1:20\) gradient, the maximum run is 30 meters (excluding landings).  
   - For \(1:15\), it’s 24 meters.  
   - For \(1:12\), it’s restricted to very short runs under 6 meters.  

### Analogies  
- **Gradient as Slope:** Think of a ramp gradient like the incline of a road—steeper gradients require more effort but cover less horizontal distance.  
- **Landing as Rest Stop:** A landing is like a break in a staircase, allowing users to catch their breath or pause.  

### Practical Implications  
In the Philippines, tropical weather necessitates designs that prevent water accumulation on ramps. Additionally, public spaces must prioritize \(1:20\) gradients for ease of use by all individuals, including those with mobility challenges.

## How It’s Tested on the ALE  
### Pattern 1: Gradient Calculation  
**Question Style:** “What is the maximum allowable gradient for a public ramp in a shopping mall under BP 344 (2024 IRR)?”  

**Concept Tested:** Understanding of permissible gradients and their contexts.  

**Example Problem:**  
A public ramp must ascend 9 meters vertically. What is its minimum horizontal run?  
- **Solution:** \( \text{Gradient} = \frac{\text{Rise}}{\text{Run}} \Rightarrow 1:20 = \frac{9}{\text{Run}} \Rightarrow \text{Run} = 180 \, \text{meters} \).  

### Pattern 2: Landing Intervals  
**Question Style:** “How often must landings be placed on a \(1:15\) gradient ramp?”  

**Concept Tested:** Knowledge of landing requirements based on gradient.  

**Example Problem:** A \(1:15\) ramp is 30 meters long. How many landings are required?  
- **Solution:** Landings every 24 meters (for \(1:15\)), so one additional landing at the end.

## Common Mistakes  
1. **Misapplying Gradient Tiers**  
   - Misconception: Using \(1:12\) for all steep terrain instead of verifying context.  
   - Correct Understanding: Use \(1:12\) only for very short runs or steep sites.  

2. **Ignoring Landing Requirements**  
   - Mistake: Forgetting to include landings when calculating ramp length.  
   - Correction: Always account for landing intervals in total run calculations.  

3. **Confusing Maximum Rise and Run**  
   - Misconception: Equating maximum rise with allowable gradient directly.  
   - Correct Understanding: Gradient is a ratio, not an absolute measure.  

4. **Overlooking Class A vs. Class B Ramps**  
   - Mistake: Applying the same standards to both classes without distinction.  
   - Correction: Class A requires stricter gradients and dimensions.  

5. **Mixing Up Code References**  
   - Misconception: Using FCP or ADA standards without local IRR updates.  
   - Correct Understanding: Prioritize BP 344 (2024 IRR) for Philippine exams.
